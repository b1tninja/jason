You are required to carry your own insurance

The Association's CC&Rs (Article 8, Individual Owner's Property Insurance) require it: "Each Owner shall purchase and at all times maintain a policy of personal liability and property insurance insuring the Owner's Unit, any upgrades or additions to any fixtures or Improvements to the Owner's Unit, and personal property. The Association's insurance policies will not provide coverage against any of the foregoing. Any insurance maintained by an Owner must contain a waiver of subrogation rights by the insurer as to the other Owners, the Association, and any Mortgagee of the Owner's Unit."

The right policy is an HO-6 (condominium unit owner) policy. An HO-3 homeowner policy or an HO-4 renter's policy is not the right form. If you rent your unit out, ask for the landlord form (often a DP-6) with loss-of-rents coverage, and ask your tenants to carry renter's insurance. Under the CC&Rs, an owner is generally responsible for damage to the interior of their unit, even when the water came from something the Association maintains, such as a roof leak. Your HO-6 is what pays for that.

Loss Assessment coverage: why it matters

Loss Assessment coverage is part of your HO-6. It pays your share when the Association has to charge its members for a covered loss. The most likely case at Mystique is the master policy's $10,000 deductible.

The CC&Rs let the Board decide whether the Association or an owner pays the deductible on a master policy claim. The Owner's Manual says the owner is responsible for any deductible that applies. Suppose a fire or a burst pipe starts in or is traced to your unit and damages the building. You could then be charged up to the full $10,000. With enough Loss Assessment coverage, your own insurer can pay that charge instead of you. The same coverage can help if the Association ever has to assess all owners for a loss larger than the master policy's limits, or for a liability claim.

Many HO-6 policies include only $1,000 of Loss Assessment by default. Some also cap how much they will pay toward an assessment for the Association's deductible. Ask your agent to raise your Loss Assessment limit to at least $10,000. The Owner's Manual recommends "a minimum of $10,000 or higher." Ask your agent to confirm in writing that the coverage applies to an assessment for the master policy's deductible. The cost is usually a few dollars a year.

Other items to review with your agent

  • Dwelling (Coverage A): covers upgrades, and damage inside your unit that costs less than the master deductible. The Owner's Manual recommends at least $5,000. With a $10,000 master deductible, consider $10,000 or more.
  • Personal property, personal liability, and loss of use: loss of use pays for living elsewhere if a covered loss makes your unit uninhabitable.
  • Flood contents: the Association's flood policies do not cover your belongings or temporary living costs. A personal flood policy can.
  • Earthquake: because the Association has none, consider earthquake coverage for your unit, your contents, and earthquake loss assessment.
  • Waiver of subrogation: confirm that your policy waives subrogation against the Association, the other owners, and your lender, as the CC&Rs require.

Proof of insurance for your lender

Lenders often ask for proof of insurance. They may send letters warning that they will buy insurance for you (often at a much higher cost) if you don't provide it. Most lenders want three things:

  1. the Association's master policy certificate (enclosed);
  2. your own HO-6 declarations page (from your insurer);
  3. the flood policy declarations page for your building (enclosed).

The Association's flood policies usually satisfy a lender's flood requirement for the A99 zone.

Some lenders will accept the enclosed Certificate of Insurance as it is. Others want a certificate that shows your unit's address. The enclosed Assessor's Parcel Map shows that your address is part of the Mystique Community subdivision, and some lenders will accept that.

You can also order a custom certificate with your name, address, and loan number from EOIdirect, the service the Association's insurance agent uses, at www.eoidirect.com or by phone at (877) 456-3643.
