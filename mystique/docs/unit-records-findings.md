# Insurance and original components: what the documents say (October 3, 2026)

Findings for [docs/unit-records-design.md](../../docs/unit-records-design.md). The words are recited from the consolidated
declaration on disk, which is not an official restatement; the recorded instruments control. Readings are labeled. This is not legal
advice, and jason decides neither coverage nor liability.

## Does the association's policy cover original components?

**Short answer (a reading):** yes for the unit's standard fixtures and improvements as originally installed and their equivalent
replacements; not for upgrades beyond that, and not for contents. Two sections must be read together, and one open question remains.

Declaration 8.1(a)(i)(B), Units (property covered by the master policy):

> "The standard fixtures and Improvements within individual Units as originally installed by Declarant and any equivalent
> replacements thereto, including, but not limited to, interior walls and doors; ceiling, floor and wall surface materials (e.g.,
> paint, wallpaper, mirrors, carpets, and hardwood floors); utility fixtures (including gas, electrical and plumbing); cabinets;
> built-in appliances; heating and air-conditioning systems; water heaters installed as a part of the original construction of the
> residence and any equivalent replacements thereto; but excluding any personal property located in the Unit; and excluding any
> Improvements or upgrades to any of the foregoing to the extent the replacement cost of any such Improvement or upgrade made after
> completion of the original construction of the Unit exceeds the replacement cost immediately before the installation of the
> Improvement or upgrade."

Declaration 8.4, Individual Owner's Property Insurance:

> "Each Owner shall purchase and at all times maintain a policy of personal liability and property insurance insuring the Owner's
> Unit, any upgrades or additions to any fixtures or improvements to the Owner's Unit, and personal property. The Association's
> insurance policies will not provide coverage against any of the foregoing."

Reading the two to give each effect (Civil Code 3541; Code of Civil Procedure 1858): 8.1(a)(i)(B) says what the master policy
covers, and 8.4 says what the owner must insure and that the master policy does not cover "the foregoing", which on this reading
means upgrades, additions, and personal property rather than the original fixtures 8.1(a)(i)(B) names. Read as covering the Unit
whole, 8.4 would empty 8.1(a)(i)(B) of effect. This is the board's reading to adopt; if two readings remain, the board asks counsel.

**The betterment line.** An upgrade is outside the master policy only "to the extent" its replacement cost exceeds the replacement
cost immediately before it was installed. A like-for-like replacement ("equivalent") stays inside. So the record that matters is what
the original was and what it cost to replace, then what the owner put in. That is what the unit manual would hold.

**Other words that bear on a claim.**

- 8.1(a)(vii), Deductible: "The amount of any deductible shall be paid by the Association and/or Owner pursuant to guidelines adopted
  by the Board." **No such guidelines were found in the library or the governing-document search.** The Owner's Manual says the
  owner pays "any deductible that applies." The deductible allocation is therefore the board's to write down.
- 8.6, Adjustment of Losses: the Board is each owner's attorney-in-fact to negotiate the value and extent of a loss under the master
  policy and may compromise and settle claims.
- The members' annual statement (Bylaws, Exhibit A) says the association's policies may not cover an owner's personal property or real
  property improvements, and that an owner may owe all or part of a deductible.
- Civil Code 4775(a)(3): unless the declaration provides otherwise, the owner repairs and maintains the separate interest.
  Section 4775(a)(1) gives the association the common area.

## The policy itself (read October 3, 2026)

Source: the master package for the 2025-09-28 to 2026-09-28 term (150 pages, the file in `data/insurance/documents`), read from its
text layer. The renewed term's declarations are not on file, so this is the expiring term; the forms usually carry over, which the
agent can confirm. Declarations: building limit $36,269,905, valued at guaranteed replacement cost, **$10,000 deductible**;
equipment breakdown $10,000; "Property Enhancement - Platinum" and a protective safeguards condition (automatic sprinklers).

**Endorsement N CP 12301, "Property Covered - Residential Community Associations - Unit Interior - Single Entity."** It replaces the
form's paragraph about property inside units with:

> "(6) Any of the following types of property, indivisibly owned by a unit owner and contained within the interior surfaces of the
> walls, floors and ceilings that was originally installed or replaced in accordance with your condominium's original plans and
> specifications: (a) Fixtures, including but not limited to: i. permanently installed built-in cabinets ii. built-in appliances
> iii. electrical and plumbing fixtures that are a part of the building or structure; and (b) Appliances, such as those used for
> refrigerating, ventilating, cooking, dishwashing, laundering, security or housekeeping. Coverage is limited to 6(a) and 6(b) above
> regardless of any insurance requirement imposed by your condominium association agreement."

**Endorsement N CP 12501, "Unit Definition."** Defines a unit by its boundaries and the property inside them "permanently installed
and dedicated to the exclusive service of the enclosed portion," including "(2) All walls, floors, ceilings, partitions, columns, and
dividers, lath, wallboard, plasterboard, plaster, paneling, molding, tiles, wallpaper, paint, finished flooring and their finished
surfaces," plus fixtures, appliances, pipes, ducts, and wires.

**Valuation (N CP 12010).** Replacement cost with comparable material and quality, and "Local builder's grade costs for any property
included in units which was initially installed in accordance with your condominium's original plans and specifications, if the
original plans and specifications cannot be documented or determined." The carrier pays replacement cost only once the property is
actually repaired or replaced, and the insured may elect actual cash value first and claim replacement cost within 200 days.
Guaranteed replacement cost carries a condition about reporting additions or improvements that raise value by $25,000 or more.

### Readings, labeled

1. **The policy's list is narrower than the declaration's.** Declaration 8.1(a)(i)(B) lists interior walls and doors; ceiling, floor
   and wall surface materials (paint, wallpaper, mirrors, carpets, hardwood); utility fixtures; cabinets; built-in appliances;
   heating and air-conditioning; and water heaters. N CP 12301 names fixtures (cabinets, built-in appliances, electrical and plumbing
   fixtures) and appliances, and says coverage is limited to those "regardless of any insurance requirement imposed by your
   condominium association agreement." It does not name floor and wall finishes, heating and cooling equipment, or water heaters
   (the "including but not limited to" in 6(a) may reach fixtures such as those). N CP 12501 does list finished surfaces within
   the definition of a unit, but a definition is not a grant of coverage, and how the two fit is not clear to me. This is a
   question for the agent and the carrier in writing, and for counsel if the answers differ: **are unit finishes (flooring, paint,
   wall coverings) and unit heating, cooling, and water heating covered under the master policy?** If not, the declaration
   requires coverage the policy does not give, which is a `Conflict`-type finding for the board, and an owner's HO-6 must carry it.
2. **Original means "per original plans and specifications," and the carrier asks for proof.** The policy pays local builder's
   grade if the original specification cannot be documented. So the developer's finish and appliance specification by plan is
   not only an owner convenience: without it, a covered claim can be valued below the original. That is the case for the community
   facts in [unit-records-design.md](../../docs/unit-records-design.md).
3. **An upgrade is outside the single-entity grant.** Only property "originally installed or replaced in accordance with" the original
   plans is listed, so an upgrade (granite for laminate) is the owner's to insure. The declaration's own line is the cost
   comparison; the policy's line is the original specification. They usually agree and can differ at the margin.
4. **The association's own guidance already says this.** The "Personal Insurance Check-List" given to owners asks for an HO-6 with
   dwelling coverage of $5,000 "for claims that are less than the Association's deductible," loss assessment of $10,000 or more, and
   item 12, "Inventory": "You won't be given granite if the home was originally built with laminate countertops and you have no proof
   of an upgrade." The unit manual is that advice made into a record. The annual summary tells members the association's policies
   may not cover real property improvements to their dwelling and that they may owe part of a deductible.
5. **Equipment breakdown** carries its own $10,000 deductible and a condition that pays up to 125% of like kind and quality for
   safer or more efficient replacement equipment after an accident; whether that reaches unit equipment follows the question in 1.
6. **The Joint or Disputed Loss Agreement (CP 12 70)** is about a boiler-and-machinery insurer and the property insurer disagreeing
   on coverage. It is not an agreement between the association's insurer and an owner's, so it does not settle an owner-versus-
   association dispute.

### Who answers for what: the ladder in the declaration

A loss inside a unit passes through these provisions, in this order. Quoted words govern; the notes are readings.

1. **7.3, Association Liability:** "the Association shall not be responsible or liable for any maintenance, repair, or replacement of
   a Unit or Exclusive Use Common Area or any Improvement thereon, except to the extent that the need for such maintenance, repair,
   or replacement results from the negligence or fault of the Association, its employees, contractors, or agents."
2. **7.11, Owner Responsibility for Consequential Damage:** "Except as provided by Article 11 of this Declaration, an Owner is
   responsible for the cost of repair of those portions of the Owner's Unit which are required to be maintained by the Owner ... and
   the fixtures and personal property located on the Owner's Unit ..., even if the cause of the damage originates from a source
   maintained by the Association, unless the cause is the gross negligence of the Association or its agents. As an example, water
   damage to the interior of a Unit that is caused by a leak in the roof is the responsibility of the Owner even though the repair of
   the roof is the responsibility of the Association."
3. **Article 11, Damage or Destruction:** "If any portion of the Development is damaged or destroyed by fire or other casualty, all
   available insurance proceeds shall be paid to or on behalf of the Association," and the Board repairs "those portions of the
   damaged Units which are covered by the Associations' insurance."
4. **8.1(a)(vii), Deductible:** paid "by the Association and/or Owner pursuant to guidelines adopted by the Board" (none on file).
5. **8.1(a)(vi) and 8.4, Waiver of Subrogation:** the master policy "shall waive all subrogation rights against any Owner or occupant";
   owners' policies must waive it as to the other owners, the association, and mortgagees.
6. **7.9, Owner Liability:** an owner's willful or negligent act, or that of a household member, tenant, guest, or pet, that causes the
   association to repair is charged to the owner as a Reimbursement Assessment.

Reading to give each effect: 7.11 allocates the **cost** of repairing an owner's portions and fixtures to the owner, except as Article 11
provides, and Article 11 governs an insured casualty. So the order of questions after a loss is: (1) what is the item (original,
replaced, upgraded, or personal property); (2) where did the cause originate (association-maintained source, the owner's own system,
a contractor, a tenant); (3) is it a covered, insured casualty above the deductible (Article 11 and the policy) or not (7.11); (4)
negligence of the association or its agents (7.3, 7.11's "gross negligence"), or of an owner or household (7.9); (5) who pays the
deductible (the missing guidelines). 7.3 says "negligence or fault" and 7.11 says "gross negligence": on the usual rule that a
particular provision controls a general one (Code of Civil Procedure 1859), 7.11 governs consequential damage and 7.3 governs
maintenance responsibility. That is a reading, and where it matters the board asks counsel.

Civil Code 4775(a)(3) allocates repair of the separate interest to the owner "unless otherwise provided in the declaration," so the
declaration may allocate by agreement. Whether 7.11 is lawful and fair as applied to a roof leak is the question the board sent to
counsel on March 19, 2026 and is not answered here.

### What the records show in practice

(Details are in `mystique/notes/unit-record-incidents.md`, which is not checked in; this is the summary.)

- Roof leaks into units (seven events, 2021 to 2026) were all handled the same way: the association's roofer repaired the roof as
  maintenance, the cost stayed under the $10,000 deductible, no claim was made, and the roofer's invoices say interior damage and mold
  are the owner's or tenant's. That is 7.11 applied.
- The master policy was used for a unit interior once in the period, and the owner's own carrier deferred to it, which is 8.1(a)(iv)
  ("primary and non-contributing"). Two other water claims closed without payment and one was disclaimed by an owner's carrier; the
  reasons are in confidential letters that should be read before advising owners.
- A Google Doc of April 30, 2026, "The Allocation of Liability for Below-Deductible Damage Claims," already puts the below-deductible
  allocation in writing (owner: unit interior, "Drywall / Paint / Flooring"; association: the roof and exterior; HO-6 for the owner's
  share). It matches 7.11 and the practice. It is silent on an insured loss above the deductible (Article 11), on original versus
  upgraded items, and on the policy's narrower list. As the board's written deductible guideline, it needs those three additions,
  and its "Drywall / Paint / Flooring: Homeowner" line is true below the deductible but may not be above it if the master policy covers
  original finishes, which is the question to the agent.
- A contractor's proposal for a 2026 collision repair says it will match existing stucco, paint, and drywall texture but "exact match
  cannot be guaranteed due to weathering and age," which the paint screen's touch-up answer should repeat.

### The March 19, 2026 meeting

The minutes' AI-written summary (which says on its face that AI can make mistakes) records that the board approved a roof repair,
deferred interior damage repairs "pending legal review of CCRs regarding owner responsibility for consequential damage," and
discussed a contractor's water heater replacement that damaged a sprinkler and garage drywall. It also states that the association's
policy "typically covers damages not covered by individual unit owners' policies." That does not match the policy's text, which covers
the building and the listed unit property regardless of owners' policies, with a $10,000 deductible; it is not a backstop to owners'
coverage. The summary may not capture what was said, but it is the kind of sentence an owner would rely on, so the board should
correct it in the record if it was said that way, and not repeat it to owners.
