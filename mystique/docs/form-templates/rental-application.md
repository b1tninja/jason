# Rental application, exception request, and rehearing request

**HELD: designed, not offered.** The board has not taken up its rental approvals (board item `rental-approvals-4-15`). **No owner is contacted about occupancy until the board decides.** This page designs the forms so they are ready; nothing here is sent, posted, linked from a letter or an email, or made a PayHOA form, and no owner is asked who lives in a unit or whether a unit is rented because of it. The annual owner-information form’s occupancy question (CIV 4041(a)(4)) is a different act and is not this one.

Status: design (2026-10-05). Key `rental-application`; proposed marker code `RN`. It follows the standard in [form-templates.md](../../../docs/form-templates.md). The inventory row is "Rental application" in [standard-forms.md](../standard-forms.md) (the worked example); the general standard and the family it belongs to are [docs/standard-forms.md](../../../docs/standard-forms.md) (family two: the documents make the owner ask). This is the association’s page, so it names the association’s sections.

**CC&Rs 4.15(d), Written Application** (the application’s content, and what the Board shall not ask):

> Any Owner desiring to lease or rent his or her Unit shall submit an application in writing to the Board of Directors, which shall state: the name, mailing address, Unit address, and record of ownership date of the Owner; the proposed lease term; and such other information which the Board of Directors may reasonably require from time to time, provided that the Board shall not inquire regarding the identity of the proposed tenants. Each Owner shall have the further right to appear in person before the Board of Directors and to discuss the request to lease or rent his or her Unit.

**CC&Rs 4.15(e), Board Review of Application** (the clock, the decider, and what is not considered):

> Within thirty (30) days after receipt of such application to lease or rent, the Board of Directors shall review such application, and approve or disapprove it in a written notice transmitted to the requesting Owner. If the application is disapproved, the notice shall specify the reason(s) for disapproval. The Board shall approve the application, unless doing so will increase the number of Units leased or rented within the Development to more than allowed under Section 4.15 (a), or will otherwise result in the violation of any provision of this Section 4.15. The identity of the proposed tenants shall not be considered and shall have no bearing upon the Board’s review and decision regarding an application to permit the rental of a Unit.

**CC&Rs 4.15(f), Rehearing:**

> If The application is disapproved, the Owner concerned shall have a right to rehearing upon written request to the Board of Directors, at its next regular meeting, or as otherwise agreed between the Owner and the Board. The Owner shall have the right to appear at the rehearing and present his or her case. Within ten (10) days after the conclusion of such rehearing, the Board shall transmit its written determination to the requesting Owner and, if again disapproved, shall specify the reasons for such disapproval.

**CC&Rs 4.15(g), Decision of Board Conclusive:**

> Subject To Section 4.15 (f), the decision of the Board of Directors in approving or disapproving an application of an Owner to lease his or her Unit shall be final and conclusive.

**CC&Rs 4.15(c), Exceptions:**

> The Board of Directors shall have the right but shall not be obligated to waive some or all of the provisions of this Section 4.15 either
>
> in case of deserving and unusual hardship or
>
> for a limited term, not to exceed one (1) year upon written request of an Owner representing that he or she will retake possession and occupancy of the Unit as a Resident thereof upon the expiration of such limited term and subject to such other conditions as the Board may determine. The Board shall have the right to review and approve the lease for such a limited term. Exceptions as authorized by the Board shall take precedence over the order of priority established pursuant to Section 4.15 (i).

**CC&Rs 4.15(a), Restriction on Number of Units Leased** (as jason’s consolidated copy reads):

> Not more than twenty percent (25%) of the Units within the Development shall, at any particular time, be leased or rented or occupied by anyone other than an Owner, members of his or her household, or temporary guests, except as provided in this Section 4.15. The restriction on the number or percentage of Units that may be leased or rented as set forth in this Section 4.15 shall not apply to any Unit which is being leased or rented on the date this Declaration is Recorded, but shall apply upon transfer of title to such Unit after this Declaration is Recorded.

(The Second Amendment’s own amended text reads “Not more than twenty percent (20%) twenty-five (25%) of the Units ...”, with the strike and the addition shown together; jason’s leasing rules use 25 percent. Lead 4.)

**CC&Rs 4.15(i), Priority of Applicants:**

> The Board shall establish and maintain a priority list, identifying the name, mailing address, address of Unit, record date of ownership,and date the written application or request of each Owner to lease or rent his or her Unit was submitted to the Board. When the number of Units leased or rented in the Development is less than the number allowed under Section 4.15(a), the Board shall authorize the Owner who submitted the earliest application to lease or rent his or her Unit. One an Owner obtains permission to lease or rent, he or she may do so to consecutive lessees or renters or for consecutive terms without interruption of more than sixty (60) days or may reoccupy his or her Unit for a period not to exceed sixty (60) days, without having to reapply to the Board for permission to Iease or rent.

**CC&Rs 4.15(h), List of Rented Units:**

> The Board of Directors shall prepare a list of all Owners currently leasing or renting a Unit, which list shall include the Owner’s name, mailing address, Unit address, date of record ownership, and term of the lease. Such a list shall be made available to any Owner upon payment of reasonable administrative charge to be set by the Board of Directors.

**CC&Rs 4.15(b), Implementation:**

> Upon request from the Board after this Declaration is Recorded, each Owner renting or leasing a Unit shall provide such information as the Board may reasonably require to implement the provisions of this Section 4.15, including but not limited to the names of the tenants and the members of the tenants’ household and a copy of the signed lease. Any permitted rental or lease of Unit commencing after this Declaration is recorded and the renewal of tenancy in effect on the date this Declaration is Recorded shall be pursuant to a written lease or rental agreement in accordance with Section 4.15 (m).

**CC&Rs 4.15(m), Requirements of Written Rental Agreement** (as amended by the Second Amendment):

Any lease or rental agreement entered into between an Owner and a lessee or renter shall be for a minimum term of thirty (30) days. An Owner renting a Unit shall provide the Association with proof of current criminal background check and proof of credit report for each prospective tenant

> 4740 (c) Prior to renting or leasing their separate interest as provided by this section, an owner shall provide the association verification of the date the owner acquired title to the separate interest and the name and contact information of the prospective tenant or lessee or the prospective tenant’s or lessee’s representative.
>
> 4741 (a) An owner of a separate interest in a common interest development shall not be subject to a provision in a governing document or an amendment to a governing document that prohibits, has the effect of prohibiting, or unreasonably restricts the rental or leasing of any of the separate interests, accessory dwelling units, or junior accessory dwelling units in that common interest development to a renter, lessee, or tenant.

**What the documents and the statute do not say** (so the form does not say it either): the form of the application; a fee for it (the fee schedule lists none under this name); a time within which an owner may ask for a rehearing; what follows if the Board does not act in thirty days; whether an application disapproved because the cap is full stays on the priority list; what “regular meeting” means for the rehearing (lead 7); who counts as a household member.

## 2. Who uses it, and when

- **An Owner** (a member) who wishes to lease or rent a Unit. 4.15(d): “Any Owner desiring to lease or rent his or her Unit”. 4.15(i) shows the application is also the owner’s place in line: its date is the priority date.
- **An Owner asking for an exception** (4.15(c)): for deserving and unusual hardship, or for a limited term of not more than one year, because the Owner will retake possession and occupancy.
- **An Owner whose application was disapproved** and who asks for a rehearing (4.15(f)).
- **When:** only once the board has decided to offer the forms (the hold above). The documents set no season; an application may be made whenever an owner desires to lease.
- **Who it is not for:** a tenant (not a member); an owner who occupies the unit; a buyer; an owner renting a portion of an owner-occupied unit (CIV 4739(a): such an owner “shall not be subject to a provision in a governing document ... that prohibits the rental or leasing of a portion of the owner-occupied separate interest ... for a period of more than 30 days”; whether 4.15 reaches such a rental is for counsel); an owner answering the annual occupancy question.

**Not asked, on purpose:**
- **Who the proposed tenants are**: their names, phone numbers, household, employer, income, or the signed lease. 4.15(d) says “the Board shall not inquire regarding the identity of the proposed tenants”; 4.15(e) says their identity “shall not be considered and shall have no bearing upon the Board’s review and decision”. A form that asked would put it before the Board. If an owner writes it anyway, the handler files it in the restricted store, copies it to no board packet or list, and tells the owner once that it was not read for the decision.
- **The tenant’s background check or credit report.** 4.15(m) and B-1(h) ask the Owner to provide the proof. The application does not. See lead 1.
- **Why the owner wants to rent, where the owner lives, or the owner’s income.** Not required by 4.15(d).
- **A fee.** The fee schedule lists none for an application; lead 6.

## 6. What the member is told

The sentence the Owner reads (`member_clock`), for Part 1:

> Within thirty (30) days after we receive your application, the Board will review it and tell you in writing whether it is approved or disapproved. If it is disapproved, the notice will give the reasons. The Board must approve the application unless approving it would put more Units out for rent than Section 4.15(a) of the CC&Rs allows, or would otherwise violate Section 4.15. The Board does not ask who the proposed tenants are and does not consider it. You may appear in person to discuss your request. The date we receive your application is also your place on the Board’s priority list (4.15(i)). The thirty days are counted from {RECEIVED} to {DUE}.

For Part 3 (a rehearing):

> If your application is disapproved, you may ask the Board in writing for a rehearing. It is held at the Board’s next regular meeting, or at another time you and the Board agree. You may appear and present your case. Within ten (10) days after the rehearing ends, the Board will send you its written determination, with the reasons if it is again disapproved. After that, the Board’s decision is final and conclusive (4.15(g)).

Reading (label: the association’s plain-words note, not the document): “The date we receive your application is also your place on the priority list” reads 4.15(i), which lists “the date the written application ... was submitted to the Board”; whether an application disapproved because the cap is full stays on the list is not stated (lead 3).

Who decides: the Board (4.15(e)), in a written notice. For an exception, the Board “shall have the right but shall not be obligated” (4.15(c)); no clock is stated. How to reconsider: the rehearing of 4.15(f); after it, the decision is “final and conclusive” (4.15(g)). Whether Civil Code 5900 to 5920 (meet and confer) or the member’s remedies outside the documents remain is for counsel (lead 9).

## 7. The association’s clocks

“Counted from” is the association’s receipt, stamped on the day it arrives (mail on the day opened; email, PayHOA, and the portal on the day sent). The documents count in “days”; jason counts them as calendar days and says so (lead 8).

| # | Counted from | Number and kind of day | Who sets it | What follows if it passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 8. The acknowledgment text

> Reference {REFERENCE}. We received your application to lease or rent your Unit on {RECEIVED}. The Board will review it and tell you in writing whether it is approved or disapproved by {DUE} (thirty days after we received it). {DECIDER} is the Board. You may appear in person before the Board to discuss it. The date we received it is the date on the Board’s priority list. To change or withdraw it, write to {RETURN_BY_EMAIL} or {RETURN_BY_MAIL} and quote the reference.

For an exception: “We also received your request for an exception on {RECEIVED}.” For a rehearing: “We received your request for a rehearing on {RECEIVED}. The Board will hear it at its next regular meeting or at the time you and the Board agree, and will send its written determination within ten days after the rehearing ends.” `{DUE}` is `{RECEIVED}` plus thirty days.

## 9. Channels and the reference

- **Channels:** designed for paper, fillable PDF, email, PayHOA, and the portal; **none is made while the hold stands**. A signed-in PayHOA answer would be `SIGNED_IN`; a reply email from the address on file `MATCHED`; a signed paper copy `CLAIMED`. A Google Form is not offered: an application is an owner’s act, and the date received sets a priority.
- **Marker code proposed: `RN`** (rental). Letters from the alphabet `0-9 A C E F H K M N P R T V X`; the codes in use or proposed (see [the design pages’ table](../../../docs/form-templates/README.md)) include `NP`, `NC`, `NA`, `NV`, `CN`, `HM`, `AM`, `RR`, `MN`, `RT`, `MC`, `RV`, `PP`, `PR`, `AP`, `RC`, `EV`, `PV`, `PX`, and the profile’s `VR`, `RH`, `PK`; `RN` collides with none found in `docs/` or `mystique/forms.py`. A standing form’s campaign is the year it was generated (`RN26P-…`).
- The reference is a hint, never what the reading depends on ([form-identifiers.md](../../../docs/form-identifiers.md)).

## 11. Handler and procedure

- **Handler:** `@handler("ccrs#4.15", role=Role.FORM_RETURN, form="rental-application", procedure="rental-application", channels=(PAYHOA, GMAIL, MAIL))` ([arrivals-design.md](../../../docs/arrivals-design.md); the documents’ section is the key because no statute is). `accepts` takes an arrival with this form’s marker (`RN`) or a PayHOA form id. `read` is evidence only. `plan` stamps the date received (the priority date), opens the 30-day clock as a schedule occurrence, drafts the acknowledgment, adds a row to the **priority list** register (name, mailing address, unit address, record date of ownership, date submitted: the five things 4.15(i) names) and, once the Board approves, to the **list of rented units** register (4.15(h): name, mailing address, unit address, date of record ownership, term of the lease), and offers the count of rented units against the cap (`jason rentals`, the unit tags “Rental” and “Rental Approved”, [owner-information.md](../../../docs/owner-information.md)). It writes nothing outside jason. The “Rental Approved” tag is a person’s, after the Board’s resolution; the resolution or minutes, not a tag, hold the date, the term, and the source.
- **Procedure:** `rental-application` does not exist in `src/jason/community/procedures.py`; the nearest is `respond`. Add it, **held** like the forms: (1) stamp the date received; (2) acknowledge; (3) count the rented units against the cap (`jason rentals`); (4) put the application on the board packet with the count and the priority list; (5) the Board decides within the 30 days and sends its written notice, with reasons if disapproved; (6) update the priority list and the list of rented units; (7) a rehearing request is stamped, scheduled for the next regular meeting or the agreed time, and the ten days are counted from the rehearing’s end; (8) write each failure as a lesson. Step (4) names `rental-approvals-4-15`.
- **jason never:** approves or denies an application, ranks or chooses among owners, contacts an owner about occupancy while the hold stands, reads or records who the tenants are, or opens a tenant’s screening report ([forms.md](../../../docs/forms.md): “Rental applications and screening reports are never opened”; AGENTS.md, Boundaries).

## 12. Edge cases

- **Co-owners.** An application is the Owner’s; one co-owner may sign for the Unit with the others’ names, or each signs. The priority date is the first receipt. The record of ownership date is the earliest of the owners’, as the deed shows.
- **A representative.** A person with a power of attorney may sign for the Owner with the authority attached; the application is the Owner’s.
- **A tenant or a non-owner.** Not an Owner. A tenant who writes “my landlord wants to rent” is told the owner applies; the association does not act on it.
- **An Owner who writes the tenants’ names anyway.** Filed in the restricted store; not read for the decision; the Owner told once (section 4).
- **An application with something missing** (the record date, the term). It is an application on receipt; the date received is the priority date; the missing detail is asked once; the 30 days run from receipt. Whether the 30 days run when the application is too incomplete to read is for counsel (lead 8).
- **A request in other words.** An email or letter that says “I want to rent my unit” is an application in writing if it states the four things of 4.15(d) and the term; otherwise the handler fills the form from it and asks once. Its date is the day it arrived.
- **Language and accessibility.** The statutory and documents’ words stay in English; large print, another format, a person to read the form aloud or write it down, and a reasonable accommodation are offered.
- **The request is really something else.** A question about the cap is not an application; a request to be told whether the rental list has room is a question for the Board’s list (4.15(h)); a request to waive a part of 4.15 is Part 2; a request for a variance from another Article 4 restriction is [variance-request.md](variance-request.md).

## 13. Leads for the board and counsel

These are readings, not rules, and jason resolves none. Each should be read in full by a person before anyone acts on it.

1. **The tenants’ identity, the proof, and the statute.** 4.15(d) and (e) bar the Board from inquiring into, or considering, “the identity of the proposed tenants”. 4.15(b) lets the Board request, “upon request”, “the names of the tenants and the members of the tenants’ household and a copy of the signed lease”; 4.15(m) says an Owner renting “shall provide the Association with proof of current criminal background check and proof of credit report for each prospective tenant”; B-1(h) says the Owner “is required to notify management of the names and phone numbers of their tenants and provide a copy of the rental agreement, and proof of background and credit checks for each prospective tenant”; and CIV 4740(c) says that before renting, an owner “shall provide the association verification of the date the owner acquired title ... and the name and contact information of the prospective tenant or lessee or the prospective tenant’s or lessee’s representative”. **A reading that gives each provision effect** (labeled, jason’s): the decision on the application does not consider the tenants; the information that 4.15(b) and (m), B-1(h), and 4740(c) ask for goes to the manager and is requested separately, as a condition of the tenancy and not of the application, so the identity stays out of the Board’s decision. **Whether that satisfies the tenant-contact requirement of 4740(c), the bar on rules that “unreasonably” restrict rental in 4741(a), and the use of screening reports under fair-housing law is for counsel.** The form asks none of it. A separate “before occupancy” form is not designed here.
2. **The priority list and the cap against CIV 4741(a) and (b).** CIV 4741(a) bars a provision that “prohibits, has the effect of prohibiting, or unreasonably restricts” rental. Whether board approval of each rental and a priority list are such a restriction is for counsel. The existing conflict row concerns the cap (4.15(a) against 4741(b)); the Second Amendment raised the cap to twenty-five percent; whether the row still stands is for the board.
3. **Does a disapproved application stay on the priority list?** 4.15(i) lists “the date the written application or request of each Owner ... was submitted”, and (e) has the Board disapprove an application that would exceed the cap. The documents imply a list that holds waiting applicants; they do not say so. The notice of disapproval states the place on the list only if the Board adopts the reading.
4. **The cap’s words.** The consolidated text of 4.15(a) reads “twenty percent (25%)”; the Second Amendment shows “twenty percent (20%) twenty-five (25%)”, a strike and an addition shown together. A person confirms the recorded instrument’s words; jason’s leasing rules use 25 percent.
5. **Counting units that owners occupy.** CIV 4741(e): “a separate interest shall not be counted as occupied by a renter if the separate interest ... is occupied by the owner.” How 4.15(a)’s count treats them is for the board’s counting rule (`jason rentals` counts the tags).
6. **A fee.** The fee schedule lists no rental application fee. “Administrative Fee - Other $25” and a move-in/move-out fee are listed elsewhere in the Enforcement Policy; they are not read as applying. Whether a fee may be charged for an application is for the board and counsel.
7. **“Its next regular meeting.”** 4.15(f): the rehearing is “at its next regular meeting, or as otherwise agreed”. The board’s regular meetings are the January, April, July, and October meetings under Administrative Resolution 20230130-1; the board meets monthly in practice, and whether the other months’ meetings are regular or special is before it (`meeting-schedule-resolution`). A rehearing request received in November would otherwise wait for January. The form therefore offers “the next meeting of any kind (I agree to this)”, which 4.15(f) allows (“as otherwise agreed between the Owner and the Board”). The board decides whether to say that to every owner on the notice.
8. **“Thirty (30) days.”** Not defined as calendar or business days; jason counts calendar days. The documents state no consequence if the Board does not act within them (no deemed approval); whether a late decision is a failure of good faith is for counsel. Whether the 30 days run from an application that lacks a required item is for counsel; the form treats receipt as the start.
9. **“Final and conclusive.”** 4.15(g) makes the decision final subject to (f). Whether that leaves an owner meet and confer (CIV 5900 to 5920), a request for reasonable accommodation, or a claim under CIV 4741(g) is for counsel. 4741(g): “A common interest development that willfully violates this section shall be liable to the applicant or other party for actual damages, and shall pay a civil penalty ... not to exceed one thousand dollars ($1,000).”
10. **The Manual and the Declaration.** B-1(h) asks for what 4.15(d) and (e) say the Board shall not inquire into. The Manual’s A-3 says “If there is any conflict the provisions of the Declaration will prevail”; that is the documents’ own rule between them, and it does not answer the statute above both.
11. **The exception lease.** 4.15(c): “The Board shall have the right to review and approve the lease for such a limited term.” A lease names the tenants; 4.15(e) says the identity of the proposed tenants “shall not be considered”. Whether (c)’s review of the lease is an exception to that, or how the lease may be reviewed without the names, is for counsel; the form does not ask for the lease.
12. **The Third Amendment’s copy.** jason’s stored copy of the Third Amendment restates the rental-agreement sentence under Section 7.4(c), “Garage Doors”, which looks like a drafting slip. A person reads the recorded instrument.
13. **The list of rented units (4.15(h)).** It is to be “made available to any Owner upon payment of reasonable administrative charge”, with owners’ names and mailing addresses. Its interaction with an owner’s membership-list opt-out (CIV 5220) is for counsel.

Expected: a priority-list row dated 2026-10-20; `{DUE}` = 2026-11-19 (thirty days); acknowledgment; the packet line “count vs cap”; no tenant field read.

**Minimal** (the smallest valid return): `owner-name`, `owner-mailing`, `unit-address`, `ownership-date`, `lease-start`, `lease-end`, signature.

**Edge cases:**
- An owner writes “my tenants will be the X family” in a comment: filed in the restricted store; the Board’s copy is silent; the owner told once.
- An exception with kind “limited term” and `retake` not ticked: incomplete; one request for the representation.
- An application with `ownership-date` after a recorded deed to someone else: a lead; the registry shows it; a person confirms.
- A rehearing request received the day after the notice, with `rehearing-when` = “any meeting”: scheduled at the next meeting; the ten days run from its end.
- A rehearing request six months after the notice: read as a request; the proposed 30 days are not applied until the board adopts them.
- A form with no marker (a photocopy): identified by its printed lines and title; a person attaches it to its campaign.

**The statutory (here, documents’) checklist test, in words.** (1) The form carries each item 4.15(d) lists: the Owner’s name, mailing address, Unit address, record of ownership date, and the proposed lease term. (2) **No question’s text asks for the name, phone, household, employer, or screening of a tenant**, and the form says the Board does not inquire. (3) The form says thirty days and a written notice with the reasons if disapproved. (4) The form offers the right to appear. (5) The exception block asks the representation of 4.15(c). (6) The rehearing block offers “next regular meeting” and “as otherwise agreed”. (7) The recital block contains the exact words of 4.15(c), (d), (e), (f), (g), and (i), each filled from the document, and the build fails if a section is gone. (8) The acknowledgment carries `{RECEIVED}`, `{DUE}`, `{DECIDER}`, `{REFERENCE}`. (9) The form is not generated while the hold stands: the generator refuses it and says “held: rental-approvals-4-15”. (10) The same template renders paper, PDF, email, and PayHOA with the same questions in the same order, each carrying `RN`.
