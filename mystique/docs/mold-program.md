# The mold inspection and prevention program

Read October 5, 2026. This page says what the governing documents require, what exists, what is missing, and what jason proposes the board adopt. jason proposes; the board adopts. It is not legal advice. The words quoted are the working copy's of the Restated Declaration (`jason cite "ccrs#7.8"`; the recorded base is read by OCR and differs by letters, `jason living ccrs --working`). A reading is labeled as one. The findings behind this page (units, owners, ids, dates by document) are in `mystique/notes/mold-program.md`, which is private.

## What the documents require

CC&Rs 7.8(a), the Association: "the Association shall adopt and implement an mold inspection and prevention program which shall include the following steps:"

| Step | Operative words | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | "Inspect the Association Common Area and Building Common Area not less frequently than quarterly to check for water leaks, moisture collection, or other breaches of the watertight integrity of the Common Area and for the presence of mold;" | quarterly |
| (ii) | "If any water leaks, moisture collection, or mold is detected, immediately take appropriate corrective action to repair the Common Area and remove the mold;" | "immediately": no day count |
| (iii) | "Maintain proper ventilation and humidity levels within enclosed Common Areas to reduce the risk of mold;" | continuous |
| (iv) | "Periodically inspect the irrigation system to ensure proper water use and to correct any leaks, misdirected, or excessive watering;" | "periodically": no interval |
| (v) | "Periodically inspect the ground surface around the foundations of Condominium Buildings to ensure that no water is pooling near the foundations; maintain rain gutters and roof drainage systems in a clean and proper operating condition at all times; and" | "periodically"; "at all times" |
| (vi) | "Take such other prudent steps as may be appropriate to prevent mold and eliminate any existing mold in the Common Areas." | as appropriate |

7.8(b), each Owner: "each Owner shall adopt and implement an mold inspection and prevention program" with six steps (inspect the Unit quarterly; correct immediately; ventilation; water fixtures and refrigerator pans; "Replace HVAC filters semiannually or as recommended by the manufacturer"; other prudent steps). 7.8(c): "In the event of any water leak or overflow within a Unit or its Exclusive Use Common Area, the Owner of such Unit shall cooperate with the Association and the other Condominium Building Unit Owners in the inspection and correction of the problem. Cooperation shall include access ..." The defined term "mold" in 7.8 is wider than the word: it includes spores, pollens, other botanical substances, and other allergens.

Related provisions: 7.1(b) (the Association maintains the roof coverings, gutters, and downspouts, and "shall adopt an inspection and maintenance manual"); 7.4 (the owner's unit, garage interior, fixtures, appliances, and deck); 7.6 (the Board's notice to an owner, 60 days, a hearing, a Reimbursement Assessment); 6.7 and 7.9 (Reimbursement Assessments); 7.10 (owners cooperate); 7.11 (an owner bears the cost of repair to the unit, "even if the cause of the damage originates from a source maintained by the Association"). The owners' manual and rules B-17(c) is the one operating rule that prevents mold: potted plants stand on trays raised "to allow sufficient airflow".

On the law shelf (`data/authorities`): no statute requires an association to run a mold program. What is there bears on it only indirectly: Health and Safety Code 17920(j) ("Mold" defined), 17920.3(a)(11) and (13) (dampness and visible mold as a substandard condition, for local officers), Civil Code 4775 (the association maintains the common area, the owner the separate interest, "unless otherwise provided in the declaration"), 4350 and 4360 (an operating rule), 4045 and 5310(a)(12) (general notice; the annual policy statement), 5200 and 5210 (minutes are association records), and 945.5(b), (c) (a builder's defenses for an owner's failure to minimize damage or follow maintenance obligations). The Toxic Mold Protection Act of 2001, which the association's own program document names, is not on the shelf.

## What exists, by kind and date

The minutes and Docs quoted here were read live from the association's Drive on October 5, 2026 (read-only). Most are not in jason's passage index, so `jason verify-quotes` cannot confirm them; that is itself an ingestion gap (below). The statutes and the declaration's words are checked against the shelf and the working copy.

- **The program:** one Google Doc in the association's Drive, created September 2022, last edited February 2026. It restates the six steps, adds a seventh (fill out a worksheet and add photos), and adds advice, definitions, and a "Legal Overview". Its footer reads "Adopted Sep 19, 2022".
- **The adoption:** the board's minutes of August 30, 2022 record that the board "unanimously approved the mold inspection and prevention program, consisting of the steps outlined in CC&R 7.8 (a)" and delegated a director to draft a checklist; the minutes of September 19, 2022 call it "the adopted" program. It is a minute entry, not a resolution, and the footer's date and the vote's date differ (labeled: two dates, both kept). The same minutes record that the board "will not be implementing a Owner Self-Inspection Certification program due to concerns about increased liability".
- **The tools:** a worksheet (a Google Sheet; area, item, condition, comments; no date, inspector, or signature); a copy of it made January 2023 whose text equals the first; and the same worksheet reused as a template, cleared in May 2024 and saved with partial entries in December 2024 and December 2025.
- **Inspections on record:** one dated worksheet, September 2022, reported to the board on September 19, 2022. The board's minutes of August 20, 2024 say it "will schedule a quarterly mold inspection where the board will walk through the common areas"; no record shows one scheduled. The program was an agenda item ("Review Procedures") in May and August 2024, and is not on the board's action register or the October 20, 2026 agenda.
- **Corrective action, not recorded under the program:** the board cited 7.8(a)(ii) in a special resolution (January 30, 2023) and in meetings (spring 2025); gutter cleaning, storm drain jetting, irrigation repairs, a roof inspection (January 2026), and painting with mildew treatment (2023) have vendor records; mold on a balcony and on roof boards appear in the minutes of November 2023 and December 2025.
- **Owner notice:** none on file apart from a reimbursement letter to one owner. The owners' manual and rules, the library, and the PayHOA documents do not mention mold or 7.8(b).
- **Insurance:** the flood policy's summary excludes "Damage caused by moisture, mildew, or mold that could have been avoided by the property owner (the Association)" (a reading: a record of inspection and prompt correction is evidence). The master policy's own wording is not on disk in text.

## Proposed obligations (rows of the shape of `src/jason/community/obligations.py`; not added)

An `Obligation` counts from a record's own date, a PayHOA category, or a payee. No store reads an inspection log, so a person sets `done_on` from the latest dated worksheet, or the row is untracked.

| Row | Authority | Cadence | Evidence it checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common Area mold and moisture inspection | 7.8(a)(i), quoted above | every 3 months | `done_on`: the latest dated worksheet and the minutes; a person decides whether the two undated saves were walks |
| Irrigation system inspected | 7.8(a)(iv) | proposed every 3 months, with the walk ("periodically" sets none) | the worksheet line; a payment for a repair is not an inspection |
| Ground at the foundations checked | 7.8(a)(v) | proposed every 3 months, once in rain | the worksheet line |
| Gutters and roof drainage cleaned | 7.8(a)(v) "at all times" | proposed every 6 months (the builder's manual says twice a year in one place and once in another; the board may choose once) | the PayHOA "Gutter Cleaning" payments and the invoice |
| The program reviewed and the year's results reported | the board's own | proposed yearly | the minutes |

Not an `Obligation`: corrective action "immediately" is a response clock, and goes in `mystique/schedule.py` as an event `Assignment` beside a cadence `Assignment` for the quarterly walk (`adoption=ADOPTED`, `adopted` naming the August 30, 2022 minutes). The owners' steps stay with the `owner-maintenance` assignment (standing); a reminder is canvas item 7(b), the board's choice.

## Proposed written program (outline, for the board to adopt)

Where the documents are silent, the board writes it down and applies it the same way each time (Civil Code 4350 for a rule; the board asks counsel whether this is one).

1. **Authority and scope:** 7.8(a); the Common Area and the Building Common Area; the owners' steps are the owners'.
2. **Terms:** a list of the "enclosed Common Areas"; "Building Common Area" read as the Common Area of each Condominium Building; "mold" as 7.8 defines it.
3. **Who inspects:** the board's walk, led by a named director with a backup; vendor reports (roof, drain, irrigation) serve their steps and are filed by step.
4. **The quarterly walk:** January, April, July, October, one in rain; each building's exterior, balcony undersides, gutters and downspouts, foundation grade, storm drains, irrigation, enclosed Common Areas.
5. **The worksheet:** a date, inspector, weather, and signature; one dated copy per walk, never reused; photos named by area.
6. **Corrective action:** a same-day work order; a proposed meaning of "immediately" (stop the source, begin repair, record the date), with days the board sets; spending authority by the manager up to the amount the board has set, the board above it.
7. **A cause in a unit:** 7.6 notice, 7.8(c) access, a hearing before a Reimbursement Assessment; the Association acts first where the Common Area is at risk.
8. **Ventilation, irrigation, foundations, gutters:** each walk; gutters twice a year and after an overflowing storm.
9. **Other prudent steps:** the deck-plant rule by courtesy notice first; deck waterproofing on the reserve schedule.
10. **Records:** the dated worksheet, photos, work orders, invoices, and a quarter summary, filed together; the summary in the open minutes without an owner's unit or name.
11. **Report to the board:** each quarter a summary; each year the program's review.
12. **Owners:** a general notice reciting 7.8(b) and (c) and B-17(c); a reminder each spring and autumn; no certification (the board's 2022 decision).
13. **Insurance:** each water event reported promptly; the record kept.
14. **Review:** yearly; the Legal Overview cites only what the shelf holds or says it is unverified.

**Counsel reads first:** whether the 2022 minute entry is an adequate adoption or the board should adopt by resolution; whether any owner-facing text is an operating rule needing Civil Code 4360 notice; the owner's duty in 7.8(b) and what a reminder implies; "immediately" and "at all times"; who acts and who pays under 7.8(a)(ii), 7.4, and 7.11 when a Common Area leak damages a unit; the Exclusive Use Common Area decks under Civil Code 4775(a)(4); the master policy's mold and seepage terms; and what "Building Common Area" and "enclosed Common Areas" reach.

## The collection, the context pack, the lens, and the ingestion gaps

This case is the worked example for the general design of programs ([docs/programs.md](../../docs/programs.md), by another author).

**The collection `program-mold`.** Collections are code today (`src/jason/community/document_collections.py`): a legal case's Drive folder is the only named collection, and a `Scope` selects catalogs, kinds, and folders, never "the documents that mention mold". So `jason collection program-mold` cannot be run and was not built. The proposal is a `CollectionKind.PROGRAM` whose members are named documents by role: the mandate (7.8 and its related provisions, B-17), the program document, the adopting acts (the minutes, the special resolution that cites 7.8(a)(ii)), the implementation records (worksheets, with their revision dates), the vendor records by step, the related mail, and the owner-facing documents (none but one letter). The context lines are the specification's record: each step with its cadence, its last evidence date and standing, the adoption act, and the open questions (two adoption dates; "Building Common Area"). It is confidential at the level of its strictest member.

**The context pack by tier.** S: the statutes above, as of a day (Health and Safety Code 17920.3 was amended in 2024, so the pack is asked as of September 19, 2022 and today). G: 7.8 with its defined terms, 7.1(b), 7.4, 7.6, 7.9 to 7.11, 6.7, B-17, and the special resolution. R: the program document, the minutes of the adoption and review, the worksheets, the vendor records, by kind. C: the collection. F: `document_duties` for the 7.8 rows, `meeting_records`, `schedule_assignments`, `incident_history`, `insurance_review`, the obligations' standing, and the collection's context and summary page. D: the draft program.

**The lens checks, each with a `Finding.basis`.** Required step present (TEXT, PROFILE). Adopted by a cited act (STORE, PROFILE, TEXT). Implemented quarterly, listing the quarters with no dated record (STORE, TODAY). Owner duties communicated (STORE, TEXT). Consistent with the law in force on the day (LAW, TEXT). Each finding states a fact tied to its source; none says the law was violated.

**Ingestion gaps** (what jason would need to find this by itself):
- The minutes that adopt the program (2022) are in Drive and `data/meetings` but in no index catalog; the library holds minutes only from 2024, so a search does not find the act.
- The program and the worksheets are Drive-native, unclassified (no kind), and unread; there is no `DocumentKind` for a program, a checklist, or an inspection log; one minutes file name ("Meeting Minutes ...") matches no name rule.
- OCR in the mandate: 28 intake questions on 7.8 (7 open), a stray word in the quarterly step, a lost heading that makes a library passage label the owners' steps as the Association's.
- The duty reader gives "immediately", "periodically", and "at all times" no deadline or recurrence, and has no notion that a duty's object is a program a document must contain and that adopting it is a board act.
- References from minutes and agendas ("CC&R 7.8 (a)") are not read, so nothing links the adoption to the section; a reference without a document name is ambiguous (the bylaws also have a 7.8).
- A revision report prints "adoption on record" for minutes that only quote the section: naming a section is not adopting it.
- An inspection log with no date field, whose only dates are Drive revisions jason keeps no store of; photos in an album jason cannot read; a copy that looks like a new inspection.
- Transcripts and agenda comments that carry the 7.8(a)(ii) discussion are not in the index; vendor reports are filed by vendor, not by the program step they serve.
- A program document that states the law is not checked against the shelf.
