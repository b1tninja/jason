# The maintenance manual and schedule: this association

The declaration requires the association to adopt an inspection and maintenance manual. This page says what the requirement is, what the association's records hold for it on October 5, 2026, where they fall short, the recurring work a manual would turn into obligation rows, and the outline to put to the board. It is the worked example for the general design of a standing program (docs/programs.md, when it lands). Findings that name people, vendors, files, and figures are in `mystique/notes/maintenance-manual.md`, which git ignores.

A reading is labeled as one and says whose. jason proposes; the board adopts. Nothing here is legal advice, and a question that turns on meaning is for counsel.

## The requirement

**CC&Rs 7.1(b)(viii)**, as the recorded declaration states it (`jason cite "ccrs#7.1(b)(viii)"`):

> The Association shall adopt an inspection and maintenance manual for the periodic inspection and maintenance of the Common Area. Such maintenance inspection may be undertaken by a qualified reserve study analyst as part of the Association statutory reserve study requirement. The Board may, from time to time, make appropriate revisions to any maintenance manual based on the Board's review thereof, to update such manual to provide for maintenance according to current industry practices so long as such changes do not reduce the useful life or functionality of the items being maintained.

"Common Area" is, in 1.10, "all of the property comprising the Development, excluding the Units"; it includes each building's structure and the exclusive use common area (1.20).

**Around it** (each as the document states it; the notes recite them whole):

- **7.1(a):** the Association shall maintain, repair and replace the Common Area, "including painting of the exterior surfaces of the buildings"; the Board has "discretion to determine the nature, extent and level of care".
- **7.1(b)(i) to (vii):** a non-exclusive list: exterior surfaces and structure; garage doors and frames; roof coverings, roof structures, gutters, downspouts; walls and fences facing the common area; landscaping; sewer, water, storm drain, electrical and other utility lines up to the point they serve one unit; cluster mailboxes "to the extent they are not maintained by another entity".
- **7.7** (wood destroying pests: an inspection and preventive program, "If the Association adopts" one) and **7.8(a)** (a mold inspection and prevention program the Association "shall adopt and implement": quarterly inspection of the common area; periodic irrigation and foundation checks; gutters and roof drainage "in a clean and proper operating condition at all times"). Each is its own program; the manual cites them and does not count them again.
- **10.12:** if the Association fails to maintain the Common Area, the City may do so and charge the Association.
- **Bylaws 9.3:** a visual inspection of the major components at least every three years, as part of the reserve study, and an annual review of the study.

**A mandate outside the declaration.** The City's conditions on the 2006 PUD special permit for the project (the Planning Commission staff report, in the library) say, as the report words them:

> D5. The homeowner's association shall conduct periodic inspections, not less than monthly, of the exterior of all buildings, trash enclosures and recreation facilities. D6. The homeowner's association shall establish and conduct a regular program of routine maintenance for the property. ... Owner/Operator shall repaint or retreat all painted or treated areas at least once every 8 years; provided that the Planning Director may approve less frequent painting or re-treatment ... The program shall be subject to review and approval by the Planning Director.

The CC&Rs do not carry these words. Whether they bind this association, and which buildings, is a question for the City or counsel.

**The statutes on the authorities shelf that tie in** (recited whole by `jason cite`; the notes give the operative words):

- **Civil Code 5550(a):** the board "shall cause to be conducted a reasonably competent and diligent visual inspection of the accessible areas of the major components" at least every three years, as part of the reserve study, and shall review the study "annually". 7.1(b)(viii)'s allowance that the analyst may undertake the inspection rests on this.
- **5550(b), (c); 5565; 5570(b)(2); 5300(b)(4):** what the study must contain, that gas, water and electrical lines are major components where 4775 makes the association responsible, and that a deferral of a major component's repair or replacement is disclosed with its justification.
- **5551(b), (i), (j):** the engineer's sample inspection of exterior elevated elements every nine years, and "The continued and ongoing maintenance and repair of the load-bearing components and associated waterproofing systems ... shall be the responsibility of the association as required by the association's governing documents." This is the one statute that points at the governing documents for the maintenance itself.
- **4775(a):** the default responsibility for the common area and exclusive use common area, and the 14-day clock to begin restoring interrupted gas, heat, water, or electrical service.
- **907, 945.5(c), 912(b) and (c):** a "homeowner", which in a common interest development includes the association (895(f)), is "obligated to follow all reasonable maintenance obligations and schedules communicated in writing" by the builder; the builder gives its maintenance recommendations with the initial sale.
- **10 CCR 2792.23(a)(8), (11):** the subdivider delivers the approved plans for facilities the association must maintain and "Any written warranty being transferred to the Association". The list has no maintenance manual; the turnover checklist the developer's counsel sent in April 2022 does.
- **A reference, not law:** the Department of Real Estate's Reserve Study Guidelines (RE 25, 2010) ask that a study state the maintenance each component's life assumes, and flag a study as incomplete if "the association does not have a documented maintenance schedule and related assumptions for each major component" or its component list omits significant common area components the CC&Rs list.

### The checklist

| What the manual must cover | Source | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| A manual is adopted | 7.1(b)(viii) "shall adopt" | A duty, with no date |
| It covers the Common Area's inspection and its maintenance | "an inspection and maintenance manual for the periodic inspection and maintenance of the Common Area" | Everything in 1.10, structure included |
| It is periodic: it sets intervals | "periodic" | The intervals are open; the City's monthly walk and the declaration's quarterly mold check are fixed floors |
| It covers the components 7.1(b) lists | 7.1(b)(i) to (vii), "without limitation" | The list is a floor |
| Who prepares it | not stated | Open. The analyst "may" undertake the inspection |
| Who adopts it, and by what act | "The Association shall adopt" | The Association acts through its Board; a resolution at an open meeting, or a rule change under Civil Code 4350 to 4365, is **a question for counsel** |
| How often it is reviewed | not stated | Open; the annual review of the reserve study (5550(a)) is the natural meeting |
| Revisions | "from time to time ... based on the Board's review" | Permissive, and only if the change does not "reduce the useful life or functionality" |
| How it relates to the reserve study | "as part of the Association statutory reserve study requirement" | The analyst's triennial inspection may carry it; RE 25 would have the study state its maintenance assumptions |
| Elevated elements | 5551(j)(2) | The association's maintenance of load-bearing components and waterproofing is required by its governing documents |
| The City's conditions | D5, D6, D7 | A monthly exterior walk; a routine maintenance program the Planning Director reviews; repainting at least every 8 years unless approved otherwise |

## The manual's proposed outline

For the board to adopt, amend, or reject. Where the documents are silent, this is a proposal and not a rule. A reserve analyst can supply the component inventory, the useful lives, each component's recommended interval, the maintenance behind each life, and the condition at the triennial visit. A licensed professional decides roofing, waterproofing and structural method, fire sprinkler work, and the irrigation audit. Counsel decides the adoption act and whether the City's conditions bind.

1. **Authority and standing.** 7.1(b)(viii), 7.1(a), the City's conditions; the adopting act and its date; the version history; who may revise and the limit on revision (no reduction of useful life or functionality).
2. **Scope and definitions.** The Common Area (1.10) and exclusive use common area (1.20); who maintains what (the declaration's table: 7.1, 7.4, with the three cost centers).
3. **The component register.** For each component: description and quantity, location by building, cost center, the reserve study's component and useful life, the responsible party, the inspection interval and method, the maintenance task and interval, the assumption behind the life, the record kept, and the vendor or contract.
4. **The inspection program.** The monthly exterior walk (a checklist and a log); the quarterly mold check (by cross-reference to the mold program); the annual and seasonal inspections; the triennial reserve inspection (5550(a)); the nine-year elevated-elements inspection (5551) and the maintenance between.
5. **The maintenance program.** By season and by component group: roofs and gutters, exterior surfaces and paint, balconies and decks, streets and walks, drainage, landscaping and irrigation, lighting, mechanical and water equipment, and the cross-references to the fire, backflow, pest and mold programs.
6. **Emergencies and entry.** The 14-day restoration clock (4775(b)), entry under 7.2 (24 hours' notice; emergencies), temporary relocation under 7.7.
7. **Records.** What is kept, where, for how long: the logs, the reports, the contracts and invoices and board approvals (Civil Code 5200), the elevated-element reports for two inspection cycles (5551(i)), the Planning Director's approval if a longer paint cycle is used.
8. **Annual review.** At the October meeting with the reserve study's annual review (5550(a)): what was done against what was scheduled; the deferred items and their disclosure (5300(b)(4)); the revisions; the minute that records the review.
9. **Owner responsibilities** (a short appendix; not the manual's subject): 7.4, 7.8(b), 7.10.

## The reference material for a review of this program

These are the proposals for the general design to generalize. Collections are code, not data (`jason.community.document_collections`: the only named collections are legal cases), so the definition is a proposal to add, not something built; the details are in the private notes.

- **The collection `program-maintenance-manual`.** Members: the mandating provisions and their neighbors; the City's conditions; the developer's manual and warranty set; the board's drafts (the chart, the mold program); the studies and the working component list; the contracts and proposals by kind; the minutes and agendas; the inspection reports. Not members: privileged material and the generated summary page.
- **The context pack by tier.** S: the reserve study and inspection sections and 5551, as of the review day. G: the mandating provisions with their defined terms. R: the developer's manual, the studies, the contracts, the adoption minutes, the inspection records. C: the collection. F: jason's records (the duty reading, the deadlines, the minutes catalog, the conflicts). D: the draft outline or the draft resolution.
- **The lens.** Every component covered; an interval set; an interval that agrees across sources; a responsible party; an adopting act; implemented on schedule; aligned with the study's components and lives; no double count; a revision that does not reduce useful life; reviewed annually; the City's conditions carried. Each finding states the basis it read: the document's text, the specification, another store, the date, or the law.
- **Ingestion gaps.** There is no document kind for a manual, a schedule, a responsibility chart, a worksheet, an equipment manual, or a warranty; a Drive Doc outside a Drive root has no path rule; the Docs are not in the passage index; the developer-file model treats "maintenance plans" as the approved plan sets; a duty that names an output document does not link to it; a City condition of approval is not outlined as a duty; "the manual" is a reference nothing resolves; a sheet of dated worksheets is not read; the reserve study's per-component narratives are not extracted; and minutes' verbs (delegated, directed, adopted) are not told apart.
