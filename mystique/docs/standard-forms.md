## Leads for the board and counsel

These are readings, not rules, and jason resolves none. Each should be read in full by a person before anyone acts on it.

1. **A maximum response time for architectural applications.** CIV 4765(a)(1): "The procedure shall state the maximum time for response to an application or a request for reconsideration by the board." The passages found for B-18 and the printed application state the owner's 30 days of lead time and no board response time. Whether B-18 states one elsewhere is for a person to check in the whole section. If it does not, this is a gap (not yet a `Conflict` row); the policy to propose would adopt a maximum response time, such as the statute's own 60-day marks for the protected uses, for the board to adopt.
2. **The rental application against CIV 4740(c) and 4741.** The existing conflict row concerns the cap (4.15(a) against CIV 4741(b)). Three more things are for counsel to read, none a conflict yet:
   - 4.15(e) bars the Board from considering "the identity of the proposed tenants", while 4.15(m)(iii) asks for "proof of a current criminal background check and proof of a credit report for each prospective tenant", and CIV 4740(c) asks an owner, before renting, for "the name and contact information of the prospective tenant or lessee". A reading that gives every provision effect: the proof goes to the manager and the tenant's identity stays out of the decision; whether that satisfies 4740(c) and 4741(a)'s bar on rules that "unreasonably" restrict rental is the open question.
   - CIV 4741(a): no rule that "prohibits, has the effect of prohibiting, or unreasonably restricts" rental. Whether a priority list (4.15(i)) and a board approval of each rental are such a restriction is a question the statute leaves to counsel.
   - CIV 4741(e): an owner-occupied separate interest is not counted as rented. How 4.15(a)'s count treats them is for the board's counting rule.
3. **A flat fee for copies of records.** The fee schedule lists "Copies of Records (Civ. 5205)" at "$20 per document". CIV 5205(f): "The association may bill the requesting member for the direct and actual cost of copying and mailing requested documents." A flat per-document amount may exceed the direct and actual cost; for the board and counsel.
4. **Variance hearings are not discipline hearings.** CC&Rs 4.20(b) requires a hearing with 15 days' notice to all Members; it is a different procedure from CIV 5855. A variance that concerns a physical change is also an application under CIV 4765, whose reconsideration right is at an open board meeting. Reading 4.20 and 4765 together to give both effect: the 4.20 hearing and decision are the first decision, and reconsideration under 4765(a)(5) follows a disapproval.
5. **A "shall impose a fine" for registration.** B-1(d): "The association shall impose a fine for failure of an owner to maintain updated registration." A fine is valid only under a schedule distributed to members and after the hearing CIV 5855 provides; the rule is the board's to apply, not the manager's.

6. **A cross-reference that points at the wrong section.** The Owner's Manual's parking-permit rule (B-12(o)) cites "CC&Rs §4.20" for the permit program, and 4.20 is Variances; the parking provisions are 4.11. A person should read both and correct the Manual's citation, or say what the permit program rests on.

## What would be forms, in order

1. **Rental application** (with the exception request and the rehearing request): the most specific, the clocks are in the documents, and it needs the board's decision on `rental-approvals-4-15` before it is offered.
2. **Variance request**: one decider, one hearing, three clocks.
3. **Home improvement request**: already on paper; the form is the existing one, with a board response time added once the board adopts one.
4. **Request to inspect or copy records** (CIV 5205, 5210), with the written designation of a representative.
5. **Payment plan request**: the owner has 15 days from the notice, so it is time-critical.
6. **Resident registration**: a Google Form exists; it becomes a campaign with a handler of its own (the register), since no law governs it.
7. **Parking and guest permits**: a convenience form; a register.

Each gets a procedure (`jason sop`) and a handler before a copy is sent ([arrivals-design.md](../../docs/arrivals-design.md#the-handler-is-chosen-when-the-form-is-made)).
