# Candidate nomination and candidate statement

Status: design (2026-10-05). Key `candidate-nomination`; proposed marker code `CN`. It follows the standard in [form-templates.md](../form-templates.md). The inventory row is "Candidate nomination and statement" in [standard-forms.md](../standard-forms.md) (family one: “Run for the board; nominate”, no form today). The election itself is the `election` procedure and the notice rows of [notices.md](../notices.md); this page is the member’s form.

## 1. Authority and what the law requires

As of: the shelf’s 2025 session publication of the code (`data/authorities/CIV/CIV-5100-5145.md`, `CIV-4900-4955.md`, `CIV-5900-5920.md`, read with lawlibrary, 2026-10-05). CIV 5105 is as amended by Stats. 2024, Ch. 383 (operative 2025-01-01); CIV 5115 as amended by Stats. 2025, Ch. 67 (operative 2026-01-01); CIV 5103 as amended by Stats. 2023, Ch. 770 (operative 2024-01-01). jason’s copy is kept from the publication; it is not an official restatement, and the official page controls.

**CIV 5105(a)(3), the nomination method the election rules must carry:**

> (a) An association shall adopt operating rules in accordance with the procedures prescribed by Article 5 (commencing with Section 4340) of Chapter 3, that do all of the following:
>
> (3) Specify the qualifications for candidates for the board and any other elected position, subject to subdivision (b), and procedures for the nomination of candidates, consistent with the governing documents. A nomination or election procedure shall not be deemed reasonable if it disallows any member from nominating themself for election to the board.

**CIV 5105(a)(1), equal access to association media (what a candidate statement rests on):**

> (1) Ensure that if any candidate or member advocating a point of view is provided access to association media, newsletters, or internet websites during a campaign, for purposes that are reasonably related to that election, equal access shall be provided to all candidates and members advocating a point of view, including those not endorsed by the board, for purposes that are reasonably related to the election. The association shall not edit or redact any content from these communications, but may include a statement specifying that the candidate or member, and not the association, is responsible for that content.

**CIV 5105(a)(2), the meeting space:**

> (2) Ensure access to the common area meeting space, if any exists, during a campaign, at no cost, to all candidates, including those who are not incumbents, and to all members advocating a point of view, including those not endorsed by the board, for purposes reasonably related to the election.

**CIV 5105(a)(7), the candidate registration list and the voter list:**

> (7) Require retention of, as association election materials, both a candidate registration list and a voter list. The candidate list shall include names and addresses of individuals nominated as a candidate for election to the board of directors. The voter list shall include the name, voting power, and either the physical address of the voter’s separate interest, the parcel number, or both. The mailing address for the ballot shall be listed on the voter list if it differs from the physical address of the voter’s separate interest or if only the parcel number is used. The association shall permit members to verify the accuracy of their individual information on both lists at least 30 days before the ballots are distributed. The association or member shall report any errors or omissions to either list to the inspector or inspectors who shall make the corrections within two business days.

**CIV 5105(b), who must be disqualified:**

> (b) An association shall disqualify a person from a nomination as a candidate for not being a member of the association at the time of the nomination. An association shall disqualify a nominee if that person has served the maximum number of terms or sequential terms allowed by the association. A director who ceases to be a member shall be disqualified from continuing to serve as a director.
>
> (1) This subdivision does not restrict a developer from making a nomination of a nonmember candidate consistent with the voting power of the developer as set forth in the regulations of the Department of Real Estate and the association’s governing documents.
>
> (2) If title to a separate interest parcel is held by a legal entity that is not a natural person, the governing authority of that legal entity shall have the power to appoint a natural person to be a member for purposes of this article.

**CIV 5105(c), who the rules may disqualify** (and only through the bylaws or the election operating rules):

> (c) Through its bylaws or election operating rules adopted pursuant to subdivision (a) only, an association may disqualify a person from nomination as a candidate pursuant to any of the following:
>
> (1) Subject to paragraph (2) of subdivision (d), an association may require a nominee for a board seat, and a director during their board tenure, to be current in the payment of regular and special assessments, which are consumer debts subject to validation. If an association requires a nominee to be current in the payment of regular and special assessments, it shall also require a director to be current in the payment of regular and special assessments.
>
> (2) An association may disqualify a person from nomination as a candidate if the person, if elected, would be serving on the board at the same time as another person who holds a joint ownership interest in the same separate interest parcel as the person and the other person is either properly nominated for the current election or an incumbent director.
>
> (3) An association may disqualify a nominee if that person has been a member of the association for less than one year.
>
> (4) An association may disqualify a nominee if that person discloses, or if the association is aware or becomes aware of, a past criminal conviction that would, if the person was elected, either prevent the association from purchasing the insurance required by Section 5806 or terminate the association’s existing insurance coverage required by Section 5806 as to that person should the person be elected.

**CIV 5105(d) and (e), what may not be a ground:**

> (d) An association may disqualify a person from nomination for nonpayment of regular and special assessments, but may not disqualify a nominee for nonpayment of fines, fines renamed as assessments, collection charges, late charges, or costs levied by a third party. The person shall not be disqualified for failure to be current in payment of regular and special assessments if either of the following circumstances is true:
>
> (1) The person has paid the regular or special assessment under protest pursuant to Section 5658.
>
> (2) The person has entered into and is in compliance with a payment plan pursuant to Section 5665.
>
> (e) An association shall not disqualify a person from nomination if the person has not been provided the opportunity to engage in internal dispute resolution pursuant to Article 2 (commencing with Section 5900) of Chapter 10.

**CIV 5105(g), nominations from the floor and write-ins:**

> (g) Except as provided in subdivision (i), notwithstanding any other law, the rules adopted pursuant to this section may provide for the nomination of candidates from the floor of membership meetings or nomination by any other manner. Those rules may permit write-in candidates for ballots.

(5105(i)(1)(F): an election rule for electronic secret ballots shall include “Prohibiting nomination of candidates from the floor of membership meetings, notwithstanding subdivision (g).”)

**CIV 5115(a), the notice of the procedure and the deadline:**

> (a) An association shall provide general notice of the procedure and deadline for submitting a nomination at least 30 days before any deadline for submitting a nomination. Individual notice shall be delivered pursuant to Section 4040 if individual notice is requested by a member. This subdivision shall only apply to elections of directors and to recall elections.

**CIV 5115(b), the list of candidates:**

> (b) For elections of directors and for recall elections, an association shall provide general notice of all of the following at least 30 days before the ballots are distributed:
>
> (4) The list of all candidates’ names that will appear on the ballot.

**CIV 5103(b) and (c), where the association may seat qualified candidates without balloting** (the Act’s only stated clocks for a nomination):

> (b) The association provided individual notice of the election and the procedure for nominating candidates as follows:
>
> (1) Initial notice at least 90 days before the deadline for submitting nominations provided for in subdivision (a) of Section 5115. ...
>
> (2) A reminder notice between 7 and 30 days before the deadline for submitting nominations provided for in subdivision (a) of Section 5115. ...
>
> (c) (1) The association provides, within seven business days of receiving a nomination, a written or electronic communication acknowledging the nomination to the member who submitted the nomination.
>
> (2) The association provides, within seven business days of receiving a nomination, a written or electronic communication to the nominee, indicating either of the following:
>
> (A) The nominee is a qualified candidate for the board of directors.
>
> (B) The nominee is not a qualified candidate for the board of directors, the basis for the disqualification, and the procedure, which shall comply with Article 2 (commencing with Section 5900) of Chapter 10, by which the nominee may appeal the disqualification.
>
> (3) The association may combine the written or electronic communication described in paragraphs (1) and (2) into a single written or electronic communication if the nominee and the nominator are the same person.

(5103 opens: the association “may, but is not required to, consider the qualified candidates elected by acclamation” if its conditions are met, among them that “the number of qualified candidates is not more than the number of vacancies to be elected, as determined by the inspector or inspectors of the elections”; 5103(d)(1): “The association permits all candidates to run if nominated, except for nominees disqualified for running as allowed or required pursuant to subdivisions (b) to (e), inclusive, of Section 5105.”)

**CIV 5110, the inspector of elections** (the parts that bear on a nomination):

> (a) The association shall select an independent third party or parties as an inspector of elections. The number of inspectors of elections shall be one or three.
>
> (b) For the purposes of this section, an independent third party includes, but is not limited to, a volunteer poll worker with the county registrar of voters, a licensee of the California Board of Accountancy, or a notary public. An independent third party may be a member, but may not be a director or a candidate for director or be related to a director or to a candidate for director. ...
>
> (c) The inspector or inspectors of elections shall do all of the following:
>
> (1) Determine the number of memberships entitled to vote and the voting power of each.
>
> (5) Hear and determine all challenges and questions in any way arising out of or in connection with the right to vote.

**CIV 5105(h)(1) to (3), who may vote** (the voters’ side of the same election):

> (h) Notwithstanding any other law, the rules adopted pursuant to this section shall do all of the following:
>
> (1) Prohibit the denial of a ballot to a member for any reason other than not being a member at the time when ballots are distributed.
>
> (2) Prohibit the denial of a ballot to a person with general power of attorney for a member.
>
> (3) Require the ballot of a person with general power of attorney for a member to be counted if returned in a timely manner.

**CIV 5135, association money and a candidate’s name:**

> (a) Association funds shall not be used for campaign purposes in connection with any association board election. ...
>
> (b) For the purposes of this section, “campaign purposes” includes, but is not limited to, the following:
>
> (2) Including the photograph or prominently featuring the name of any candidate on a communication from the association or its board, excepting the ballot, ballot materials, or a communication that is legally required, within 30 days of an election. This is not a campaign purpose if the communication is one for which subdivision (a) of Section 5105 requires that equal access be provided to another candidate or advocate.

**What the Act does not say** (so the form does not say it either): the deadline for a nomination (5115(a) speaks of “any deadline”; 5105(a)(3) leaves the nomination “procedures” to the election rules); whether a nominee’s consent is required; the form of a candidate statement or its length; who determines a nominee’s qualification outside 5103; how long the nominee has to appeal. For each the form says “the election rules set it” and the page proposes a policy.

## 2. Who uses it, and when

- **A member** (an owner of a separate interest, CIV 4160) who nominates themself, or nominates another member, for a seat on the board; and a nominee who gives a candidate statement.
- **When:** only while the nomination period is open. The period is the one the election rules set and the general notice of 5115(a) announces; the form is printed or posted with that notice and carries its dates (`{NOMINATION_DEADLINE}`, filled for each election, section 10).
- **Who it is not for:** a member voting (the ballot); a member who wants to run in a recall or an election of an officer by the board (a different procedure); a member asking whom the association has nominated (the candidate list: [records-request.md](records-request.md) for the records, and the general notice of the candidates 5115(b)(4) requires); a member challenging a result (CIV 5145).
- **A nomination from the floor** (5105(g)) is recorded by the secretary or the inspector on this form, in the member’s presence, when the election rules allow it.

Not asked, on purpose:
- **Anything about a criminal conviction.** 5105(c)(4) lets the rules disqualify a nominee who “discloses”, or whom the association “is aware or becomes aware of”, a conviction that would affect insurance. It does not ask the association to ask. A question would be one the Act does not require; counsel decides, with the insurer’s terms, whether the election rules adopt 5105(c)(4) and whether and how to ask (lead 5).
- **Anything about money owed** (assessments, fines): the association reads its own ledger; 5105(d) bars disqualification for fines, late charges, and collection costs, and for an assessment paid under protest or under a payment plan in compliance.
- **How long the nominee has been a member, how many terms they have served, whether a co-owner is also running**: the association reads these from its records (5105(b), (c)(2), (c)(3)).
- **Age, occupation, a photograph, or a phone number.** A photograph is not asked; see lead 3.

## 6. What the member is told

The sentence the member reads (`member_clock`):

> You may nominate yourself, or another member, for the board. Nominations are open until {NOMINATION_DEADLINE}; the election rules set that date, and the election rules and this notice are the whole procedure. Within seven business days after we receive a nomination we will tell the member who sent it that we have it, and we will tell the nominee whether the nominee is a qualified candidate; if not, we will say why, and how to appeal. The election’s inspector, an independent person who is not a director or a candidate, handles the lists and counts the votes. Every member may check the list of voters at least 30 days before ballots go out. Every member who is a member on the day ballots are sent may vote. A candidate may give a short statement; we will print it as written and will not edit it, and we may add a note that the candidate, not the association, is responsible for it. Send this form to {NOMINATION_RETURN}, or answer it online at {PORTAL}.

Reading (label: the association’s plain-words note and its proposed policy, not the statute): the first sentence’s “the election rules and this notice are the whole procedure” states 5105(a)(3). “Within seven business days” is the Act’s number in 5103(c), which applies to an election where the association wants the option of acclamation; the proposed policy applies it to every election so the member has one clock (section 7, rows 1 and 2). “Every member who is a member on the day ballots are sent may vote” reads 5105(h)(1).

Who decides: **PROPOSED POLICY** (the Act is silent outside 5103): the inspector of elections reads each nomination against the election rules’ qualifications and reports to the association whether the nominee is qualified and the basis; the association sends the communication of 5103(c)(2) in the inspector’s words. The Act names the inspector for “the number of qualified candidates” in an acclamation (5103) and for “challenges and questions” about “the right to vote” (5110(c)(5)); it does not say who determines qualification otherwise. The election rules say; where they do not, the board adopts the proposal in writing.

How to reconsider: a nominee found not qualified may appeal by the procedure of CIV 5900 to 5920 (5103(c)(2)(B)); “an association shall not disqualify a person from nomination if the person has not been provided the opportunity to engage in internal dispute resolution” (5105(e)). A member may also bring an action within the year 5145 allows. The notice of non-qualification says so, and says how to ask.

## 7. The association’s clocks

“Counted from” is the association’s receipt, stamped on the day it arrives (mail on the day opened; email and PayHOA on the day sent; a nomination from the floor on the day of the meeting).

| # | Counted from | Number and kind of day | Who sets it | What follows if it passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | receipt of a nomination | acknowledge to the member who submitted it within 7 business days | statute where acclamation is to be available (5103(c)(1)); PROPOSED POLICY to apply it to every election | the board may not seat the qualified candidates by acclamation (5103(c)); otherwise a member left without a date |
| 2 | receipt of a nomination | tell the nominee “qualified” or “not qualified, the basis, and how to appeal” within 7 business days | as row 1 (5103(c)(2)) | as row 1 |
| 3 | the day the nomination period ends | the election rules set the deadline; **PROPOSED POLICY (a shape, not a date):** a stated calendar date, set when the board calls the election, and not before the later of (a) 30 days after the general notice of the procedure and deadline (5115(a)) and (b) where acclamation is to be available, 90 days after the initial individual notice (5103(b)(1)) | the election rules (5105(a)(3)); the number floors are statute | a nomination after it is a late nomination; the rules say what happens |
| 4 | the deadline | the general notice of the procedure and deadline goes out at least 30 days before it; where acclamation is to be available, an individual initial notice 90 days before and a reminder between 7 and 30 days before | statute: 5115(a); 5103(b) | the deadline is not valid until noticed; acclamation is not available |
| 5 | the day ballots are distributed | the list of all candidates’ names in the general notice, at least 30 days before ballots are distributed; so a nomination deadline must fall at least 30 days before that day for the list to be known (a reading: 5115(b)(4)) | statute: 5115(b)(4); the arithmetic is a reading | the notice lists an incomplete slate |
| 6 | the day ballots are distributed | the inspector delivers the ballot and the election rules at least 30 days before an election; ballots mailed or delivered “not less than 30 days prior to the deadline for voting” | statute: 5105(h)(4); 5115(c) | the election is open to challenge (5145) |
| 7 | the day ballots are distributed | members may verify the candidate and voter lists at least 30 days before; errors reported to the inspector are corrected “within two business days” | statute: 5105(a)(7) | as row 6 |
| 8 | the election | the qualification block of the election rules is not changed less than 90 days before the election | statute: 5105(h)(4)(B)(iii) | the changed rule does not apply to this election |
| 9 | the nomination deadline | candidate statements are due the same day, so they are published together; the election rules may set another day | PROPOSED POLICY (the Act sets none) | a late statement is still published, in the media that remain open, and the candidate is told |
| 10 | the day a disqualification notice is sent | the nominee may ask to meet and confer within 5 business days; the association designates a director who is not a candidate; the parties meet “promptly” (5915(b)(4)) | PROPOSED POLICY for the 5 days; statute for the right (5103(c)(2)(B), 5105(e)) | the disqualification is not final; 5105(e) bars it without the opportunity |
| 11 | a vote to seat by acclamation | at a board meeting under CIV 4900 to 4955, on an agenda that names each qualified candidate to be seated | statute: 5103(e) | the acclamation is void |

A “business day” is not defined on the shelf. PROPOSED POLICY: the association states the definition it uses on the form (`{BUSINESS_DAY_DEFINITION}`) and counts the day after receipt as day 1.

## 8. The acknowledgment text

To the member who submitted the nomination (and, where the nominee and the nominator are the same person, combined with the nominee’s, 5103(c)(3)):

> Reference {REFERENCE}. We received a nomination of {NOMINEE} for {SEAT} on {RECEIVED}. We will tell the nominee by {DUE} whether the nominee is a qualified candidate. {DECIDER} reads each nomination against the election rules. Nominations are open until {NOMINATION_DEADLINE}. To add to or withdraw this nomination, use the same form or write to {RETURN_BY_EMAIL} or {RETURN_BY_MAIL} and quote the reference.

To the nominee:

> Reference {REFERENCE}. {QUALIFIED_OR_NOT}. If you are a qualified candidate, your name will be on the list of candidates in the general notice we give at least 30 days before ballots are distributed, and you may give a candidate statement by {STATEMENT_DUE}. If you are not, the reason is stated above and you may ask to meet and confer under Civil Code 5900 to 5920 by {APPEAL_BY}. The inspector of elections is {INSPECTOR}.

`{DUE}` is 7 business days after `{RECEIVED}` (row 1). `{QUALIFIED_OR_NOT}` is “You are a qualified candidate for {SEAT}” or “You are not a qualified candidate: {BASIS}”.

## 9. Channels and the reference

- **Channels:** paper, fillable PDF, email, PayHOA, portal, and the floor of a meeting where the election rules allow it. A signed-in PayHOA answer is `SIGNED_IN` (the member and the unit are known); a reply email from the address on file is `MATCHED`; a signed paper copy is `CLAIMED` ([forms.md](../forms.md)). A Google Form is not offered: a nomination is a member’s act, and an unsigned-in answer could place a name on a candidate list, so it is a lead only. An election is conducted by an independent inspector, so the nominations are delivered to the inspector or to the person the election rules name; PayHOA is the association’s copy.
- **Marker code proposed: `CN`** (candidate nomination). Letters from the alphabet `0-9 A C E F H K M N P R T V X`; `NP`, `NC`, `NA`, `NV`, `RR` are in use or proposed in sibling pages; `CN` collides with no code found in `docs/` or `mystique/forms.py`. A campaign is the year of the election: `CN26M-…` for the mailed blank, `CN26P-…` for the PayHOA form. The marker names the campaign only; each nomination also gets a reference number from the register.
- The reference is a hint, never what the reading depends on ([form-identifiers.md](../form-identifiers.md)).

## 11. Handler and procedure

- **Handler:** `@handler("CIV 5105", "CIV 5103", role=Role.FORM_RETURN, form="candidate-nomination", procedure="election", channels=(PAYHOA, GMAIL, MAIL))` ([arrivals-design.md](../arrivals-design.md)). `accepts` takes an arrival with this form’s marker (`CN`) or a PayHOA form id. `read` is evidence only. `plan` does four things and writes nothing outside jason: it stamps the receipt date and opens the two seven-business-day clocks (as schedule occurrences); it drafts the two acknowledgments (`acknowledgment_draft`); it hands the nomination to the inspector (or to the person the rules name) with the question “qualified or not, and the basis”, and keeps the candidate registration list entry as election material; and it keeps a register row (who, unit, date received, status), because the candidate list is custody of the inspector until the challenge period ends (CIV 5125).
- **Procedure:** `election` exists in `src/jason/community/procedures.py` (“An election: of directors, a recall, or a member vote”); its steps read the notices the election requires and record the seats filled, and none takes a nomination. Add a step after “Read each notice’s requirement”: **receive each nomination, stamp the day, acknowledge the submitter and tell the nominee within seven business days, hand it to the inspector for the qualification reading, enter the candidate registration list, and list the qualified candidates for the 5115(b)(4) notice at least 30 days before ballots are distributed**; name its lessons when one is written.
- **Template changes this needs:** a base form with a `required_if` on `nominator-*` (only when another member is nominated) and on `entity-appointee`; a profile slot block for the election rules’ qualification questions; a campaign per election, with its own deadline.
- **jason never:** decides whether a nominee is qualified, disqualifies anyone, seats anyone, edits a candidate statement, or opens a ballot (AGENTS.md, Boundaries; 5105(a)(1), 5120).

## 12. Edge cases

- **Co-owners.** Each is a member and may be nominated; a co-owner of a unit may nominate the other. If the election rules adopt 5105(c)(2), the association disqualifies a person who would serve with a joint owner who is also nominated or an incumbent director; the association reads this from its records, and the form does not ask.
- **A representative.** The nominee is a natural person who is a member. A person with a power of attorney may submit the form for the nominee, with the authority attached; the nominee’s acceptance (`accepts`) is the nominee’s own. A general power of attorney does not make the agent the candidate. Voting is separate: 5105(h)(2), (3).
- **A tenant or another non-owner.** A person who is not a member “at the time of the nomination” is disqualified (5105(b)). The form says so and the nomination is acknowledged and answered under 5103(c)(2)(B), with the basis and the appeal. A developer’s nonmember nominee is outside this (5105(b)(1)).
- **An entity or trust holds title.** The governing authority of the entity may appoint a natural person to be a member for the election (5105(b)(2)); the form asks the person’s name and attaches the appointment.
- **A nomination from the floor, or a write-in.** Only if the election rules allow it (5105(g)); not where the rules provide electronic secret ballots (5105(i)(1)(F)). The secretary or inspector writes it on this form at the meeting, and the same clocks run from that day.
- **Language and accessibility.** The statutory words stay in English; large print, another format, a person to read the form aloud or write it down, and a reasonable accommodation are offered. A candidate statement is printed as written; a translation is the candidate’s to supply.
- **No form used.** An email or letter that names a nominee is a nomination if it reaches the person or address the election rules name before the deadline. The handler reads it into the same fields, marks what is missing, and asks once; the clocks run from receipt.
- **The request is really something else.** A member asking “who is running” is asking for the list of candidates in the notice of 5115(b)(4), not the candidate registration list; a member asking to see the voter list is within 5105(a)(7); a member asking for the election’s ballots is for 5125. A member disputing a disqualification is meeting and conferring (5900 to 5920), not nominating.

## 13. Leads for the board and counsel

A reading is labeled as one and whose it is. A conflict between a document and the statute is noted, never resolved.

1. **The deadline.** The Act sets none. 5115(a) says “any deadline”; 5103(b) counts from it; 5115(b)(4) and (c) fix the 30-day steps after it. The board sets the date in its election rules or when it calls the election, and says so in the general notice. The shape in section 7, row 3 only keeps the dates the Act fixes in order; the date is the board’s.
2. **A candidate statement’s length.** 5105(a)(1) says the association “shall not edit or redact any content”. Whether a stated limit asked of every candidate in advance is “editing” is not answered. The form prints `{STATEMENT_LIMIT}` only if the election rules set one, and says that a statement over it will be returned for the candidate to shorten, never cut. For counsel.
3. **Photographs and names in association communications.** 5135(b)(2) makes a candidate’s photograph or prominent name in an association communication within 30 days of an election a “campaign purpose” (so not a use of association funds) unless the communication is one for which 5105(a) requires equal access. The form therefore asks no photograph and does not promise one. For counsel.
4. **Who decides qualification.** Outside acclamation the Act names no decider. The proposal of section 6 (the independent inspector reads the nomination and states the basis) keeps the board, whose members may be running, out of the reading. The board adopts it in its election rules (a rule change under CIV 4340 to 4370, `jason rule-change`); the notice and the 90-day limit of 5105(h)(4)(B)(iii) then apply.
5. **A criminal-conviction question.** 5105(c)(4) is optional for the rules and speaks of disclosure or awareness. Whether to ask is for counsel and the insurer’s terms (5806 is not on the shelf); the form asks nothing.
6. **Whether the nominee must consent.** The Act does not say. The proposal (`accepts`, required when another member nominates) avoids a name on a ballot without the person’s agreement; the election rules may say otherwise.
7. **The seven business days outside acclamation.** 5103(c) is a condition of acclamation. The proposal applies it to every election. If the board does not want acclamation available, the rule is optional; the form’s clock is still the better practice.
8. **The IDR route for an election dispute.** 5915(b)(3) has the board designate a director to meet and confer. A nominee’s dispute with a board of which the nominee may be a member needs a director who is not a candidate. For the board and counsel.
9. **Privacy of the candidate list.** 5105(a)(7) requires the candidate list to hold names and addresses and be retained. Who may inspect it and when is for the records article (5200 to 5240) and counsel; this form tells the nominee that the name and address are kept as election material.
10. **Voter eligibility and the unit’s co-owners.** 5105(a)(4) leaves the voting power of each membership to the election rules and governing documents. The form does not restate it.

Expected: a candidate list entry (C. Candidate, 125 Main St); acknowledgments to both by 7 business days after the day received; the statement kept as written; the nomination passed to the inspector with the question.

**Minimal** (the smallest valid return, a self-nomination): `self` = myself, `nominee-name`, `nominee-unit`, `member-statement`, signature. Expected: the combined acknowledgment (5103(c)(3)); no statement.

**Edge cases:**
- A tenant is nominated by an owner: “not a qualified candidate: not a member at the time of the nomination (5105(b))”, with the appeal procedure.
- The unit is held by a trust; `entity-appointee` blank: incomplete; one request for the appointment.
- Two co-owners nominated together while the rules adopt 5105(c)(2): the second is read from the records; the communication states the basis.
- A nomination received the day after the deadline: acknowledged with the date; “late” per the rules; the member told.
- A statement over `{STATEMENT_LIMIT}`: returned to the candidate to shorten, never cut.
- A nomination received without a marker (a photocopy): identified by its printed lines and title.
- A nomination from the floor: written on the form by the secretary; the clocks run from the meeting’s day.

**The statutory checklist test, in words.** (1) The recital block contains the exact words of 5105(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(3), (b), (c), (d), (e), 5115(a), 5103(c), 5110(a), (b), and 5105(h), each filled from the shelf, with the build failing if a cited subdivision is gone. (2) The form offers a self-nomination and a nomination of another. (3) The form says that a member of the association at the time of the nomination is the only basis for a disqualification the form states itself; every other ground is the election rules’ own, each printed with its section. (4) The form never asks about a conviction, a fine, a late charge, or an amount owed. (5) The form’s statement block says the association will not edit or redact it and may add the responsibility note. (6) The form names where nominations go and the nomination deadline as filled from the election rules, and the build fails if `{NOMINATION_DEADLINE}` is empty. (7) The acknowledgments carry `{RECEIVED}`, `{DUE}`, `{DECIDER}`, `{REFERENCE}`, and the nominee’s carries the basis and the appeal. (8) The form says who may vote (5105(h)(1)) and that the voter list may be checked (5105(a)(7)). (9) The same template renders paper, PDF, email, and PayHOA with the same questions in the same order, each carrying `CN`. (10) A made-up return of each fixture reads back to the same answers after a bad scan.
