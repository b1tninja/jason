# Reasonable accommodation or modification request

**Pointer: outside the authorities shelf.** The federal Fair Housing Act and California’s Fair Employment and Housing Act (Government Code 12900 and following, with 12927 and 12955) are not on jason’s authorities shelf and are not quoted here. This page is written from what the shelf does say: the Act’s own references to them, the Act’s sections on modifications for a disability and on pets, and the architectural-procedure section. Where the page says what the fair-housing law requires, it says “for counsel”. The words below that are quoted are the shelf’s words and only those.

Status: design (2026-10-05); built in the US pack of the library as version 1 (2026-10-05), `src/jason/community/form_library/us/accommodation.py`, marker code `AM`, handler `response-clock` with procedure `respond` (the `accommodation-request` procedure and the restricted-store flag are not built; the module's docstring lists where it departs from this page). Key `accommodation-request`. It follows the standard in [form-templates.md](../form-templates.md). The inventory row is "Reasonable accommodation request" in [standard-forms.md](../standard-forms.md) (family one: “not on the authorities shelf; a question for counsel”). No form of this kind exists today. **The decision is counsel’s to read first** (section 6); jason never decides, never copies what a member says about their health into a shared record, and never refuses a request for being on the wrong form.

## 1. Authority and what the law requires

As of: the shelf’s 2025 session publication of the code (`data/authorities/CIV/CIV-4700-4753.md`, `CIV-4760-4766.md`, `CIV-4600-4620.md`, `CIV-4205-4235.md`, `CIV-4900-4955.md`, read with lawlibrary, 2026-10-05). jason’s copy is kept from the publication; it is not an official restatement, and the official page controls.

**CIV 4765(a)(3), the Act’s own reference to the Fair Employment and Housing Act:**

> (3) Notwithstanding a contrary provision of the governing documents, a decision on a proposed change may not violate any governing provision of law, including, but not limited to, the Fair Employment and Housing Act (Part 2.8 (commencing with Section 12900) of Division 3 of Title 2 of the Government Code), or a building code or other applicable law governing land use or public safety.

**CIV 4700, the article that lists the limits the Act itself notes** (intro and (e)):

> This article includes provisions that limit the authority of an association or the governing documents to regulate the use of a member’s separate interest. Nothing in this article is intended to affect the application of any other provision that limits the authority of an association to regulate the use of a member’s separate interest, including, but not limited to, the following provisions:
>
> (e) Section 12927 of the Government Code, relating to the modification of property to accommodate a disability.

**CIV 4760(a)(2), a member’s right to modify for access, and what the association may do with the plans:**

> (a) Subject to the governing documents and applicable law, a member may do the following:
>
> (2) Modify the member’s separate interest, at the member’s expense, to facilitate access for persons who are blind, visually handicapped, deaf, or physically disabled, or to alter conditions which could be hazardous to these persons. These modifications may also include modifications of the route from the public way to the door of the separate interest for the purposes of this paragraph if the separate interest is on the ground floor or already accessible by an existing ramp or elevator. The right granted by this paragraph is subject to the following conditions:
>
> (A) The modifications shall be consistent with applicable building code requirements.
>
> (B) The modifications shall be consistent with the intent of otherwise applicable provisions of the governing documents pertaining to safety or aesthetics.
>
> (C) Modifications external to the dwelling shall not prevent reasonable passage by other residents, and shall be removed by the member when the separate interest is no longer occupied by persons requiring those modifications who are blind, visually handicapped, deaf, or physically disabled.
>
> (D) Any member who intends to modify a separate interest pursuant to this paragraph shall submit plans and specifications to the association for review to determine whether the modifications will comply with the provisions of this paragraph. The association shall not deny approval of the proposed modifications under this paragraph without good cause.

**CIV 4765(a)(1), (2), (4), and (5), the architectural procedure** (it governs a physical change, whatever its reason):

> (1) The association shall provide a fair, reasonable, and expeditious procedure for making its decision. The procedure shall be included in the association’s governing documents. The procedure shall provide for prompt deadlines. The procedure shall state the maximum time for response to an application or a request for reconsideration by the board.
>
> (2) A decision on a proposed change shall be made in good faith and may not be unreasonable, arbitrary, or capricious.
>
> (4) A decision on a proposed change shall be in writing. If a proposed change is disapproved, the written decision shall include both an explanation of why the proposed change is disapproved and a description of the procedure for reconsideration of the decision by the board.
>
> (5) If a proposed change is disapproved, the applicant is entitled to reconsideration by the board, at an open meeting of the board. This paragraph does not require reconsideration of a decision that is made by the board or a body that has the same membership as the board, at a meeting that satisfies the requirements of Article 2 (commencing with Section 4900) of Chapter 6. Reconsideration by the board does not constitute dispute resolution within the meaning of Section 5905.

**CIV 4715(a), pets, and what it does not affect:**

> (a) No governing documents shall prohibit the owner of a separate interest within a common interest development from keeping at least one pet within the common interest development, subject to reasonable rules and regulations of the association. This section may not be construed to affect any other rights provided by law to an owner of a separate interest to keep a pet within the development.

**CIV 4600(b)(3)(F), a grant of exclusive use “to accommodate a disability”** (the 67 percent member vote of 4600(a) “does not apply” to it):

> (b) Subdivision (a) does not apply to the following actions:
>
> (3) Any grant of exclusive use that is for any of the following reasons:
>
> (F) To accommodate a disability.

**CIV 4225(a), the Act’s reference to the Government Code’s section on restrictive covenants:**

> (a) No declaration or other governing document shall include a restrictive covenant in violation of Section 12955 of the Government Code.

**What the shelf does not say** (and so this page does not): who may ask (an owner, a resident, a person acting for them); what a request must contain; what supporting information an association may ask for; how long the association has to respond; whether a request must be in writing; what “reasonable” means; what the association may charge. The statutes that answer these are outside the shelf. The page sets out the form’s shape and a proposed clock and leaves each of these to counsel.

## 2. Who uses it, and when

- **An owner or a resident** who needs a change to a rule, a policy, or a practice (an accommodation), or a change to the home or the common area (a modification), because of a disability. A person acting for them, such as a family member or a caregiver, may also ask. The form does not decide who may ask; **a request from any of them is received and answered** (section 12).
- **When:** at any time. A request may be made orally or in writing; the form makes it easy, and **it is never required**. A request in other words is a request.
- **Who it is not for:** a physical change that is not about a disability (the architectural application: [improvement-requests.md](../../mystique/docs/improvement-requests.md) for the worked profile, and the base template when it exists); a hearing before discipline; a payment plan.

**How it differs from an architectural application.** An architectural application (CIV 4765) asks the association to approve a physical change under its procedure, which “shall state the maximum time for response” (4765(a)(1)). It is decided on the standards the documents state and on good faith, and it is reconsidered at an open meeting of the board when disapproved (4765(a)(5)). An accommodation request asks for an exception to, or a change in, a rule, policy, or practice, or for a modification, **because of a disability**; it has no architectural procedure for a rule change, and the clock is not the architectural procedure’s. A request may need both (a ramp is both): then one record keeps both, the accommodation request starts the clock on the first day the need is mentioned, and the plans and specifications (4760(a)(2)(D)) are part of it. A decision on a physical change “may not violate any governing provision of law, including ... the Fair Employment and Housing Act” (4765(a)(3)); the statute that makes it so is outside the shelf.

## 6. What the member is told

The sentence the member reads (`member_clock`):

> You may ask us for a change to a rule, a policy, or a practice, or for a change to your home or the common area, because of a disability. You may ask at any time, in writing or aloud, with this form or without it. You do not need to name a condition or a diagnosis. **We will not turn your request away because it is on the wrong form, or comes from someone other than the owner.** We will tell you in writing the day we received your request. Our attorney reads every request first; the board decides, and we will give you our answer in writing, with the reasons. We will offer to talk with you about the request. If the change is to the home or the common area, we will also ask for plans, and the time our architectural procedure states applies as well. If you disagree with our answer, you may ask the board to reconsider it at an open meeting, and you may ask to meet and confer (Civil Code 5900 to 5920). The association does not give legal advice; the law on this subject is mostly outside what we have quoted here, and our attorney reads it. Send this form to {RETURN_BY_MAIL} or {RETURN_BY_EMAIL}, or answer it online at {PORTAL}, or tell {BOARD_CONTACT}, who will write it down for you.

Reading (label: the association’s plain-words note and its proposed policy, not the statute): the sentences that begin “We will tell you in writing the day we received” and “our attorney reads every request first” state the proposed policy of section 7. The sentence “we will not turn your request away” states the standard (5, 7), not a statute on the shelf.

Who decides: the board, on counsel’s reading. The shelf says only that the decision “may not violate any governing provision of law” (4765(a)(3)); who is to read the request first is the association’s choice. How to reconsider: for a physical change, 4765(a)(5) gives reconsideration at an open meeting of the board when it is disapproved; for any request, the member may ask in writing for the board to reconsider, and may ask to meet and confer (5900 to 5920).

## 7. The association’s clocks

**Every number below is PROPOSED POLICY**, for counsel and the board; none comes from the shelf. The statute that sets any time for an accommodation is outside the shelf, and counsel reads whether it sets one. “Counted from” is **the first day the need is mentioned** to the association in any form or words (the `earlier` date, or the day this form arrives, whichever is earlier), stamped on the day it arrives.

| # | Counted from | Number and kind of day | Who sets it | What follows if it passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the first mention | acknowledge in writing within 2 business days | PROPOSED POLICY (as `acknowledge_days=2`) | the member is left without a date; the association’s good-faith record is weaker |
| 2 | the first mention | counsel reads the request within 5 business days | PROPOSED POLICY | the request waits; the member is told |
| 3 | the first mention | offer to talk with the member within 5 business days | PROPOSED POLICY | the association has not engaged with the request |
| 4 | the first mention | a written decision, or a written request for the specific information needed, within 14 days | PROPOSED POLICY | the member is told why and when the answer will come |
| 5 | a complete request | a final written decision, with reasons, within 30 days | PROPOSED POLICY (for a physical change, the architectural procedure’s maximum response time also applies, 4765(a)(1): whichever is shorter) | nothing on the shelf is “deemed approved” if the association is late; whether law outside the shelf is, is for counsel |
| 6 | a disapproval of a physical change | reconsideration at an open meeting of the board | statute: 4765(a)(5) | the member may bring the matter under 5900 to 5920 and to court |
| 7 | the decision | the written decision is given to the member; the board’s minutes note it generally (4935(e) if heard in executive session) | PROPOSED POLICY; statute for the minutes | see lead 3 |

A “business day” is not defined on the shelf. PROPOSED POLICY: the association states the definition it uses on the form (`{BUSINESS_DAY_DEFINITION}`).

## 9. Channels and the reference

- **Channels:** paper, fillable PDF, email, PayHOA, portal, and a person who writes it down. The PayHOA form is the preferred channel (a signed-in answer is `SIGNED_IN`); but **no channel is required, and a request that comes in any other way is a request**. A Google Form is not offered: a response in a shared Forms account is a store of health-related text outside the restricted store (section 11).
- **Marker code proposed: `AM`** (accommodation, modification). Letters from the alphabet `0-9 A C E F H K M N P R T V X`; the codes in use or proposed in [the table of design pages](README.md) include `NP`, `NC`, `NA`, `NV`, `CN`, `HM`, `RR`, `MN`, `RT`, `MC`, `RV`, `PP`, `PR`, `AP` (the architectural application), `RC`, `EV`, `PV`, `PX`; `AM` collides with none found in `docs/` or `mystique/forms.py`. A standing form’s campaign is the year it was generated (`AM26M-…`, `AM26P-…`).
- The reference is a hint, never what the reading depends on ([form-identifiers.md](../form-identifiers.md)).

## 11. Handler and procedure

- **Handler:** `@handler("CIV 4760", "CIV 4765(a)(3)", role=Role.REQUEST, form="accommodation-request", procedure="accommodation-request", channels=(PAYHOA, GMAIL, MAIL))` ([arrivals-design.md](../arrivals-design.md)). `accepts` takes an arrival with this form’s marker (`AM`) or a PayHOA form id, **or any arrival classified as a request that mentions a disability-related need**, whatever its form: the arrival’s date is the first mention. `read` is evidence only: the request’s text is kept in a **restricted store** (level P4: never in a shared catalog, never in the board or governance tools’ open views, never in a Google Form or a register that a wider group can read), and nothing about its content is shown outside the people who must read it. `plan` stamps the first mention, opens the clocks of section 7 (as schedule occurrences), drafts the acknowledgment (`acknowledgment_draft`), and puts a single line on counsel’s list: “request R-n received on the date, kind, the clock”. It never decides.
- **Procedure:** `accommodation-request` does not exist in `src/jason/community/procedures.py`; the nearest is `respond` (“Answering members’ requests on their clocks”). Add it, with steps: (1) stamp the first mention, whatever form it came on; (2) acknowledge; (3) keep the text in the restricted store, and list only the reference for counsel; (4) counsel reads first; (5) offer to talk with the member, and record the conversation’s date and who took part, not its content; (6) if the request is for a modification, start the architectural application as part of the same record and run its clock beside this one; (7) the board decides on counsel’s reading, in writing, with reasons; (8) record the decision and the date; (9) write each failure as a lesson. The step in (1) is the one that never lets a request be refused for its form.
- **Template changes this needs:** a form kind with no signature; a restricted-store flag on a template (P4) so no copy of its answers goes to a shared place; `required_if` on `email` and `phone`.
- **jason never:** decides, or recommends a decision on, a request; reads a request’s health content into any catalog or tool; or tells a member what the law requires (AGENTS.md, Boundaries; the standard’s “Not legal advice”).

## 12. Edge cases

- **Co-owners.** Either may ask; one request is one clock; the answer goes to the one who asked.
- **A representative or advocate.** A family member, a caregiver, an attorney, or an advocate may ask for the owner or resident. The form accepts it and asks only that the person say whom it is for. Whether the association may ask for the person’s consent is for counsel.
- **A tenant or another non-owner.** The Act’s architectural procedure speaks of a “member”. The form does **not** refuse a resident, a tenant, a prospective tenant, or a buyer: it receives the request, dates it, and counsel reads who must act (the owner, the association, or both). The tenant is told that the owner may need to take part.
- **An oral request.** A person writes it down on the form, dates it, reads it back or sends it for confirmation; the date is the day the need was first mentioned.
- **A request inside another document** (a complaint, an architectural application, a pet registration, a parking permit, an email about a violation notice). The handler reads it as a request, stamps the date, and tells the member in the acknowledgment that it is being treated as one. It never says “use the other form”.
- **A request about a pet or an animal.** 4715(a) covers “at least one pet ... subject to reasonable rules and regulations”, and “may not be construed to affect any other rights provided by law”. A request that asks to keep an animal that a rule would bar is read as an accommodation request, not as a pet registration; the handler does not decide the animal’s category.
- **A request for a reserved parking space or exclusive use of a common area.** 4600(b)(3)(F) takes a grant “to accommodate a disability” out of the 67 percent vote; the board’s authority to grant one is for counsel. The request is read as an accommodation request and the parking-permit form ([parking-permit.md](../../mystique/docs/form-templates/parking-permit.md) for the worked profile) is not required.
- **Language and accessibility.** The statutory words stay in English; large print, another format, an interpreter, a person to write the request down, and a reasonable accommodation are offered, and `help` asks. A request written down for the member is returned for confirmation.
- **A request after an adverse decision on another matter** (a fine, a violation notice). Read as a request; the other matter’s clock is for counsel.
- **A request that is not for a disability** (a preference). Counsel reads whether it is; the member is told the board will consider it as any other request. It is never closed for that reason by jason.

## 13. Leads for the board and counsel

A reading is labeled as one and whose it is. A conflict between a document and the statute is noted, never resolved. **Every item below that turns on the Fair Employment and Housing Act or the federal Fair Housing Act is for counsel; those texts are not on the shelf.**

1. **The statutes are not on the shelf.** The Fair Employment and Housing Act (Government Code 12900 and following, 12927, 12955) and the federal Fair Housing Act should be added so a form’s recitals can be built and tested (`citation_gaps` lists the sections the shelf’s statutes cite and the shelf lacks). Until then this page is a pointer.
2. **Who may ask, what the association may ask for, and how long it has.** Not on the shelf. The proposed clock (section 7) and the questions (section 4) are the association’s proposal for counsel to confirm or change; the form asks no diagnosis because the shelf gives no reason to.
3. **Closed-session consideration.** 4935(a) lists the grounds for executive session: “litigation, matters relating to the formation of contracts with third parties, member discipline, personnel matters, or to meet with a member, upon the member’s request, regarding the member’s payment of assessments”. An accommodation request is not listed. How the board may consider a request that holds a member’s health information without a listed ground is for counsel. Reading (labeled): the board may delegate the decision to a committee of fewer directors than a quorum or to an agent (4155 defines an item of business as one “within the authority of the board, except those actions that the board has validly delegated”), but the decision “may not” violate the law; counsel says whether to delegate and to whom.
4. **A governing document that bars what the law allows.** 4765(a)(3) says a decision may not violate the law “notwithstanding a contrary provision of the governing documents”. Where a document’s rule would bar an accommodation, the association notes it as a `Conflict` row for the board and counsel (`jason conflicts`), and never resolves it.
5. **Modifications and the architectural procedure.** 4760(a)(2)(D) says the association “shall not deny approval ... without good cause”; 4765(a)(1) requires a stated maximum response time, and a profile whose documents state none has a gap of its own to close (the profile’s architectural page says where). The two clocks run beside each other; the shorter controls.
6. **A fee.** The form states none. Whether a fee may be charged for an accommodation request, or for an architectural application that is one, is for counsel; the architectural application fee on a profile’s schedule is not read as applying to this request.
7. **What jason keeps.** The text is a restricted record. A summary sentence for the board (“a request is open, counsel has it, the clock is on day n”) names no person and no health fact. The board decides what it sees.
8. **A request made by a prospective tenant or buyer.** Not a member; the association’s role is for counsel.
9. **The 4600(b)(3)(F) path.** The shelf says the member vote is not needed for an exclusive-use grant to accommodate a disability. Whether and how the board grants one is for counsel.
