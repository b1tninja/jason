# Protected-use application or notice (`protected-use-application`)

Status: design (2026-10-05). Form key `protected-use-application`, marker code `PX` (proposed), authority `CIV 4700 to 4753` (the sections it carries are 4705, 4706, 4710, 4715, 4720, 4725, 4735, 4736, 4750, 4751, 4752, 4753; the sections with their own pages are 4745 and 4745.1, [ev-charger.md](ev-charger.md), and 714, 714.1, and 4746, [solar.md](solar.md)). The standard it follows is [../form-templates.md](../form-templates.md). Where a use can carry an application, it runs on the same procedure (`improvement-request`) and workflow as [architectural-application.md](architectural-application.md) ([../improvement-requests.md](../improvement-requests.md), reused here and not changed).

**One generic template, two modes.** A single form has a "which protected use" choice. For each use, the form shows the section's operative words and any limit the section allows. Then it is one of three things, and says which:

- **An application**, where the governing documents may still require approval and the section limits what the association may refuse (4720, 4725, 4735(a), 4750, 4751, 4752, and 4753 for something that has to be installed).
- **A notice**, where the section gives the owner a right that needs no approval (4705, 4706, 4710, 4715 for at least one pet, 4735(c) and (e), 4736, and 4753 for a portable rack). The form is the owner's way to tell the association; it is never a condition of the right. Nothing is decided.
- **A request for an agreement**, the one case in which the section itself leaves the decision to the association and the owner ("other animal as agreed to between the association and the homeowner", 4715(b)).

Quotations are from the statutes on jason's authorities shelf (`data/authorities/CIV/CIV-4700-4753.md`), which are not official restatements. A reading is labeled as one and is never the rule.

## 1. Authority and what the law requires

**As-of.** The shelf's 2025 session publication of the Civil Code, exported 2026-10-04. The history of the sections used here: 4735 amended by Stats. 2015, Ch. 780; 4751 amended by Stats. 2024, Ch. 7 (SB 477), operative 2024-03-25; 4752 added by Stats. 2025, Ch. 548 (SB 625); 4753 added by renumbering (Stats. 2016, Ch. 714). The form's recital line says "as on jason's shelf of 2026-10-04. jason's copy is not an official restatement."

**CIV 4700, what the article is for** (opening words and the list quoted):

> This article includes provisions that limit the authority of an association or the governing documents to regulate the use of a member’s separate interest. Nothing in this article is intended to affect the application of any other provision that limits the authority of an association to regulate the use of a member’s separate interest, including, but not limited to, the following provisions: **(a)** Sections 712 and 713, relating to the display of signs. **(b)** Sections 714 and 714.1, relating to solar energy systems. **(c)** Section 714.5, relating to structures that are constructed offsite and moved to the property in sections or modules. **(d)** Sections 782, 782.5, and 6150 of this code and Section 12956.1 of the Government Code, relating to racial restrictions. **(e)** Section 12927 of the Government Code, relating to the modification of property to accommodate a disability. **(f)** Section 1597.40 of the Health and Safety Code, relating to the operation of a family day care home.

**The summary: which uses, in which mode, and where the statute leaves the association no discretion.**

Where the table says "no discretion" it states what the quoted words say; whether a particular decision falls inside or outside the words is a reading for the board and counsel, and the board never decides a case by this table: it recites the section and applies it.

### 1.1 Flag of the United States, CIV 4705

> **(a)** Except as required for the protection of the public health or safety, no governing document shall limit or prohibit, or be construed to limit or prohibit, the display of the flag of the United States by a member on or in the member’s separate interest or within the member’s exclusive use common area.
>
> **(b)** For purposes of this section, “display of the flag of the United States” means a flag of the United States made of fabric, cloth, or paper displayed from a staff or pole or in a window, and does not mean a depiction or emblem of the flag of the United States made of lights, paint, roofing, siding, paving materials, flora, or balloons, or any other similar building, landscaping, or decorative component.

**Mode: a notice, at most.** No approval exists to give. **No discretion** except the public-health-or-safety exception. The section is silent on size and place beyond (b).

### 1.2 Religious items on the entry door, CIV 4706

> **(a)** Except as restricted in Section 1940.5, no governing document shall limit or prohibit the display of one or more religious items on the entry door or entry door frame of the member’s separate interest.
>
> **(b)** If an association is performing maintenance, repair, or replacement of an entry door or door frame that serves a member’s separate interest, the member may be required to remove a religious item during the time the work is being performed. After completion of the association’s work, the member may again display or affix the religious item. The association shall provide individual notice to the member regarding the temporary removal of the religious item.

**Mode: a notice, at most.** **No discretion** except as restricted in Section 1940.5, which is not on the shelf (section 13). The temporary-removal notice in (b) is the association's duty ("shall provide individual notice").

### 1.3 Noncommercial signs, posters, flags, banners, CIV 4710

> **(a)** The governing documents may not prohibit posting or displaying of noncommercial signs, posters, flags, or banners on or in a member’s separate interest, except as required for the protection of public health or safety or if the posting or display would violate a local, state, or federal law.
>
> **(b)** For purposes of this section, a noncommercial sign, poster, flag, or banner may be made of paper, cardboard, cloth, plastic, or fabric, and may be posted or displayed from the yard, window, door, balcony, or outside wall of the separate interest, but may not be made of lights, roofing, siding, paving materials, flora, or balloons, or any other similar building, landscaping, or decorative component, or include the painting of architectural surfaces.
>
> **(c)** An association may prohibit noncommercial signs and posters that are more than nine square feet in size and noncommercial flags or banners that are more than 15 square feet in size.

**Mode: a notice, at most.** **No discretion** over a display within (b) and the (c) sizes, except public health or safety and violation of law; the size limits in (c) are the one limit the section lets the association use ("An association may prohibit ... more than nine square feet ... more than 15 square feet"). A display outside (b) or (c) is not within the section's protection; the documents' own rule applies to it, and the form does not decide that.

### 1.4 Pets, CIV 4715

> **(a)** No governing documents shall prohibit the owner of a separate interest within a common interest development from keeping at least one pet within the common interest development, subject to reasonable rules and regulations of the association. This section may not be construed to affect any other rights provided by law to an owner of a separate interest to keep a pet within the development.
>
> **(b)** For purposes of this section, “pet” means any domesticated bird, cat, dog, aquatic animal kept within an aquarium, or other animal as agreed to between the association and the homeowner.
>
> **(c)** If the association implements a rule or regulation restricting the number of pets an owner may keep, the new rule or regulation shall not apply to prohibit an owner from continuing to keep any pet that the owner currently keeps in the owner’s separate interest if the pet otherwise conforms with the previous rules or regulations relating to pets.
>
> **(d)** For the purposes of this section, “governing documents” shall include, but are not limited to, the conditions, covenants, and restrictions of the common interest development, and the bylaws, rules, and regulations of the association.
>
> (Subdivision (e) says the section became operative on January 1, 2001, and applies to "governing documents entered into, amended, or otherwise modified on or after that date.")

**Mode: a notice** for a domesticated bird, cat, dog, or an aquarium animal (at least one pet, subject to reasonable rules; a registration, where the rules require one, is the rules' and the form shows only what the profile's rules say). **A request for an agreement** for "other animal as agreed to between the association and the homeowner": the association's agreement is part of the definition, so the decision is the association's. **No discretion** to prohibit the one pet; discretion only through "reasonable rules and regulations" (a rule on subject 4355(a)(1) or (a)(2) takes general notice before it is adopted, 4360(a)).

### 1.5 Roofs, CIV 4720

> **(a)** No association may require a homeowner to install or repair a roof in a manner that is in violation of Section 13132.7 of the Health and Safety Code.
>
> **(b)** Governing documents of a common interest development located within a very high fire severity zone, as designated by the Director of Forestry and Fire Protection pursuant to Article 9 (commencing with Section 4201) of Chapter 1 of Part 2 of Division 4 of the Public Resources Code or by a local agency pursuant to Chapter 6.8 (commencing with Section 51175) of Part 1 of Division 1 of Title 5 of the Government Code, shall allow for at least one type of fire retardant roof covering material that meets the requirements of Section 13132.7 of the Health and Safety Code.

**Mode: an application**, where the documents require approval for roof work (4765 applies). **No discretion** to require a roof that violates Health and Safety Code 13132.7 (not on the shelf; the form cites it and does not state its terms). In a very high fire severity zone the documents must allow at least one compliant material.

### 1.6 Video and television antennas, CIV 4725

> **(a)** Any covenant, condition, or restriction contained in any deed, contract, security instrument, or other instrument affecting the transfer or sale of, or any interest in, a common interest development that effectively prohibits or restricts the installation or use of a video or television antenna, including a satellite dish, or that effectively prohibits or restricts the attachment of that antenna to a structure within that development where the antenna is not visible from any street or common area, except as otherwise prohibited or restricted by law, is void and unenforceable as to its application to the installation or use of a video or television antenna that has a diameter or diagonal measurement of 36 inches or less.
>
> **(b)** This section shall not apply to any covenant, condition, or restriction, as described in subdivision (a), that imposes reasonable restrictions on the installation or use of a video or television antenna, including a satellite dish, that has a diameter or diagonal measurement of 36 inches or less. For purposes of this section, “reasonable restrictions” means those restrictions that do not significantly increase the cost of the video or television antenna system, including all related equipment, or significantly decrease its efficiency or performance and include all of the following:
> **(1)** Requirements for application and notice to the association prior to the installation.
> **(2)** Requirement of a member to obtain the approval of the association for the installation of a video or television antenna that has a diameter or diagonal measurement of 36 inches or less on a separate interest owned by another.
> **(3)** Provision for the maintenance, repair, or replacement of roofs or other building components.
> **(4)** Requirements for installers of a video or television antenna to indemnify or reimburse the association or its members for loss or damage caused by the installation, maintenance, or use of a video or television antenna that has a diameter or diagonal measurement of 36 inches or less.
>
> **(c)** Whenever approval is required for the installation or use of a video or television antenna, including a satellite dish, the application for approval shall be processed by the appropriate approving entity for the common interest development in the same manner as an application for approval of an architectural modification to the property, and the issuance of a decision on the application shall not be willfully delayed.
>
> **(d)** In any action to enforce compliance with this section, the prevailing party shall be awarded reasonable attorney’s fees.

**Mode: an application.** The section itself lists "requirements for application and notice" as a reasonable restriction (b)(1), so an application is lawful. **Discretion** is confined to reasonable restrictions: those that do not "significantly increase the cost of the video or television antenna system, including all related equipment, or significantly decrease its efficiency or performance". The limit is for an antenna of "36 inches or less"; a larger one is outside the section (federal rules on antennas are outside the shelf).

### 1.7 Low water-using plants, artificial turf, and drought, CIV 4735

> **(a)** Notwithstanding any other law, a provision of the governing documents or architectural or landscaping guidelines or policies shall be void and unenforceable if it does any of the following:
> **(1)** Prohibits, or includes conditions that have the effect of prohibiting, the use of low water-using plants as a group or as a replacement of existing turf.
> **(2)** Prohibits, or includes conditions that have the effect of prohibiting, the use of artificial turf or any other synthetic surface that resembles grass.
> **(3)** Has the effect of prohibiting or restricting compliance with either of the following: **(A)** A water-efficient landscape ordinance adopted or in effect pursuant to subdivision (c) of Section 65595 of the Government Code. **(B)** Any regulation or restriction on the use of water adopted pursuant to Section 353 or 375 of the Water Code.
>
> **(b)** This section shall not prohibit an association from applying landscaping rules established in the governing documents, to the extent the rules fully conform with subdivision (a).
>
> **(c)** Notwithstanding any other provision of this part, except as provided in subdivision (d), an association shall not impose a fine or assessment against an owner of a separate interest for reducing or eliminating the watering of vegetation or lawns during any period for which either of the following have occurred:
> **(1)** The Governor has declared a state of emergency due to drought pursuant to subdivision (b) of Section 8558 of the Government Code.
> **(2)** A local government has declared a local emergency due to drought pursuant to subdivision (c) of Section 8558 of the Government Code.
>
> **(d)** Subdivision (c) shall not apply to an owner of a separate interest that, prior to the imposition of a fine or assessment described in subdivision (c), receives recycled water, as defined in Section 13050 of the Water Code, from a retail water supplier, as defined in Section 13575 of the Water Code, and fails to use that recycled water for landscaping irrigation.
>
> **(e)** An owner of a separate interest upon which water-efficient landscaping measures have been installed in response to a declaration of a state of emergency described in subdivision (c) shall not be required to reverse or remove the water-efficient landscaping measures upon the conclusion of the state of emergency.

**Mode:** (a), (b): **an application**, where the documents require approval for a landscape change (it is a physical change, 4765(a)); the approval requirement is not itself what (a) voids, the provision that "prohibits, or includes conditions that have the effect of prohibiting" the use is. (c), (e): **a notice**; there is no approval to give. **Discretion:** none to prohibit low water-using plants as a group or as a replacement for turf, or artificial turf; the association may apply landscaping rules that "fully conform" with (a).

### 1.8 Pressure washing, CIV 4736

> **(a)** A provision of the governing documents shall be void and unenforceable if it requires pressure washing the exterior of a separate interest and any exclusive use common area appurtenant to the separate interest during a state or local government declared drought emergency.
>
> **(b)** For purposes of this section, “pressure washing” means the use of a high-pressure sprayer or hose and potable water to remove loose paint, mold, grime, dust, mud, and dirt from surfaces and objects, including buildings, vehicles, and concrete surfaces.

**Mode: a notice, at most.** **No discretion.** The section states no limit and no exception.

### 1.9 Personal agriculture, CIV 4750

> **(a)** For the purposes of this section, “personal agriculture” has the same definition as in Section 1940.10.
>
> **(b)** Any provision of a governing document, as defined in Section 4150, shall be void and unenforceable if it effectively prohibits or unreasonably restricts the use of a homeowner’s backyard for personal agriculture.
>
> **(c)(1)** This section does not apply to provisions that impose reasonable restrictions on the use of a homeowner’s yard for personal agriculture.
>
> **(c)(2)** For purposes of this section, “reasonable restrictions” are restrictions that do not significantly increase the cost of engaging in personal agriculture or significantly decrease its efficiency.
>
> **(d)** This section applies only to yards that are designated for the exclusive use of the homeowner.
>
> **(e)** This section shall not prohibit a homeowners’ association from applying rules and regulations requiring that dead plant material and weeds, with the exception of straw, mulch, compost, and other organic materials intended to encourage vegetation and retention of moisture in the soil, are regularly cleared from the backyard.

**Mode: an application**, only where the documents require approval for a physical change (a raised bed, a structure, fencing); planting needs none unless the documents say so. **Discretion** is confined to reasonable restrictions (those that do not "significantly increase the cost of engaging in personal agriculture or significantly decrease its efficiency") and to the (e) clearing rules; the section applies "only to yards that are designated for the exclusive use of the homeowner". "Personal agriculture" is defined in Section 1940.10, which is not on the shelf.

### 1.10 Accessory dwelling units, CIV 4751

> **(a)** Any covenant, restriction, or condition contained in any deed, contract, security instrument, or other instrument affecting the transfer or sale of any interest in a planned development, and any provision of a governing document, that either effectively prohibits or unreasonably restricts the construction or use of an accessory dwelling unit or junior accessory dwelling unit on a lot zoned for single-family residential use that meets the requirements of Article 2 (commencing with Section 66314) or Article 3 (commencing with Section 66333) of Chapter 13 of Division 1 of Title 7 of the Government Code, is void and unenforceable.
>
> **(b)** This section does not apply to provisions that impose reasonable restrictions on accessory dwelling units or junior accessory dwelling units. For purposes of this subdivision, “reasonable restrictions” means restrictions that do not unreasonably increase the cost to construct, effectively prohibit the construction of, or extinguish the ability to otherwise construct, an accessory dwelling unit or junior accessory dwelling unit consistent with the provisions of Article 2 (commencing with Section 66314) or Article 3 (commencing with Section 66333) of Chapter 13 of Division 1 of Title 7 of the Government Code.

**Mode: an application**, where the documents require approval for the construction. **Discretion** is confined to reasonable restrictions as (b) defines them. The section reaches "a planned development" and "a lot zoned for single-family residential use": the profile's development type decides whether the choice is shown (section 10). The standards an accessory dwelling unit must meet are the Government Code's, which are not on the shelf. 4741(d) says an accessory dwelling unit "shall not be construed as a separate interest" for the rental provisions.

### 1.11 Rebuilding after a disaster, CIV 4752 and 4766

> **4752(a)** Any covenant, restriction, or condition contained in any deed, contract, security instrument, or other instrument, and any provision of a governing document shall be void and unenforceable to the extent that it prohibits, or includes conditions that have the effect of prohibiting, a substantially similar reconstruction of a residential structure that was destroyed or damaged in a disaster.

The definitions of "disaster" and "substantially similar reconstruction of a residential structure" (4752(c)), and the whole of 4766 with its 30, 45, and 60 calendar-day clocks, are quoted in [architectural-application.md](architectural-application.md#1-authority-and-what-the-law-requires) and are not repeated; this form's rebuild choice is a doorway to that section. **Mode: an application** on the architectural application's rebuild track. **Discretion:** none to prohibit a reconstruction that meets 4752(c)(3); review is on 4766's timelines; once approved the body "shall not subject the applicant to any appeals or additional hearings" (4766(f)(1)).

### 1.12 Clotheslines and drying racks, CIV 4753

> **(a)** For the purposes of this section, “clothesline” includes a cord, rope, or wire from which laundered items may be hung to dry or air. A balcony, railing, awning, or other part of a structure or building shall not qualify as a clothesline.
>
> **(b)** For the purposes of this section, “drying rack” means an apparatus from which laundered items may be hung to dry or air. A balcony, railing, awning, or other part of a structure or building shall not qualify as a drying rack.
>
> **(c)** Any provision of a governing document, as defined in Section 4150, shall be void and unenforceable if it effectively prohibits or unreasonably restricts an owner’s ability to use a clothesline or drying rack in the owner’s backyard.
>
> **(d)(1)** This section does not apply to provisions that impose reasonable restrictions on an owner’s backyard for the use of a clothesline or drying rack.
>
> **(d)(2)** For purposes of this section, “reasonable restrictions” are restrictions that do not significantly increase the cost of using a clothesline or drying rack.
>
> **(d)(3)** This section applies only to backyards that are designated for the exclusive use of the owner.
>
> **(e)** Nothing in this section shall prohibit an association from establishing and enforcing reasonable rules governing clotheslines or drying racks.

**Mode: a notice** for a portable rack or a line that needs nothing built; **an application** only where something must be installed that the documents require approval for. **Discretion** is confined to reasonable restrictions (those that do not "significantly increase the cost of using a clothesline or drying rack") and "reasonable rules"; the section applies "only to backyards that are designated for the exclusive use of the owner".

**What the law requires of this form (the association's plain-words note, not the statute's):**

- It names the use and shows the section's operative words and its limit for that use, before it asks anything.
- It says plainly whether the use needs approval. Where it does not, the form is a notice and says "you do not have to tell us, and nothing here asks permission".
- It never states more discretion than the section leaves, and never less.
- Where the section applies only to an exclusive-use yard or backyard (4750(d), 4753(d)(3)) it asks whether the area is one.
- Where approval is required by the documents and 4765 applies, the application has the same stated maximum times, written decision, and reconsideration as the architectural application.

## 2. Who uses it, and when

- **Who.** An owner of a separate interest, or a person the owner authorizes in writing. The sections speak of "a member", "an owner", or "a homeowner"; a tenant is told to ask the owner (section 12).
- **When.** Before an installation that needs approval; or, for a notice, whenever the owner wants the association to know, and never as a condition of the right.
- **Which use.** The form's first question. A use not on the list goes to the architectural application with a question for the manager ("other protected use").
- **Not this form.** A charging station ([ev-charger.md](ev-charger.md)); a solar energy system ([solar.md](solar.md)); a rental, which the documents and CIV 4739 to 4741 govern and the profile's rental form takes; the marketing of a unit (4730); a reasonable accommodation request (its own form); an ordinary change ([architectural-application.md](architectural-application.md)).

## 6. What the member is told

The panel depends on the mode. The words in braces are slots.

**A notice (a right).**

>

>

>

**An application.**

> **We got it on {RECEIVED}.** We will tell you in writing that we have it within {ACK_DAYS} business days (a proposed target, not a law).
>

We may not prohibit it, or impose conditions that have that effect, where the section says so.
>

Your procedure says we answer within **{MAX_DAYS_APPLICATION} days** of the day we receive your application ({PROCEDURE_CITATION}). {WHOSE_APPLICATION_CLOCK}. The decision is in writing; if it is a disapproval, it says why and how to ask the board to reconsider.

Your procedure says: {SILENCE_RULE}. If it says nothing, write to {BOARD_CONTACT}; the board will put your application on its next agenda and tell you the date.
>

## 11. Handler and procedure

- **Handler.** `@handler("CIV 4720", "CIV 4725", "CIV 4735", "CIV 4750", "CIV 4751", "CIV 4753", role=Role.FORM_RETURN, form="protected-use-application", procedure="improvement-request", channels=(PAYHOA, GMAIL, MAIL, FORMS))` for the application uses, and the same form is registered for the notice and agreement uses (`CIV 4705`, `4706`, `4710`, `4715`, `4736`, and 4735(c) and (e)) with the same procedure, since one returned form may be any of them; `accepts` reads the `protected_use` answer, the form's printed lines, or the cited section. A rebuild is handed to `@handler("CIV 4766", role=Role.REQUEST)` of the architectural application.
- **Procedure: `improvement-request`** (to be added, as set out in [architectural-application.md](architectural-application.md#11-handler-and-procedure)). The step this form adds: **"Read the mode."** (1) An application: run the ordinary or rebuild track. (2) A notice: record it, acknowledge it (a draft a person sends), and open no clock; if its words fall outside the section (a sign over nine square feet, an item made of lights) say so in the acknowledgment and open nothing. (3) A request for an agreement: put it on the next agenda. (4) **A notice that the documents appear to bar** is a lead for the board and counsel, never a violation notice: `jason conflicts --leads` lists it, the board records a `Conflict` row, and until it is resolved the documents' text is not enforced against the use where the section's words apply ("follow what is written, as far as a higher authority allows" and "jason notes a conflict and never resolves one"). The step names its lessons once there are any.
- **`ResponseKind.PROTECTED_USE`** (proposed) with `KindRule` words for each use (flag, religious item, sign, pet, satellite dish, antenna, artificial turf, drought, accessory dwelling unit, clothesline, drying rack) and a `ResponseRule` of `ClockSource.DOCUMENTS` or `POLICY` for applications; a notice kind has no clock, so its rule is `ClockSource.NONE` with only an acknowledgment. The existing `ResponseKind.ARCHITECTURAL` rule "Asking leave to change the unit or the common area is an application on any form" classifies an application that never names the section.
- jason never approves, denies, or assigns anything, and never sends an enforcement notice on a protected use.

## 13. Leads for the board and counsel

Readings are labeled; conflicts are noted and never resolved.

1. **Drift in the inventory.** [../form-templates.md](../form-templates.md) says of this form: "a document's approval requirement is void where it bars the use". The words of the sections are narrower, and differ by use: 4735(a) voids a provision that "Prohibits, or includes conditions that have the effect of prohibiting" the use; 4750 and 4753 void a provision that "effectively prohibits or unreasonably restricts" it; 4725 and 4750 list "requirements for application and notice" (4725(b)(1)) or approval on another's separate interest (4725(b)(2)) as reasonable restrictions; 4735(b) lets the association apply conforming landscaping rules. *jason's reading:* an approval requirement is not itself void; a provision, or a condition of an approval, that has the prohibited effect is. The inventory row should read "a provision or condition that bars the use is void; an approval requirement that does not is still followed". This is the labeled reading; counsel reads it.
2. **Reading the sections to give each effect (3541, 1858; AGENTS.md "Read the law to give it effect").** Harmonize before finding a conflict: an approval requirement for a roof material is not at odds with 4720 if the approval is among compliant materials; a conflict is found only where both cannot be obeyed, and then it is a `Conflict` row for the board and counsel, never resolved by jason.
3. **A notice requirement can itself limit.** The sections that give a right (4705, 4706, 4710, 4736) state no notice or application. A document that requires prior notice for them is a provision that "limit[s]" the display (4705(a): "no governing document shall limit or prohibit"). *jason's reading:* the form's notice is the owner's option, never a condition, and the form says so. Counsel reads whether the documents may ask for notice where the section is silent; 4725(b)(1) is the contrast: it lists notice as a reasonable restriction for an antenna.
4. **Reasonable rules.** 4715(a) leaves "reasonable rules and regulations", and 4753(e) "reasonable rules governing clotheslines or drying racks". A rule on these subjects is an operating rule that must be in writing, within the board's authority, not in conflict with governing law, adopted in good faith, and reasonable (4350), and takes general notice at least 28 days before adoption (4355(a)(1), (a)(2); 4360(a)).
5. **The date limit in 4715(e).** The pet section "shall only apply to governing documents entered into, amended, or otherwise modified on or after" January 1, 2001. A pet rule in a document written and never amended since is outside the section's words; whether such a rule still binds, and whether a later amendment brings it in, is a reading for counsel. The form shows the pet use as a notice either way and does not say which documents it reaches; the profile's pet-rule row records the document's date.
5a. **A pet outside the definition.** 4715(b) makes "other animal as agreed to between the association and the homeowner" a matter of agreement. A request for an assistance animal as a reasonable accommodation is governed by law not on the shelf (fair housing), and it is a different request; the form never asks whether an animal is an assistance animal, and a person reads an accommodation request on its own track.
6. **Where the statute leaves discretion and where it does not.** The summary table states it per use. *Reading, labeled:* 4705, 4706 (but for 1940.5), 4736, and 4735(c) and (e) leave the association no discretion on the use itself; 4710 leaves it only the (a) exceptions and the (c) size limits; 4725, 4735(a), 4750, 4751, 4752, and 4753 leave it only reasonable restrictions or reasonable rules, decided in good faith and not arbitrarily (4765(a)(2), where approval is required); 4715(b) leaves it the agreement. The board applies each section as written and records each use, and does not settle a case by this table.
7. **"Backyard" and "yard".** 4750 and 4753 apply "only to" yards or backyards "designated for the exclusive use" of the owner. Whether a front patio, a balcony, or an enclosed yard is a "backyard" is not defined on the shelf. Two readings remain; the board asks counsel. 4750(a) points to Section 1940.10 for "personal agriculture"; it is not on the shelf.
8. **4751 and the kind of development.** 4751(a) speaks of "a planned development" and a "lot zoned for single-family residential use". Whether it reaches a condominium is a question on the text; the profile's `{DEVELOPMENT_TYPE}` decides whether the form offers it, and counsel reads the rest. The Government Code articles it cites are not on the shelf.
9. **4752 and the form.** The rebuild is the architectural application's section. A rebuild may be filed on this form by mistake or by choice; the handler hands it to the rebuild track and runs the 4766 clocks on the received day (the course lawful under either reading, as the architectural application's leads explain).
10. **Fee.** The sections mention no application fee. An architectural fee in the documents may or may not be a "reasonable restriction" (for an antenna: one that does not "significantly increase the cost of the video or television antenna system"); the form carries a fee only where the documents set one and the profile says it may be charged here; counsel reads it.
11. **Attorney's fees and liability.** 4705(c) ("the prevailing party shall be awarded reasonable attorney’s fees and costs"), 4725(d) ("the prevailing party shall be awarded reasonable attorney’s fees"), and 4752(b) ("A court shall award reasonable attorney’s fees to the owner of a separate interest ... who prevails") put the association's exposure in the statutes. That is a reason the board applies the sections as written, and a reason this form never lets a right be treated as a request.
12. **Sections in the article not on this form, and why.** 4730 (restrictions on marketing a unit; not an owner's application); 4739, 4740, 4741 (rental; the documents and the profile's rental form); 4745, 4745.1, 714, 714.1, 4746 (their own pages); 4747 (floor area ratio for an eligible housing development project; not an owner's request). The list in 4700(a) to (f) points to sections that limit the association's authority and are not on the shelf.
13. **Conflict notes (noted, not resolved).** Where a profile's documents bar a use that a section protects, or require approval where the section gives a right without one, `jason conflicts --leads` finds the change in the Act since the document was written, and the board records a `Conflict` row with counsel. Until then the provision is applied as the sections allow; jason does not enforce the part that yields (4205(a)).
14. **Statutes this page points at and the shelf does not hold:** Civil Code 712 and 713 (signs), 714.5, 782, 782.5, 801.5, 1940.5 (religious items; the exception in 4706(a)), 1940.10 (personal agriculture); Government Code 12927, 12956.1, 8558, 8625, 8630, 65595, 65913.11, and the ADU articles (66314 and following, 66333 and following); Health and Safety Code 13132.7 (roofs) and 1597.40; Water Code 353, 375, 13050, 13575; Public Resources Code 4201 and following; and the federal rules on antennas. They are pointers, not recitals; a form that states their terms from memory is refused.
15. **Open board decisions that remain** ([../improvement-requests.md](../improvement-requests.md#open-decisions)): which changes the documents require approval for (the uses this form offers as applications follow from it); the maximum times; and whether the association offers the notice at all (it may offer a form for rights, labeled not-a-condition, or only an acknowledgment of an email).

## 14. Test fixtures

Made-up answers; the unit is "123 Main St"; the owner is "A. Owner".

**Typical** (an application for a satellite dish): `protected_use` a television antenna or satellite dish (36 inches or less); `unit` 123 Main St; `owner_name` A. Owner; `submitted_by` the owner; `decision_delivery` by mail to the unit; `use_details` "A small satellite dish on the rear balcony railing."; `where_is_it` on the outside of my unit; `antenna_size` 36 inches or less; `antenna_property` my own unit; `antenna_visible` no; `plans_attached` photos of the area; signature A. Owner, 2026-10-12. Expected: mode application; the recital shows 4725(a), (b), (c); the 4765 panel with the profile's maximum time; the acknowledgment states the due day with its source word.

**Minimal** (an email, no form, a right): "I plan to fly a flag of the United States from a pole at my unit, 123 Main St. A. Owner." Expected: classified as a notice under 4705; no clock; the acknowledgment quotes 4705(a) and (b) and says no approval is needed; nothing goes to the board.

**Edge** (a right with a display outside the section's words): `protected_use` a sign, poster, flag, or banner (not commercial); `display_where` yard; `display_size` bigger; `use_details` "A 12 square foot political sign in my front yard." Expected: mode notice; the recital shows 4710(a), (b), (c); the acknowledgment says the section lets the association prohibit signs and posters over nine square feet, that the documents' own rule applies to it, and that no violation notice comes from this form; no clock opens; the handler records the notice and a lead for the manager (the documents' sign rule), never an enforcement matter.

**The statutory checklist test, in words.** The generated form passes when, for each use it offers:

1. The use's tokens (section 5) resolve against the shelf and print the operative words and the limit exactly; a subdivision that has moved fails the build.
2. The mode is stated and matches the summary table: a rights use (4705, 4706, 4710, 4715 for a listed animal, 4735(c) and (e), 4736) shows no question that asks permission, no clock, and "you do not need our permission"; an application use shows the 4765 panel (a stated maximum time from the profile's row, written decision, reasons, reconsideration at an open meeting); the pet that is not a listed animal shows the request for an agreement; a rebuild shows the 4766 clocks and hands off to the architectural application.
3. Where a section has a "reasonable restrictions" or "reasonable rules" limit (4710(c), 4715(a), 4725(b), 4735(b), 4750(c), 4751(b), 4753(d), (e)), the limit is quoted exactly beside the use; no use states more or less discretion than its row in the summary table.
4. 4750 and 4753 ask `yard_exclusive`; 4725 asks `antenna_size`; 4710 asks `display_size`; 4751 is offered only where `{DEVELOPMENT_TYPE}` allows.
5. No question asks about a person's religion, health, tenancy, income, or a unit's occupancy; no email address is required unless email is chosen; the signature is optional for a notice.
6. Every question has a `why`; the form says a request in other words is still a request and a notice is never a condition.
7. A notice that the documents appear to bar produces a lead, not a violation notice (an end-to-end test of the handler).
8. The marker `PX` round-trips; the scan test reads the paper form after a bad scan and reads the chosen use from its box; it does not confuse the form with `AP`, `EV`, `PV`, or `PR` (the disputed-charge form).
9. The generated form names its handler and procedure; one with no registered handler is refused.
