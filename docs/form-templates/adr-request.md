# Request for Resolution (alternative dispute resolution)

Status: design (2026-10-05). Key `adr-request`; proposed marker code `RV` (request for resolution). It follows the standard in [form-templates.md](../form-templates.md). The inventory row is “Alternative dispute resolution” ([standard-forms.md](../standard-forms.md)); the notice catalog row is `request-for-resolution` (CIV 5935); the request kind is `ResponseKind.ADR`. This is the most exact form in the Act: 5935(a) says what a Request for Resolution “shall include”, and the checklist in section 3 follows it item by item. The step before it is [idr-request.md](idr-request.md); a payment-related dispute may start at [disputed-charge.md](disputed-charge.md).

The template has two directions. **Member to association** is the form a member fills. **Association to member** is a letter the association sends, generated from the same base, and it carries one more item the statute requires (the copy of the article). Both have a stage 2, the response.

## 1. Authority and what the law requires

As of: the shelf’s 2025 session publication of the code (`data/authorities/CIV/CIV-5925-5965.md`, read with lawlibrary, 2026-10-05). None of these sections was amended in 2025 (all were added by Stats. 2012, Ch. 180). jason’s copy is not an official restatement. The whole article is on the shelf and is quoted here in full, since the request must carry “a copy of this article” when it is served on a member.

**CIV 5925** (the terms):

> As used in this article:
>
> (a) “Alternative dispute resolution” means mediation, arbitration, conciliation, or other nonjudicial procedure that involves a neutral party in the decisionmaking process. The form of alternative dispute resolution chosen pursuant to this article may be binding or nonbinding, with the voluntary consent of the parties.
>
> (b) “Enforcement action” means a civil action or proceeding, other than a cross-complaint, for any of the following purposes:
>
> (1) Enforcement of this act.
>
> (2) Enforcement of the Nonprofit Mutual Benefit Corporation Law (Part 3 (commencing with Section 7110) of Division 2 of Title 1 of the Corporations Code).
>
> (3) Enforcement of the governing documents.

**CIV 5930** (when it is a prerequisite):

> (a) An association or a member may not file an enforcement action in the superior court unless the parties have endeavored to submit their dispute to alternative dispute resolution pursuant to this article.
>
> (b) This section applies only to an enforcement action that is solely for declaratory, injunctive, or writ relief, or for that relief in conjunction with a claim for monetary damages not in excess of the jurisdictional limits stated in Sections 116.220 and 116.221 of the Code of Civil Procedure.
>
> (c) This section does not apply to a small claims action.
>
> (d) Except as otherwise provided by law, this section does not apply to an assessment dispute.

**CIV 5935** (the Request for Resolution, in full: its required content, its service, its response period):

> (a) Any party to a dispute may initiate the process required by Section 5930 by serving on all other parties to the dispute a Request for Resolution. The Request for Resolution shall include all of the following:
>
> (1) A brief description of the dispute between the parties.
>
> (2) A request for alternative dispute resolution.
>
> (3) A notice that the party receiving the Request for Resolution is required to respond within 30 days of receipt or the request will be deemed rejected.
>
> (4) If the party on whom the request is served is the member, a copy of this article.
>
> (b) Service of the Request for Resolution shall be by personal delivery, first-class mail, express mail, facsimile transmission, or other means reasonably calculated to provide the party on whom the request is served actual notice of the request.
>
> (c) A party on whom a Request for Resolution is served has 30 days following service to accept or reject the request. If a party does not accept the request within that period, the request is deemed rejected by the party.

**CIV 5940** (after acceptance):

> (a) If the party on whom a Request for Resolution is served accepts the request, the parties shall complete the alternative dispute resolution within 90 days after the party initiating the request receives the acceptance, unless this period is extended by written stipulation signed by both parties.
>
> (b) Chapter 2 (commencing with Section 1115) of Division 9 of the Evidence Code applies to any form of alternative dispute resolution initiated by a Request for Resolution under this article, other than arbitration.
>
> (c) The costs of the alternative dispute resolution shall be borne by the parties.

**CIV 5945** (the time limits):

> If a Request for Resolution is served before the end of the applicable time limitation for commencing an enforcement action, the time limitation is tolled during the following periods:
>
> (a) The period provided in Section 5935 for response to a Request for Resolution.
>
> (b) If the Request for Resolution is accepted, the period provided by Section 5940 for completion of alternative dispute resolution, including any extension of time stipulated to by the parties pursuant to Section 5940.

**CIV 5950** (the certificate when an action is filed):

> (a) At the time of commencement of an enforcement action, the party commencing the action shall file with the initial pleading a certificate stating that one or more of the following conditions are satisfied:
>
> (1) Alternative dispute resolution has been completed in compliance with this article.
>
> (2) One of the other parties to the dispute did not accept the terms offered for alternative dispute resolution.
>
> (3) Preliminary or temporary injunctive relief is necessary.
>
> (b) Failure to file a certificate pursuant to subdivision (a) is grounds for a demurrer or a motion to strike unless the court finds that dismissal of the action for failure to comply with this article would result in substantial prejudice to one of the parties.

**CIV 5955** (referral after an action starts):

> (a) After an enforcement action is commenced, on written stipulation of the parties, the matter may be referred to alternative dispute resolution. The referred action is stayed. During the stay, the action is not subject to the rules implementing subdivision (c) of Section 68603 of the Government Code.
>
> (b) The costs of the alternative dispute resolution shall be borne by the parties.

**CIV 5960** (what a refusal may cost):

> In an enforcement action in which attorney’s fees and costs may be awarded, the court, in determining the amount of the award, may consider whether a party’s refusal to participate in alternative dispute resolution before commencement of the action was reasonable.

**CIV 5965** (the annual summary, in full):

> (a) An association shall annually provide its members a summary of the provisions of this article that specifically references this article. The summary shall include the following language:
>
> “Failure of a member of the association to comply with the alternative dispute resolution requirements of Section 5930 of the Civil Code may result in the loss of the member’s right to sue the association or another member of the association regarding enforcement of the governing documents or the applicable law.”
>
> (b) The summary shall be included in the annual policy statement prepared pursuant to Section 5310.

**CIV 5310(a)(9)** (the policy statement’s list): “(9) A summary of dispute resolution procedures, pursuant to Sections 5920 and 5965.”

**CIV 5660(f)** (the pre-lien notice states the right to ADR): “The right to request alternative dispute resolution with a neutral third party pursuant to Article 3 (commencing with Section 5925) of Chapter 10 before the association may initiate foreclosure against the owner’s separate interest, except that binding arbitration shall not be available if the association intends to initiate a judicial foreclosure.”

Not on the shelf (listed in the report): CCP 116.220 and 116.221 (the small-claims limits that 5930(b) and 5658(a) point to), Evidence Code 1115 and the following (5940(b)), Government Code 68603(c) (5955(a)). The form states no dollar limit.

**What the law does not say** (leads in section 13): the form of the response (5935(c) says only “accept or reject”); whether a response must be in writing; whether “receipt” (5935(a)(3)) and “service” (5935(c)) are the same day; what “endeavored” in 5930(a) requires; whether a request that leaves out a required item is a Request for Resolution; who decides for the association; the allocation of costs between the parties beyond “borne by the parties”.

## 2. Who uses it, and when

- **A member** who has a dispute with the association (or with another member) that could end in an enforcement action in the superior court for an injunction, a declaration, or a writ, with or without money damages within the small-claims limits (5930(b)). The statute’s “any party” may begin it (5935(a)); the member serves the Request on the other party.
- **The association**, when it wants to enforce the governing documents or the Act against a member in superior court. This is the association’s letter, with the article attached (5935(a)(4)).
- When: **before** the enforcement action is filed (5930(a)); it is not required for a small-claims action (5930(c)) or, except as the law provides, an assessment dispute (5930(d)). Tolling starts when the request is served (5945).
- The form is offered in the annual policy statement, in the summary 5965 requires, and in any letter that tells a member the association may go to court.
- Not served: a request to meet and confer ([idr-request.md](idr-request.md), which the association “shall not refuse” and which does not have the 30-day deemed rejection); a records request; an owner paying under protest ([disputed-charge.md](disputed-charge.md)); a demand letter from counsel (a legal notice for the board and counsel, which may itself be a Request for Resolution: the handler reads it against section 3).

The statute prescribes no **form of the response**. The stage 2 above is the association’s design so that an acceptance and a rejection are both unmistakable and dated. A response in a letter or an email is still a response (the handler reads it); only silence is a rejection, by the statute’s own words (5935(c)).

Not asked, on purpose: the amount at stake (5930(b) uses jurisdictional limits that are not on the shelf, and the association reads scope, not the member); the facts in detail (a “brief description” is what 5935(a)(1) asks); the member’s phone or email unless chosen; why the member wants ADR.

## 6. What the member is told

> This is a Request for Resolution under Civil Code 5935. The party who receives it is required to respond within 30 days of receipt or the request will be deemed rejected. A party that does not accept the request in 30 days has rejected it. If the request is accepted, the parties are to complete alternative dispute resolution within 90 days of the day the acceptance is received, unless both sign an extension; the parties pay the costs of it (Civil Code 5940(c)). The association charges no fee to receive or answer this request (the association’s proposed policy, not a rule of the Act). If it is not accepted, the party who asked may file an action with a certificate saying so; a court that decides fees may consider whether a refusal to take part was reasonable (Civil Code 5960). Time limits for starting an action are tolled while the request is pending (Civil Code 5945). Alternative dispute resolution is required before certain actions in superior court and is not required for a small claims action or, except as the law provides, for an assessment dispute (Civil Code 5930). {DECIDER} answers for the association. If you do not get an answer, the request is rejected after 30 days; write to {BOARD_CONTACT} if you want it looked at again.

Reading (the association’s plain-words note; the statutory words are above): the last sentence restates 5935(c).

## 7. The association's clocks

“Counted from” is the day the Request is served on, or received by, the association. The two words differ in 5935(a)(3) (“receipt”) and 5935(c) (“service”); the association counts from the earlier of the receipt and the date the requester shows (lead 1).

| # | Counted from | Number and kind of day | Who sets it | What follows if it passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | receipt | acknowledge within 1 business day; the window is short | PROPOSED POLICY | none in the law |
| 2 | receipt | counsel and the board’s officer read the request within 10 calendar days; an urgent request (`urgent`) the same day | PROPOSED POLICY | the board has less than 20 days to decide |
| 3 | service or receipt | accept or reject within 30 days (“within 30 days of receipt”, 5935(a)(3); “30 days following service”, 5935(c)) | statute: 5935(a)(3), (c) | “the request is deemed rejected by the party” (5935(c)); the requester may file an action with the certificate that “One of the other parties to the dispute did not accept the terms offered” (5950(a)(2)); a court deciding fees may consider whether the refusal “was reasonable” (5960) |
| 4 | receipt | the board decides by day 25 and the written response goes by day 28 (a margin for delivery) | PROPOSED POLICY | day 30 passes in silence and the request is deemed rejected, unreasonably, if the board never met |
| 5 | the acceptance received by the initiating party | complete the ADR within 90 days, “unless this period is extended by written stipulation signed by both parties” | statute: 5940(a) | the ADR was not completed in compliance; what the requester may then do is for counsel (lead 6) |

## 9. Channels and the reference

- **Channels:** paper, fillable PDF, email, PayHOA, portal. The statute allows service by “personal delivery, first-class mail, express mail, facsimile transmission, or other means reasonably calculated to provide the party on whom the request is served actual notice” (5935(b)); the form lists these and offers email, PayHOA, and the portal as other means. A request on any other channel is still a Request. The association’s own letters go by an approved Mailroom letter (`jason mailroom`; a person sends it) or by personal delivery, with the article attached.
- **Marker code proposed: `RV`** (request for resolution). No collision found. The member-to-association blank is a campaign marker (`RV26M-…`); stage 2 and every association-to-member letter are copies pre-filled for one dispute, so they carry a copy marker, which lets the response be matched to the request it answers.
- The reference is a hint; the form is recognized by its heading “REQUEST FOR RESOLUTION”, its citation `CIV 5935`, and its layout.

## 11. Handler and procedure

- **Handler:** `@handler("CIV 5935", "CIV 5930", role=Role.REQUEST, form="adr-request", procedure="request-for-resolution")`. It accepts an arrival that is this form (`RV`) or is classified `ResponseKind.ADR`. `read` checks it against the checklist (rows 1 to 3 of section 3; a missing item is flagged, not rejected). `plan` opens clocks 3 to 7, drafts the acknowledgment, and marks the request urgent for counsel; it drafts the response stage; and, for an association-initiated request, builds the letter with the article attached. `RESPONSE_RULES` has an `ADR` row (`notice="request-for-resolution"`, first step “Accept or reject in writing within 30 days of service; counsel.”); the note beside it should say “receipt” as well as “service”. jason never accepts or rejects, never contacts counsel, and never files or serves.
- **Procedure:** none exists. Add `request-for-resolution`: (1) stamp the date and open the clocks; (2) check the request against section 3 and note what is missing; (3) route to counsel the same day if `urgent` or if a demand letter; (4) put it on the board’s agenda (executive session where 4935(a) allows, with the minutes’ general note, 4935(e)); (5) the board decides; (6) the written response signed by a person the board authorizes; (7) if accepted, track the 90 days and any stipulation; (8) record the outcome. A separate step belongs in the annual policy statement’s procedure: the 5965(a) sentence and the 5920 description.
- **jason never** decides the response, the kind of ADR, or who pays.

## 12. Edge cases

- **Co-owners.** Each owner is a member; any one may serve the request, and the others are named as parties if the dispute is theirs too. A response to one is a response to the dispute as that party states it.
- **A representative.** A person who serves for a member needs the member’s writing; the association serves the representative whom the member names. An attorney who serves a request for a member is the member’s representative for it.
- **A tenant or non-owner.** Not a party within 5930’s “association or a member”; answered as an inquiry.
- **A minor.** Not asked; counsel.
- **Language and accessibility.** The statutory words are English; the notice in row 3 and the article are attached in English; a plain-words note may be translated. Another format, large print, and help in completing the form are offered on it.
- **A request that arrives without the form.** A letter or an email that describes a dispute and asks for mediation or arbitration is probably a Request for Resolution. The handler reads it against section 3: it flags a missing notice (5935(a)(3)), which does not change the association’s duty to answer in 30 days; the association should answer anyway and say so. A letter that does not ask for ADR is not one.
- **A request that is really something else.** A demand for payment is a collection matter; a request to meet and confer is [idr-request.md](idr-request.md) (the association shall not refuse it; the 30-day deemed rejection does not apply to it); a small-claims threat is outside 5930(c). A letter from a lawyer that threatens suit and proposes mediation is both a legal notice for the board and a possible Request for Resolution; the handler reads the 30 days from the day of receipt and sends it to counsel at once.
- **An assessment dispute.** 5930(d) says the section “does not apply to an assessment dispute” “Except as otherwise provided by law”; 5660(f) and the foreclosure sections (5705(b), on the shelf, 5700-5740) provide ADR before foreclosure. The handler marks an assessment dispute for counsel.
- **A dispute between two members.** The association is not a party; the form can be used between the members, who serve each other. The association keeps a copy only if asked, and does not decide.
- **The association’s own enforcement action.** The association serves the Request on a member, with the article; the member’s reply is stage 2.

## 13. Leads for the board and counsel

Drift: none found. The notice catalog row `request-for-resolution` counts the 30 days from service (`Anchor.SERVICE`) and the notice’s words from receipt (“required to respond within 30 days of receipt”); both words are in the statute (5935(c) and (a)(3)). The checklist in `standard-forms.md` (“the article’s request-and-response procedure”) is consistent with the shelf.

Leads (open questions; none settled here):

1. **Receipt or service.** 5935(a)(3) says “within 30 days of receipt”; 5935(c) says “30 days following service”. Reading (the association’s, proposed): count from the earlier of the association’s receipt and the date of service the requester shows. Counsel confirms. A later date is not used.
2. **A request that omits a required item.** Whether a request without the 5935(a)(3) notice is a Request for Resolution, and so whether silence is a deemed rejection, is for counsel. The association answers every such request in 30 days, so the question does not arise.
3. **Who decides.** The board accepts or rejects. Whether that decision may be made by an officer or the manager under a standing authority, and whether it is made in executive session as litigation (4935(a): “to consider litigation”), is for counsel and the board. A decision in executive session is generally noted in the next open minutes (4935(e)).
4. **“Endeavored to submit”.** 5930(a) requires only that the parties “have endeavored to submit their dispute”. What a good-faith attempt is, and whether one letter is enough, is open; the association keeps its record of each step.
5. **Costs.** 5940(c) says the costs “shall be borne by the parties”. The allocation is not stated. PROPOSED POLICY (the board adopts): the association offers to share the neutral’s fee equally and says so in its response. Whether the association’s insurer must be told of a request is a question for the insurance review (`insurance_policies`); the policy’s notice-of-claim terms are read there, not here.
6. **If the 90 days pass.** 5940(a) says the parties “shall complete” ADR in 90 days. What the requester may do if it is not completed (a certificate under 5950(a)(1) or (2) or both) is for counsel. The association’s record is the stipulation, signed by both, for any extension.
7. **Scope.** 5930(b) limits the prerequisite to actions “solely for declaratory, injunctive, or writ relief” with damages within the small-claims limits, and 5930(c), (d) exclude a small-claims action and an assessment dispute. The association’s reading of scope is counsel’s; the form states the section and asks the member nothing that would require the amount.
8. **Binding arbitration.** 5925(a): “with the voluntary consent of the parties”. 5660(f) says binding arbitration is not available where the association intends a judicial foreclosure. A form that offers “binding arbitration” is offering what a party may refuse; the handler never accepts a binding procedure without the board’s decision and counsel.
9. **The annual summary.** 5965(a) requires a summary “that specifically references this article” with the sentence in quotation marks. The summary is checked in the annual policy statement’s procedure, with a test that finds the exact sentence.
10. **The small-claims limits.** CCP 116.220 and 116.221 are not on the shelf. The form gives no figure; once the sections are on the shelf, a profile slot `{SMALL_CLAIMS_LIMIT}` can be filled from them.

**Edge** (the association serves a member, and an urgent request arrives the same week):

*Association to member:* the dispute is stated by the association; the letter has the heading, the 30-day notice, the request, and the article’s nine sections attached. Stage 2 is the member’s reply: `response` = “We reject the request”, `response-by` = “C. Owner”, `response-date` = 2026-10-20, signature.

*Member to association, urgent:* `other-parties` = “The association and another member”, `other-party-names` = “B. Neighbor, 456 Elm St”, `urgent` = checked, `date-sent` = 2026-09-30 (received 2026-10-05).

Expected: the association’s letter fails the generation check if the article is not attached in full or lacks the 30-day notice; the urgent request is marked for counsel the same day and its clock counts from 2026-09-30 (lead 1), so `{DUE}` = 2026-10-30; an acceptance by the association that is signed by no one fails `check()` (`response-by` required).

**The statutory checklist test, in words.** (a) The template’s `required_content` has the thirteen rows of section 3; the test fails if a row has no carrier. (b) The rendered member-to-association form contains the words “required to respond within 30 days of receipt or the request will be deemed rejected” exactly as 5935(a)(3) states them, and its heading says “REQUEST FOR RESOLUTION”. (c) A question with key `dispute` exists and is required, and `adr-request` exists and is required, and neither can be removed by a profile. (d) The association-to-member letter contains each of the article’s sections 5925, 5930, 5935, 5940, 5945, 5950, 5955, 5960, and 5965, in order, whose words equal the shelf’s; the generator refuses to build one without it (5935(a)(4)). (e) The generated letter is refused if any of rows 1 to 3 is missing. (f) The annual summary carries the 5965(a) sentence exactly. (g) Clock 3 equals 30 days; clock 5 equals 90 days; both are checked against the section’s words. (h) `email` is required exactly when `contact-method` is email. (i) Every recital token resolves against the shelf and names an existing subdivision, and a changed section fails the build.
