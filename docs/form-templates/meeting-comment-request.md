# Request to be heard at a board meeting, written comment, and request to add an item

Status: design (2026-10-05); built in the library as version 1 (2026-10-05), `src/jason/community/form_library/ca/meeting_comment.py`, marker code `HM`, handler `response-clock` with procedure `respond` (the step this page adds to `board-packet` is not written; the module's docstring lists where it departs from this page). Key `meeting-comment-request`. It follows the standard in [form-templates.md](../form-templates.md). The inventory row is "Request to be heard at a meeting" in [standard-forms.md](../standard-forms.md) (family one: “Speak at a meeting; be heard”, today “the meeting room’s open forum”). One form holds three requests, each printed with its own words of the law; **only the first is a right the Act gives, so the form is, for the second and third, a courtesy form and says so.**

## 1. Authority and what the law requires

As of: the shelf’s 2025 session publication of the code (`data/authorities/CIV/CIV-4900-4955.md`, `CIV-4075-4190.md`, `CIV-4000-4070.md` (4045, 4055), `CIV-4340-4370.md`, read with lawlibrary, 2026-10-05). CIV 4925 and 4930 are as added by Stats. 2012, Ch. 180 (continuing former CIV 1363.05); CIV 4920 as amended by Stats. 2013, Ch. 183; CIV 4090 as amended by Stats. 2023, Ch. 203 (operative 2024-01-01). jason’s copy is kept from the publication; it is not an official restatement, and the official page controls.

**CIV 4925(b), the right to speak, and the board’s time limit:**

> (b) The board shall permit any member to speak at any meeting of the association or the board, except for meetings of the board held in executive session. A reasonable time limit for all members of the association to speak to the board or before a meeting of the association shall be established by the board.

**CIV 4925(a), who may attend:**

> (a) Any member may attend board meetings, except when the board adjourns to, or meets solely in, executive session. As specified in subdivision (b) of Section 4090, a member of the association shall be entitled to attend a teleconference meeting or the portion of a teleconference meeting that is open to members, and that meeting or portion of the meeting shall be audible to the members in a location specified in the notice of the meeting.

**CIV 4930(a), the agenda rule, and the one thing it does not prevent:**

> (a) Except as described in subdivisions (b) to (e), inclusive, the board may not discuss or take action on any item at a nonemergency meeting unless the item was placed on the agenda included in the notice that was distributed pursuant to subdivision (a) of Section 4920. This subdivision does not prohibit a member or resident who is not a director from speaking on issues not on the agenda.

**CIV 4930(b)(1) and (c)(2), what the board may do about what a member says, and the only words about placing an item on a future agenda:**

> (b) Notwithstanding subdivision (a), a director, a managing agent or other agent of the board, or a member of the staff of the board, may do any of the following:
>
> (1) Briefly respond to statements made or questions posed by a person speaking at a meeting as described in subdivision (b) of Section 4925.
>
> (c) Notwithstanding subdivision (a), the board or a director, subject to rules or procedures of the board, may do any of the following:
>
> (2) Request its managing agent or other agents or staff to report back to the board at a subsequent meeting concerning any matter, or take action to direct its managing agent or other agents or staff to place a matter of business on a future agenda.

**CIV 4920(a), (c), and (d), the notice and the agenda:**

> (a) Except as provided in subdivision (b), the association shall give notice of the time and place of a board meeting at least four days before the meeting.
>
> (c) Notice of a board meeting shall be given by general delivery pursuant to Section 4045.
>
> (d) Notice of a board meeting shall contain the agenda for the meeting.

(4920(b): an emergency meeting under 4923 needs no notice; a nonemergency meeting held solely in executive session needs two days’ notice.)

**CIV 4935(a), (b), (c), and (e), executive session, the one place a member has no right to speak:**

> (a) The board may adjourn to, or meet solely in, executive session to consider litigation, matters relating to the formation of contracts with third parties, member discipline, personnel matters, or to meet with a member, upon the member’s request, regarding the member’s payment of assessments, as specified in Section 5665.
>
> (b) The board shall adjourn to, or meet solely in, executive session to discuss member discipline, if requested by the member who is the subject of the discussion. That member shall be entitled to attend the executive session.
>
> (c) The board shall adjourn to, or meet solely in, executive session to discuss a payment plan pursuant to Section 5665.
>
> (e) Any matter discussed in executive session shall be generally noted in the minutes of the immediately following meeting that is open to the entire membership.

**CIV 4090(b), a teleconference meeting and the members who speak** (last sentence):

> Participation by directors in a teleconference meeting constitutes presence at that meeting as long as all directors participating are able to hear one another, as well as members of the association speaking on matters before the board.

**CIV 4950(a), the minutes:**

> (a) The minutes, minutes proposed for adoption that are marked to indicate draft status, or a summary of the minutes, of any board meeting, other than an executive session, shall be available to members within 30 days of the meeting. The minutes, proposed minutes, or summary minutes shall be distributed to any member upon request and upon reimbursement of the association’s costs for making that distribution.

**CIV 4955(a), what a member may do if the article is violated:**

> (a) A member of an association may bring a civil action for declaratory or equitable relief for a violation of this article by the association, including, but not limited to, injunctive relief, restitution, or a combination thereof, within one year of the date the cause of action accrues.

**What the Act does NOT give a member** (stated plainly on the form, section 6):

1. **A right to have an item placed on the agenda.** Nothing in the article on the shelf (4900 to 4955) gives a member that right. 4930(a) bars the board from discussing or acting on an item not on the posted agenda; 4930(c)(2) says the board or a director “may” direct staff to place a matter on a future agenda. A member’s request to add an item is therefore a request to the board’s discretion. **This is a courtesy form.** (Reading, labeled: the absence of a right is read from the text of the article on the shelf. Whether the bylaws, a document, or another statute gives one is for counsel; the Corporations Code’s provisions on a special meeting called by members are not on the shelf, and are a pointer only.)
2. **A right to speak in executive session** (4925(b)).
3. **A right to a decision, an answer, or action on what the member says.** The board may “briefly respond” (4930(b)(1)); it need not.
4. **A right to speak for any particular length.** The board establishes “a reasonable time limit for all members” (4925(b)).
5. **A right to have a written comment read aloud, or to substitute it for speaking.** The Act says nothing of written comments; accepting one is a courtesy.
6. **A right to insist the board discuss an item that is not on the agenda**, even where the member speaks about it (4930(a), (b)).

**What it does give:** a right to attend open meetings (4925(a)); a right to speak at any meeting of the board or of the association, except in executive session, within the board’s reasonable limit (4925(b)); a right to speak on issues not on the agenda (4930(a), last sentence); a notice of the time, place, and agenda at least four days ahead (4920); the minutes within 30 days (4950); and an action for a violation of the article (4955).

## 2. Who uses it, and when

- **A member** (an owner of a separate interest, CIV 4160) who wants to speak at a board meeting, send the board a written comment, or ask the board to consider an item.
- **A resident who is not an owner.** 4925(b) speaks of “any member”; 4930(a) speaks of “a member or resident who is not a director” speaking on issues not on the agenda. The form accepts a resident’s request as a courtesy and does not refuse it (section 12).
- **When:** any time. The form is a convenience for planning, and **it is never a condition of speaking**: a member may stand and speak at the open forum without it ([form-templates.md](../form-templates.md), “What a form is not”).
- **Who it is not for:** a member asking to discuss their own discipline or payment plan (4935(b), (c); 5665: the hearing and the payment-plan forms); a member asking to meet and confer (5900 to 5920); a member who wants records ([records-request.md](records-request.md)); a member who wants individual delivery of meeting notices ([individual-delivery-request.md](individual-delivery-request.md)).

Kinds: text, choice, checkbox, paragraph, date. One question holds one value, except `purpose`, a checkbox of the three things the form is for. The form needs no signature: a request to be heard, or to comment, asks nothing that binds the member; a signed-in answer or a name and unit is enough.

## 6. What the member is told

The sentence the member reads (`member_clock`):

> **You may speak at any open board meeting, without this form.** Nothing in the law lets the board refuse a member who wants to speak at an open meeting; the board sets a reasonable time limit for everyone, which is {TIME_LIMIT}. You may speak on any topic, even one that is not on the agenda. You may not speak in an executive session, which is closed to members. The board may not discuss or act on a topic that is not on the agenda, except as the law allows; a director or the manager may answer briefly or ask the manager to report back. **Asking the board to put an item on a future agenda, and sending a written comment, are courtesies, not rights the law gives:** the law does not require the board to place an item on an agenda, to answer, or to read a written comment aloud. We will tell you in writing the day we received your request and, for an item, whether the board will consider it and when. The notice of each board meeting, with its agenda, goes out at least four days before; you may read it at {AGENDA_NOTICE_PLACE}. The minutes of an open meeting are available within 30 days. If your matter is your own discipline, payment plan, or dispute, there is another route, and {BOARD_CONTACT} will help you take it.

Reading (label: the association’s plain-words note and its proposed policy, not the statute): “Nothing in the law lets the board refuse a member” reads 4925(b), which says the board “shall permit any member to speak”. The sentences on courtesies state section 1’s list.

Who decides: for a request to speak, no one; the person who conducts the meeting orders the speakers within the time limit. For a written comment, the board receives it. For an item, the board (4930(c)(2)); who sets the agenda is a matter for the governing documents (`{AGENDA_SETTER}`).

How to reconsider: there is no decision to reconsider for speaking. For an item the board declines to place, the member may ask again, or speak at the open forum about it, or ask to meet and confer (CIV 5900 to 5920) if it is a dispute. A member who thinks the article was violated may bring the action 4955 allows.

## 7. The association’s clocks

“Counted from” is the association’s receipt, stamped on the day it arrives (mail on the day opened; email and PayHOA on the day sent).

| # | Counted from | Number and kind of day | Who sets it | What follows if it passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | receipt | acknowledge within 2 business days | PROPOSED POLICY (as `acknowledge_days=2`) | nothing in the law; the member is left without a date |
| 2 | the meeting notice | the notice and agenda go out at least 4 days before the meeting (2 days for a nonemergency meeting held solely in executive session; none for an emergency meeting) | statute: 4920(a), (b) | the board may not act on an item (4930(a)); the member may sue (4955) |
| 3 | the day the board packet goes to the directors (`{PACKET_DAY}`) | a written comment received by then goes in the packet; one received later is given to each director at the meeting | PROPOSED POLICY (the Act is silent) | the directors may not have read it |
| 4 | the day the meeting notice goes out | a request to speak received by then is listed in the order received; any member may still ask to speak at the meeting | statute for the right; PROPOSED POLICY for the list | none: a member who did not use the form may still speak |
| 5 | receipt of an item request | the board’s answer (placed on the next agenda; placed on a later agenda; not placed; referred) is sent before the notice of the next meeting whose agenda was not yet set when the request arrived, and, if the agenda was set, before the notice of the one after | PROPOSED POLICY (the Act sets no clock for a courtesy request) | the member is left without an answer |
| 6 | the meeting | the board’s time limit applies to every speaker alike; the limit is printed on the notice or at the meeting | the board (4925(b): “shall be established by the board”); if none exists, PROPOSED POLICY below | the board has not met 4925(b) |
| 7 | the meeting | the minutes of the open session are available within 30 days; a distribution on request on reimbursement | statute: 4950(a) | see 4955 |
| 8 | the board’s agreement | an item the board directs onto a future agenda is placed on the agenda of a meeting the board names, and the member is told the date | the board (4930(c)(2)) | the item was not placed |

**If the board has not set a time limit** (4925(b) says it “shall” be established): PROPOSED POLICY, for the board to adopt in writing: a stated number of minutes for each speaker for each meeting, the same for every member, printed on the notice of every meeting. The number is the board’s.

A “business day” is not defined on the shelf. PROPOSED POLICY: the association states the definition it uses on the form (`{BUSINESS_DAY_DEFINITION}`).

## 8. The acknowledgment text

> Reference {REFERENCE}. We received your request on {RECEIVED}. {ASKED_FOR}. You may speak at the open forum of any board meeting whether or not you use this form; the board’s time limit for each speaker is {TIME_LIMIT}. {DECIDER} will tell you by {DUE} whether the board will consider your item and for which meeting; this is a courtesy, and the law does not require the board to place an item on its agenda. The notice and agenda of the next meeting go out at least four days before it, at {AGENDA_NOTICE_PLACE}. To change or withdraw your request, use the same form or write to {RETURN_BY_EMAIL} or {RETURN_BY_MAIL} and quote the reference.

`{ASKED_FOR}` lists what was ticked: “You asked to speak”; “Your written comment will be given to each director”; “Your item request is with the board”. `{DUE}` is row 5’s day; for a comment or a speaker request alone, the sentence about `{DUE}` is left out.

## 9. Channels and the reference

- **Channels:** paper, fillable PDF, email, PayHOA, portal, and a card at the meeting. The PayHOA form is the preferred channel (a signed-in answer is `SIGNED_IN`); a reply email from the address on file is `MATCHED`; a paper copy is `CLAIMED`. A Google Form is acceptable for this form, because it asks nothing that binds the member and an unsigned-in answer is a request for a courtesy; the response is `CLAIMED` at most.
- **Marker code proposed: `HM`** (heard at a meeting). Letters from the alphabet `0-9 A C E F H K M N P R T V X`; `NP`, `NC`, `NA`, `NV`, `CN`, and `RR` are in use or proposed in sibling pages; `HM` collides with no code found in `docs/` or `mystique/forms.py`. A standing form’s campaign is the year it was generated (`HM26M-…`, `HM26P-…`).
- The reference is a hint, never what the reading depends on ([form-identifiers.md](../form-identifiers.md)).

## 11. Handler and procedure

- **Handler:** `@handler("CIV 4925", "CIV 4930", role=Role.REQUEST, form="meeting-comment-request", procedure="board-packet", channels=(PAYHOA, GMAIL, MAIL))` ([arrivals-design.md](../arrivals-design.md)). `accepts` takes an arrival with this form’s marker (`HM`) or a PayHOA form id. `read` is evidence only. `plan` drafts the acknowledgment (`acknowledgment_draft`), places a speaker request on the speakers’ list for the meeting, a written comment on the packet’s comment list, and an item request on the list of “candidates for the agenda” that the board’s packet shows; it routes `item-personal` = “Yes” to the right form the same day; and it opens the clock of row 5 as a schedule occurrence. It writes nothing outside jason, sends nothing, and never decides whether an item goes on the agenda.
- **Procedure:** `board-packet` exists in `src/jason/community/procedures.py` (“The board packet for a meeting”: a week ahead and the day before). Add a step: **read the meeting-comment requests received since the last packet; list each speaker, put each written comment in the packet, list each item request as a candidate for the agenda for the person who sets it, and prepare the answers of row 5 for a person to send.** The `respond` procedure covers the clock and the answer, and its steps (“put on the agenda what the board decides”) already assume a person decides.
- **Template changes this needs:** a `required_if` on `topic`, `comment`, and `item-what` (by `purpose`); a form kind with no signature line; a register for the speakers and the comments ([registers.md](../registers.md)).
- **jason never:** decides whether an item is placed on an agenda, sets the order of speakers, limits anyone’s time, or answers for the board (AGENTS.md, Boundaries: it does not approve, deny, or assign requests).

## 12. Edge cases

- **Co-owners.** Each is a member and may speak; one request may name both. The board’s time limit is for each member.
- **A representative.** 4925(b) speaks of “any member”. A person speaking for a member (a spouse, an attorney, a manager) is for the board’s courtesy; the form accepts the request, says so, and does not refuse it.
- **A tenant or another resident who is not an owner.** 4930(a) says the agenda rule “does not prohibit a member or resident who is not a director from speaking on issues not on the agenda”. Reading (labeled): the Act does not require the board to let a non-member speak and does not forbid it; the board’s practice states which. The form accepts the request and says which applies.
- **A member who asks to be heard on their own discipline or payment plan.** 4935(b) and (c) give those matters their own executive-session routes; `item-personal` sends the request to the right form the same day, and the member is told which.
- **A member who asks to speak in an executive session.** Told in writing that the session is closed to members (4925(b)); if the matter is the member’s own discipline or payment plan, see above.
- **A comment that names another member or a resident.** The board reads it; it is not published by the association beyond the meeting record, and the member is told that.
- **An anonymous comment.** Not accepted on this form: a name and unit are asked. A comment with no name is read and not acknowledged.
- **A member who joins by phone or video.** If the meeting offers it (4090(b), 4926), the member is told how, and is heard “as ... members ... speaking on matters before the board”. If not, the member may speak in person or send a written comment.
- **Language and accessibility.** The statutory words stay in English; large print, another format, an interpreter, a person to write the request down, and a reasonable accommodation are offered, and `help` asks. A request written down for the member is returned for confirmation.
- **No form used.** A letter or email that says “I want to speak at the next meeting” or “please put this on the agenda” is a request. The handler reads it into the same fields, says plainly which parts are rights and which are courtesies, and the date received is the day it arrived.
- **The request is really something else.** A complaint about a neighbor goes to the violation reporting route; a request for records is [records-request.md](records-request.md); a dispute is a request to meet and confer; a request to change a rule is a courtesy item request, and a member who wants the rule reversed has the member vote of CIV 4365 (a written request within 30 days after general notice of a rule change, from members owning 5 percent of the separate interests).

## 13. Leads for the board and counsel

A reading is labeled as one and whose it is. A conflict between a document and the statute is noted, never resolved.

1. **The board must establish a time limit.** 4925(b) says one “shall be established by the board”. If none is on file, that is a gap the board closes with a written policy (section 7). Whether the limit is an “operating rule” that needs notice before adoption (CIV 4355(a) lists subjects; meeting procedure is not among them), or a decision on a specific matter, is for counsel. The form prints the limit only once it exists.
2. **A request that must come first.** A rule that conditions speaking on a written request in advance would narrow “shall permit any member to speak”. The form never conditions it. For counsel if the board is asked to adopt a sign-up rule.
3. **No right to an agenda item.** The form says so, and says the board “may” place an item (4930(c)(2)). A board that places every request treats members evenly; a board that places none leaves members without a way to raise a matter except the open forum. A written policy for the board: PROPOSED POLICY, a stated route (an item request is answered before the next agenda is set; the answer is recorded; the same rule applies to every member), without making a right the Act does not create.
4. **Written comments as records.** Whether a written comment is an “association record” a member may inspect or copy (CIV 5200 to 5240) is for counsel. The form tells the member that a comment becomes part of the meeting’s record.
5. **Who sets the agenda.** The governing documents say; the form’s `{AGENDA_SETTER}` is filled from them. Not found on the shelf; the profile fills it.
6. **A teleconference-only meeting (4926, 5450).** The notice must carry “A reminder that a member may request individual delivery of meeting notices, with instructions on how to do so”; the instructions are [individual-delivery-request.md](individual-delivery-request.md). Whether a member who is not in a location the notice names may speak is for the notice’s instructions.
7. **4930(a)’s “member or resident”.** Read above (section 12). Counsel confirms.
8. **Behavior at a meeting.** The Act leaves order to the board. A reasonable rule of decorum is a board matter; the form states none.
9. **The pointer, not a recital.** A member’s right to call a special meeting of members, and the quorum and notice rules for members’ meetings, are in the Corporations Code and the bylaws. The shelf does not hold them; this page does not quote them.
