# Periodic life-safety inspections and what jason tracks

An association's fire and life safety duties come from three places: the state's codes and statutes, the city's or county's adoption and enforcement of them, and the insurer's conditions. This page lists the recurring inspections, tests, and records that a multifamily common interest development meets in California, which of them jason tracks as obligations, and which are open questions. It was researched on October 4, 2026. A quotation is the operative text as read; where only a digest of a source was read, the entry says so, and a person confirms it at the source before relying on it. Neither jason nor this page is counsel.

For the annual backflow test, read [cross-connection-control.md](cross-connection-control.md). For the sprinkler and alarm standards, the obligation rows in the profile cite their sources.

## Found, text on the authorities shelf

These statutes are on disk (`jason cite "HSC 13146.2"`), so their words are the Legislature's own.

- **The fire department's annual inspection (HSC 13146.2(a)).** "Every city, county, or city and county fire department or district providing fire protection services required by Sections 13145 and 13146 to enforce building standards adopted by the State Fire Marshal and other regulations of the State Fire Marshal shall, annually, inspect all structures subject to subdivision (b) of Section 17921, except dwellings, for compliance with building standards and other regulations of the State Fire Marshal." Subdivision (b) lets the department charge the owner a fee sufficient to pay the cost. HSC 17921(b) reaches "all hotels, motels, lodging houses, apartment houses and dwellings, buildings, and structures accessory thereto". **Open:** whether a condominium building is an "apartment house" for this purpose. The sections read here do not define it. The question for the fire authority is whether the parcels hold an operational permit and are on its annual inspection list.
- **Smoke alarms (HSC 13113.7).** Subdivision (b): "'Dwelling units intended for human occupancy,' as used in this section, includes ... condominium, stock cooperative, time-share project, or dwelling unit of a multiple-unit dwelling complex". Subdivision (d)(1): "The owner shall be responsible for testing and maintaining alarms in hotels, motels, lodging houses, apartment complexes, and other multiple-dwelling complexes in which units are neither rented nor leased." The statute sets no annual test. **Open:** whether "the owner" of an owner-occupied unit is the association or the unit's owner. A reading for counsel: an association that does not own the units is not "the owner" of them.
- **Carbon monoxide devices (HSC 17926(a)).** "An owner of a dwelling unit intended for human occupancy shall install a carbon monoxide device ... in each existing dwelling unit having a fossil fuel burning heater or appliance, fireplace, or an attached garage". It is an installation duty and sets no periodic test.

## Found, but read only as a digest

These came through a summary of a code-reader site, not the code's own text. Confirm each at the source.

- **Private fire hydrants and mains (California Fire Code 507.5.3).** As the digest quoted it: "Private fire service mains and water tanks shall be periodically inspected, tested and maintained in accordance with California Code of Regulations, Title 19, Division 1, Chapter 5." The digest's summary of the standard's table: private hydrants inspected annually and flow-tested annually; exposed main piping inspected annually and flow-tested every five years. A property with private hydrants and fire lines is within it. The City's Fire Department has no private-hydrant program page that was found; ask its Fire Prevention office.
- **Portable fire extinguishers (California Fire Code 906).** Extinguishers are required in R-2 occupancies only where listed items apply, unless each dwelling unit holds one rated at least 1A:10-B:C. Monthly inspection is the code's, with annual maintenance from NFPA 10 through Title 19; the annual and longer cycles were not verified.
- **The City's fire inspection program for multifamily.** The City's Fire Code adoption is City Code chapter 15.36, which as the digest reads it has "An operational permit is required for apartments and/or complexes exceeding three units." The City charges an annual inspection fee; the current fee schedule was not verified.
- **The City's false fire alarm ordinance (City Code chapter 8.38).** As the digest quoted it: "A responsible party shall be assessed a false fire alarm fine for the third, and any subsequent, false fire alarm occurring on the same property or premises within any twelve (12) month period." The responsible party is "the owner of record, occupant, or lessee". A request for a hearing is due within 10 days of the notice. The fine amounts are set by Council resolution; the only schedule found is a 2012 flyer.

## Not found

- A general City duty on a property owner to inspect storm drain inlets or file an annual stormwater report. A recorded maintenance covenant for a treatment device, if the parcels have one, would be its own source.
- Pools, spas, and elevators have their own inspection regimes, which apply only to a property that has one.

## Records to keep

- 19 CCR 904.2(c): sprinkler and alarm test records "on the premises ... for a period of five years after the next required test or maintenance" (quoted in the profile's obligation row).
- A fire department correction notice: its own form says to keep it 18 months from the date of completion.
- Each inspection report, repair record, tag number, and portal receipt for the life of the policy terms it evidences (the insurer's protective-safeguard condition makes them the evidence that a system was kept in working order).
