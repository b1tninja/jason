# Fire protection systems

What a California common interest development's fire protection systems are, the law and standards that govern their upkeep, who may do the work, how often, and what the association must receive and keep. This is reference material: where it states the law, it quotes the authorities shelf; every other statement is a reading, labeled as one. The profile's own systems, vendors, and findings are in its notes, not here.

## The systems

| System | What it is | The standard it is built to |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinkler system, multi-family | A wet-pipe system with a riser (a control valve, a check valve, gauges, a waterflow switch, a main drain), branch lines, and heads, often inside the units | NFPA 13R (low-rise residential) or NFPA 13 |
| Sprinkler system, townhouse or one- and two-family | Heads fed from the domestic water service, often without a riser or monitoring | NFPA 13D |
| Fire alarm and monitoring | A fire alarm control panel that watches the sprinkler's waterflow switch and its valve supervisory ("tamper") switches, sounds a bell or horn, and reports to a supervising (central) station through a communicator | NFPA 72 |
| Backflow preventer on the fire service | A double check or reduced-pressure assembly where the fire line leaves the public water main, with control valves (often OS&Y valves with tamper switches) | The water purveyor's cross-connection rules; NFPA 25 for its fire-system tests |
| Fire extinguishers, standpipes, fire department connection | Portable extinguishers in common areas; the connection the fire engine pumps into | NFPA 10 (extinguishers); NFPA 14 (standpipes) |

**A reading:** which kind each building has is a fact of the profile, from the plans or the installer's records, not something to infer. A building's NFPA 13D system and its neighbour's 13R system are kept to different rules.

## The law, layer by layer

1. **Health and Safety Code 13195 to 13199** (on the authorities shelf). The State Fire Marshal adopts the regulations for inspecting, testing, and maintaining automatic fire extinguishing systems. 13196.5(a): "no person shall engage in the business of servicing or testing automatic fire extinguishing systems without a license issued by the State Fire Marshal pursuant to this chapter", with the exceptions in (b) to (d). Under (d), a CSLB specialty contractor (a C-16) needs no separate State Fire Marshal license.
2. **Title 19, Chapter 5** (the State Fire Marshal's regulation; the adopted text is a publication on the shelf). 19 CCR 904(a)(1):
   > "Water-based fire protection systems shall be inspected, tested and maintained in accordance with the frequencies required by NFPA 25 (2011 edition) including Annexes A, B, C, D, and G as amended by the State of California (Published as NFPA 25, 2013 California Edition)."

   That text has been operative since August 28, 2014. A later edition adopted since is not found; check the regulation's history before relying on a frequency.
3. **The California Fire Code** (Title 24, Part 9) requires systems to be maintained, and adopts NFPA 72 for fire alarms. It is not on the shelf (its model code text is not freely published), so its sections are cited and not quoted.
4. **The local fire authority** (the fire department or district that has jurisdiction) may add requirements: how reports reach it, notice before testing, and its own inspections.
5. **The water purveyor** requires an annual test of each backflow assembly by a tester it certifies, under the State Water Board's Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook.
6. **The governing documents** say who maintains what. They may give the association the risers and mains and leave in-unit heads to the owners, or the reverse. The association reads its own declaration for this.
7. **The insurance policy** may make the sprinklers a condition of coverage. ISO's Protective Safeguards endorsement (CP 04 11) lists symbols such as "P-1" (automatic sprinkler system, including related supervisory services) and "P-2" (automatic fire alarm). Under it, fire loss can go unpaid if the insured knew of an impairment and did not tell the insurer, or failed to keep the safeguard in working order. **A reading:** the monitoring of a sprinkler's waterflow and tamper switches is a "related supervisory service", so a failed waterflow alarm can be an impairment the insurer must hear about. The policy's own words govern; read them.

## Who may do the work

| Work | Who | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| Inspections (looking, not testing) | "an employee designated by the building owner or occupant who has developed competence through training and experience"; a business inspecting for a fee needs a license | 19 CCR 904.1(a) and its exception |
| Testing and maintenance of sprinklers | Those licensed under HSC 13196.5: a CSLB C-16 Fire Protection Contractor, or a State Fire Marshal "A" licensed concern; an owner with trained staff may hold an "A" (Type L) license for wet systems it owns | 19 CCR 904.2(a); NFPA 25 4.1.4.2 as amended |
| Repairs to sprinklers | The same licensees. Since 2024 the CSLB reminds C-16 contractors that their journey-level people who install or repair systems in commercial or multi-family buildings need the State Fire Marshal's Fire Sprinkler Fitter Certification (one- and two-family homes are excepted) | NFPA 25 4.1.4.2 as amended; CSLB bulletin 24-02 |
| Fire alarm service, and the sprinkler's alarm and supervisory parts | No State Fire Marshal license. HSC 13196.5(b): "Persons who engage in the business of servicing or testing fire alarm devices, water flow alarm devices, or the supervisory signaling components of automatic fire extinguishing systems shall not be subject to the licensing requirements contained in this chapter." The electrical work is licensed by the CSLB (C-10 Electrical, C-7 Low Voltage) | HSC 13196.5(b); 19 CCR 904.2(a), exception 2; B&P 7026 |
| Backflow test for the water purveyor | A tester on the purveyor's certified list | the purveyor's program |

**A reading:** an association with no employees cannot rely on 904.1(a)'s owner inspections without asking the fire authority whether a director or its manager qualifies; until then, a licensed contractor inspects. A monitoring company tests the alarm side of the sprinkler (the switches, the signal to the station) but does not service the sprinkler system: a failed switch or valve is the sprinkler contractor's to repair.

## How often

The frequencies below are NFPA 25's Table 5.1.1.2 for wet-pipe sprinkler systems as California amended it (the adopted text on the shelf). The standard's own text is not on disk, so the section numbers are the table's.

| Item | Inspect | Test or maintain |
|---|---|---|
| Waterflow alarm devices | quarterly | test annually (vane and pressure-switch types) |
| Valve supervisory (tamper) devices, other supervisory devices | quarterly | per Table 13.1.1.2 |
| Gauges, wet system | quarterly | replace or test every 5 years |
| Spare sprinklers and the wrench | quarterly | |
| Sprinklers, pipe and fittings, hangers and bracing | annually (accessible concealed spaces every 5 years) | |
| Main drain | | per Table 13.1.2 (annually) |
| Obstruction: internal inspection of piping | every 5 years | investigation when found |
| Sprinklers, fast-response | | sample-tested at 20 years, then every 10 |
| Sprinklers, standard | | sample-tested at 50 years, then every 10 |
| Backflow preventer on the fire service | per Table 13.1.1.2 | forward-flow test annually (NFPA 25 13.6) |

The fire alarm is kept to NFPA 72's chapter 14. **A reading, from secondary sources:** waterflow and valve supervisory devices are tested semiannually under NFPA 72, which is why a monitoring company visits twice a year while the sprinkler contractor's NFPA 25 test of the same switch is annual.

## What the association receives and keeps

From the adopted text of 19 CCR 904.1 and 904.2:

- **Records, five years past the next.**
  - 904.2(c), from the shelf: "Records of all testing and maintenance shall be retained on the premises by the building or system owner for a period of five years after the next required test or maintenance."
  - NFPA 25 4.3.5 as California amended it, from the shelf: "Subsequent records shall be retained for a period of 5 years after the next inspection, test, or maintenance of that type required by the standard."
  - 904.1(b) is not on the shelf: the 2014 final text prints 904.1 with subsection (a) only. As Cornell's Legal Information Institute prints it (see Sources): "Records of all inspections shall be retained on the premises by the building or system owner for a period of five years after the next required inspection."
  - `jason inspections` recites the first two from the shelf and says the third is not there.
- **The forms.** NFPA 25 4.3.1.1 as amended: "California State Fire Marshal Automatic Extinguishing Systems (AES) forms shall be used to record all inspection tests and maintenance activities". For a wet system these are AES 2.1 (quarterly and annual), AES 2.2 (five-year), and AES 10 (corrected deficiencies).
- **A report to the owner and the fire authority.** 904.2(j): "It is the responsibility of the contractor, company, or licensee to provide a written report of the test and maintenance results to the building owner and the local fire authority having jurisdiction".
- **An itemized invoice.** 904.2(e): "an itemized invoice showing work performed and parts replaced shall be provided by the licensee to the system owner".
- **An estimate before repairs.** 904.2(k): "An initial written estimate for the cost of repair shall be given to the customer before performing any repairs."
- **A tag only when clean.** 904.2(d): "A tag or label shall be affixed to a system only after all deficiencies have been corrected." **A reading:** a riser with no current tag is a sign that the last service found something not yet corrected, or that no service was done.

## Deficiencies and impairments

- **The owner corrects.** 904.1(c): "The owner or occupant shall promptly correct or repair deficiencies, damaged parts, or impairments found while performing the inspection, test, and maintenance requirements of this standard." 904.2(d): "The building or system owner shall insure immediate correction of any deficiencies noted during the service."
- **Each deficiency needs a record that clears it.** That is the repair invoice, the AES 10, or the next report showing it passed. An open deficiency with no clearing record is an open item, not a finished one. **A reading:** an invoice's subject line ("flow switch replacement") is not the work; its line items are.
- **Tell the insurer** of an impairment the policy's safeguard condition covers, and keep the record that it was told.
- **Tell the occupants** when an inspection needs entry to units. The governing documents' entry clause is the authority, and the notice says when, why, and that the work is the association's contractor's.

## Gaps associations commonly have

**A reading, from this profile's own records and practitioners' guides.** These are what to check first.

- **The wrong vendor.** The monitoring company is taken for the sprinkler contractor, so the NFPA 25 inspections are never ordered.
- **A contract no one scheduled.** A signed inspection agreement becomes a schedule only when someone books the dates; the vendor's scheduling email can sit unanswered.
- **Reports kept nowhere.** Reports arrive as email attachments and are saved wherever the day's task needed them; the fire authority may hold copies the association lacks.
- **Units not entered.** Units are closed on inspection day, and the report lists them; their heads (painted, corroded, missing escutcheons) go uninspected until someone follows up.
- **Some systems overlooked.** NFPA 13D systems are outside NFPA 25 and are often forgotten, though an insurer's safeguard condition may still reach them.
- **Long-period items missed.** The five-year internal inspection, the five-year gauges, and the 20-year sprinkler sample test pass unnoticed.

## Where jason keeps it

- **Systems.** The profile's `life_safety_systems()` lists each system (`jason.community.life_safety.LifeSafetySystem`): its kind, the standard it was installed under with the record that says so, what it serves, and who services and monitors it. A standard no record states is left empty, never inferred.
- **Obligations.** The profile's `obligations` carry each cadence (quarterly, annual, five-year, gauges, sample tests), and `jason deadlines` shows what is overdue.
- **What applies to which system.** Each obligation carries its scope as an `applies` condition (`jason.community.fire_protection`: NFPA 25's sprinkler chapter reaches a sprinkler system except one installed under NFPA 13D; NFPA 72's tests reach a fire alarm). `jason applies` asks each obligation of each system and prints what applies, what does not with the fact that decided it, and what is undetermined with its question ([applicability.md](applicability.md)). `jason applies --questions` lists those questions, one a system and fact, and `jason applies --file-questions` parks them in the intake queue, where a person's answer becomes a fact ([intake.md](intake.md)). The deliverable rules carry theirs too: a water-based system, and the vendor's testing, maintenance, inspection, or repair as each provision says.
- **Which periods have a report on file.** `jason inspections` is the completeness check ([ingestion-and-review.md](ingestion-and-review.md), the Completeness lens). For each system and each obligation that applies to it, it lists the periods by the row's frequency and what is on file in each (`jason.tasks.inspections`):
  - **What counts.** An inspection-report reading placed by its own fields (system, building, inspection date), or a completion a person recorded. A payment opens a count and never covers a period.
  - **The four standings.** Covered, partly on file (one building of several), not on file, or not yet due. "Not on file" never means "not done".
  - **What is not placed.** A reading that lacks a field is listed as unplaced with the field it lacks. A report filed from a system's vendor that no reader has read is listed as not read; `jason models --filed` reads the filed reports, and one it could not read stays listed with why (no words, or words no reader recognized). Where several obligations apply to one system, a reading must say which inspection it records (`interval_months`), or it is listed as not assigned.
  - **What is not expected.** An obligation that does not apply is listed with the fact that decided it, and an undetermined one as a question, as `jason applies` gives them.
  - **Deficiencies.** A report that reports deficiencies shows its count, and names a later report of the same system and building that reads passed with none open; otherwise "no correction on file".
  - **The record-keeping rule.** Each `RecordRule` row (`jason.community.fire_protection.RECORD_RULES`) is recited from the shelf's copy with its citation, or said to be missing from it.
  - **The page.** `--write` saves `data/reports/life-safety-records.md`, a generated summary and not the record.
- **Filing.** The filing rules file each vendor's report by system (`Reports/Fire Protection/<system>`), invoices by fiscal year, and contracts by vendor; see [gmail.md](gmail.md#filing-vendors-attachments).
- **Reading reports.** `jason.community.models.legal_inspections` reads inspection reports (result, deficiencies, devices). A vendor's public report portal is synced by `jason.tasks.report_portals`.
  - **Which inspection a report is.** The reader takes `interval_months` from the report's own words: a labeled field, a State form that names one interval (AES 2.2, the five-year report), or a title line. AES 2.1 is titled "Quarterly and Annual Report" and prints both sections on every copy, so a report on it has no interval unless it says which. That is a miss, and `jason inspections` lists the report as not assigned.
  - **The State forms' marks.** The AES forms record each item as P, F, or N/A. The reader does not read the marks, so a report on them has no result until a person reads it ([document-models/legal.md](document-models/legal.md#inspection-reports)).
- **Board items.** A deficiency or an overdue inspection becomes a board item with its authority and evidence.
