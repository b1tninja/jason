# Community facts as defaults, and a manual for each unit

Status: proposal. Prompted by interior paint questions, and by what an insurance claim needs: a clear line between what came with
the unit, what an owner changed, and who answers for each.

## The idea in one paragraph

The association keeps a **knowledge base of community facts**: what was originally installed (by plan or building), the manuals and
product documents for it, who maintains what, and answers to the questions owners ask. Those facts are **assumed defaults**: "unless
the owner has changed it, your unit has this." Each owner can keep a **unit manual** that overrides the defaults with what they
actually did (new flooring, a replaced water heater, a remodeled bath), with receipts, models, permits, and photos. The unit's
**effective record** is the community default for its plan, overridden by the unit's own entries. A claim, a resale, a maintenance
question, or a paint question reads the effective record.

## Why it matters for claims

A declaration usually draws the insurance line by what was **original**: the association's master policy insures the standard fixtures
and improvements as originally installed and equivalent replacements, and the owner insures upgrades and additions, often only to the
extent they cost more than what they replaced. To apply that line after a loss, someone must know what was original and what was
changed. Today that is reconstructed from memory, listings, and invoices, after the fact, in a dispute. With defaults and a unit
manual, the line is drawn in advance and dated.

The policy can lean on the same record. A master policy often values unit property "per the original plans and specifications," and
pays at a lower "builder's grade" if those plans and specifications cannot be documented. So the community's documented originals
protect every owner's claim, not just the paperwork. Also check that the policy's list of covered unit property matches what the
declaration says the master policy must insure; where it is narrower, the board asks the agent and counsel, and owners' own
policies must fill the gap.

jason does not decide coverage or liability. The carrier decides coverage, and the board, counsel, or a court decides responsibility.
jason assembles a **loss packet**: the unit's effective component list (original and upgraded, each with its source), the governing
words that draw the line (recited whole, with the citation, then any reading labeled as one), the policy's deductible and valuation,
the maintenance responsibility table, and prior incidents at the unit and building. Where the documents are silent or two readings
remain, the packet says so and the board asks counsel.

## What the owner gets

The unit manual is worth keeping for the owner's own sake, before any claim:

- **A repository for the unit** that stays with it: manuals, warranties, receipts, product and model numbers, contractor details,
  permits, and photos, in one place. The next owner inherits a documented home instead of starting from nothing.
- **Proof of what they improved.** Each entry carries the architectural approval (the association's own record of it, attached
  automatically), the permit, the contractor, the date, and the cost the owner paid. Together they show the value the owner added.
  The cost is the owner's figure and the association certifies nothing about value; an approval says the change was allowed, not
  that it is insured, safe, or worth what it cost.
- **A fast answer after a loss** (below), and the inventory their own insurer asks them to produce.

## Triage at a loss: original, replaced, or upgraded

When damage happens, the first question is which layer of insurance the damaged item sits in. For each component the unit's
effective record gives a status, and the packet shows two columns side by side, because they may differ:

| Status | Meaning | Where it points |
|---|---|---|
| **Original** | installed by the developer, per the documented specification | the association's master policy, if the policy's list of covered unit property includes that kind of item |
| **Equivalent replacement** | like for like, per the same plans | treated as original |
| **Upgrade or addition** | better than, or not in, the original specification | the owner's own policy (an HO-6 or equivalent) |
| **Builder option** | chosen from the developer at purchase | ask the agent whether it counts as original; until answered, shown separately |
| **Unknown** | no default and no entry | ask; a person decides |

The two columns are what the **declaration** says the master policy must insure and what the **policy** says it does. Where they
differ (the policy's list can be narrower than the declaration's), the row says so and names the question for the agent. The result
is a triage, not a coverage decision: the carrier decides coverage, the board and counsel decide responsibility, and the deductible
follows the board's written guidelines (or the gap is noted).

### The questions a loss asks, in order

Which layer an item sits in is the first question, not the only one. The packet also records, and asks a person to confirm:

1. **The item**: original, replaced, upgraded, or personal property (above).
2. **The origin of the cause**: an association-maintained source, the owner's own system, a contractor, a tenant or guest.
3. **Whether it is an insured casualty above the deductible**, or a loss the declaration leaves with the owner.
4. **Negligence**, whose, and of what degree, as the governing documents use those words.
5. **The deductible**, under the board's written guidelines or the gap.

The documents decide how these combine. The packet recites the provisions whole with their citations and labels any reading; it
never tells the owner who is at fault. A loss touching an unsettled question (the board has one pending with counsel) says so.

## Two layers

**Community facts** (the association's, published to all owners). One row per fact:

- the statement, and its scope: the whole community, a plan, a building, or a phase
- its source: a document and place (a developer specification, a manual, a declaration section), or "owner-reported"
- the date it was true, and a status: **documented** (a source on file), **reported** (owners said so, with a count), or **assumed**
- the questions it answers, so the same row is an FAQ entry

Kinds: original components by plan (flooring, fixtures, appliances, heating and cooling, water heater, windows, paint), manuals and
warranties for installed products, the maintenance responsibility table (owner, association, shared), the insurance summary members
receive each year, and the association's answers to common questions. Interior paint colors are the same kind of fact: unknown by
default, reported by owners over time, never presented as the association's record.

**The unit manual** (the owner's). One entry per improvement or repair the owner chooses to record:

- what and where, and which original component it replaces or adds to
- date, who did it (contractor and licence), permit and architectural approval numbers
- cost, invoice, product and model, serial, warranty
- photos and documents

The owner controls it and takes it when they sell. It is visible to the association only when the owner submits it or the
association itself generated the item (an architectural approval it granted). What the association holds may be an association
record, with the inspection and retention rules that follow (check the statute before promising privacy or retention to owners).
Nothing is required of an owner; the manual is an offer.

## How the layers combine

For a unit, the effective value of each component is the unit entry if there is one, else the community default for the unit's plan
and building, else "unknown." Each shows its source, so "original, per the developer's specification" is never confused with "owner
replaced in 2023, invoice on file." A default that has never been confirmed is shown as an assumption and the page says the unit may differ.

## Answers become a FAQ

A question owners ask more than once (three or more units in a year, the rule the topic report uses) becomes a fact row with a
quoted source, not a paraphrase. The FAQ page is a view of the facts grouped by topic: each answer states the default, quotes the
operative words and citation, labels any reading, and links to the manual or document. The assistant answers from the same rows, so
an email, the page, and the new-owner packet cannot drift apart.

## What is written down by the board

Where the declaration says the deductible is paid "pursuant to guidelines adopted by the Board" and no guidelines are on file, that is
a policy gap, and the rule applies: jason proposes a written policy, the board adopts it, and each use is recorded. An allocation
policy is far easier to apply when the unit's origin and improvements are already recorded. A policy on a subject in Civil Code
4355(a) needs notice to members before adoption (`jason rule-change`).

## Surfaces

- **Community facts page** (read-only, all owners): the FAQ view, with sources and the document repository (manuals, product sheets,
  warranties, plan sheets), searchable.
- **Unit manual** (owner-facing, signed in as that owner): add an entry from a form or a photo, attach documents, export as a PDF or
  a folder. Needs the owner sign-in model the console is building; until then a shared folder per unit is the fallback.
- **Board and manager view**: coverage of the facts (which plans have documented originals, which are assumptions), the questions most
  asked, and the loss packet for a unit on demand.

## Data jason needs

- The developer's original specification by plan (standard features lists, finish and appliance schedules, plan sheets), from the
  public report, sales materials, the plan set, or the developer directly.
- Manuals, warranties, and model numbers for installed equipment, from the developer's turnover documents and the association's files.
- A map from each unit to its plan (the assessor characteristics are already matched to the developer's plans).
- The declaration's maintenance and insurance sections as structured rows (already outlined) so the responsibility table is recited,
  not summarized.

## Developed further

**Where things live.** One home for each kind, so nothing is copied:

- *Community facts*: a register (a Sheet the board edits, [registers.md](registers.md)) with the fact row above; its sources are
  documents in the library or Drive, referenced by address, never pasted. Facts the declaration states are not retyped: they are
  recited from the outlined document.
- *Documents repository*: the library and Drive, grouped by kind (manuals, warranties, plan sheets, product sheets) and scoped
  (community, building, plan, unit). A document is added once and referenced from every fact that rests on it.
- *Unit manual*: a Drive folder the owner controls, one per unit, with an index (the entries). The console reads it only through what
  the owner shares.

**A fourth status: builder option.** A buyer often chose upgrades from the developer at purchase (a kitchen package, flooring). They
are neither the base specification nor an owner's later upgrade. The policy keys on "original plans and specifications," so whether
an option counts as original is a question for the agent, and the record keeps it as its own status until answered.

**Who sees what.** An owner's entries are the owner's. The community facts are read by every owner. The board and manager see the
facts' coverage (which plans have documented originals) and see a unit's entries only when shared or when a loss packet is opened
for that unit with the owner's knowledge. Entries naming a contractor or cost are never shown in a member list.

**Resale.** The owner exports the unit manual (a PDF or a folder) and gives it to the buyer. The association does not deliver it and
adds nothing to resale disclosures from it; the owner decides what a buyer sees.

**Measures the board can use.** Plans with documented originals out of all plans; facts still only assumed; the questions asked most
and the facts that answer them; units with a manual started; open questions with the agent or counsel. No unit's contents are counted
against it.

**Edges to settle.** A unit changed by the association itself (the association replaced a component) is an entry with source
"association." An improvement approved but never done is an approval without an entry; an entry without an approval where one was
required is shown as a gap to the owner, not to the board. The record never asserts that a change was lawful or safe.

## Phases

1. **Facts and sources.** The fact row, the FAQ view, and the repository of manuals; start with the questions already asked
   (touch-up paint, interior colors, original flooring, water heaters).
2. **The loss packet** from what exists: policy, declaration lines, maintenance table, incident history, the unit's plan.
3. **The unit manual** as a shared folder per owner, then a form.
4. **Owner-reported confirmations** for facts the developer's papers do not give (a count per fact).
5. **The board's deductible and responsibility policy**, proposed from the cases the packet surfaces.

## Open decisions

- Who may add a community fact, and who approves it before it is shown as documented rather than assumed?
- Does the association store unit manuals, or only point to the owner's own folder? Storing makes them association records.
- Should an owner's manual ever be shared with a buyer or escrow by the association, or only by the owner?
- Which questions are answered by default and which always go to a person (anything about fault, coverage, or a dispute)?
