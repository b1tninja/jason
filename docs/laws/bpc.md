# Common interest development managers

Business and Professions Code §§ 11500–11506, and Civil Code §§ 5375–5385, from the 2025 session publication. These duties sit on the manager. The association duties that the manager carries out are in [obligations.md](obligations.md).

## The work

Section 11500 defines management services as acts in an advisory capacity, including:

- Administering or supervising collection, reporting, and archiving of the association’s financial and common-area assets, at the board’s direction
- Carrying out board resolutions and the governing documents
- Administering contracts, including insurance contracts, and the association’s vendors

Section 11501 says an individual may not be required to obtain a real estate license in order to perform those services for an association. That does not displace a license another law requires. A CPA may still provide financial services within that license. Calling oneself a “certified common interest development manager” requires the education and examination in § 11502: at least 30 hours covering the law courses in § 11502(b)(1) and the management skills in § 11502(b)(2), plus an examination. The certification is of a person. Section 11503 says it does not include a management firm. Section 11505 makes it an unfair business practice to use the certified title without meeting § 11502, to claim a government agency licensed that title, or to omit a disclosure § 11504 or Civil Code § 5375 requires.

## What the manager discloses to the board

| When | What | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annually | Whether the manager meets § 11502; the certifying association, date, and status; the primary office | BPC § 11504(a)–(c) |
| Before a contract or renewal | Whether the manager’s or employer’s fidelity insurance covers the year’s operating and reserve funds. The association is not required to demand that insurance | BPC § 11504(d) |
| With that disclosure | Whether the manager holds an active real estate license; the written statement Civil Code § 5375 requires; any referral fee from a third party distributing documents under § 5300; a written acknowledgment that the § 4528 and § 5300 disclosures and documents are the association’s property | BPC § 11504(e)–(h) |
| Before the management agreement, and no later than 90 days before | Names and addresses of owners or general partners; licenses; certifications; ownership interests; referral fees from a document provider | CIV § 5375 |
| When presenting a bid | Any referral fee or ownership interest in a provider the association uses | CIV § 5375.5 |

## Money

If the manager receives association funds that are not in escrow or in an account the association controls, the manager deposits them in a trust account in a federally insured institution in this state and keeps them there until the association gives written instructions (§ 5380). That account is not a real estate broker trust account. Broker handling of other people’s funds in a transaction subject to the Real Estate Law is Business and Professions Code § 10145 and Title 10, sections 2830–2836, of the Commissioner’s regulations.

The manager does not commingle those funds with the manager’s own money. Interest on the account does not go to the manager. The manager keeps a separate record of receipts and disbursements. A transfer out of reserves or operations above the smaller of $5,000 or 5 percent of estimated annual income (for an association of 50 or fewer interests; the section states the other tier) needs prior written board approval.

## Escrow documents

If the management contract makes the manager responsible for the disclosures in Civil Code § 4530, the manager facilitates that delivery (§ 5376).

## Documents the developer provides

Section 11018.6 requires a person offering an interest in a subdivision described in § 11004.5 to make the declaration, the articles, the bylaws, and any other instrument that establishes the owners’ common rights and responsibilities available before an offer is signed, and to give each purchaser a copy before transfer. To the extent available, the offeror also gives the financial information in Civil Code §§ 5300 and 5565 and a governing-body statement of delinquent assessments against that interest.

Section 11018.5 is the finding the commissioner makes before issuing the public report. Completion of the subdivision and offsite improvements is assured. The deeds and leases adequately transfer the interests represented. After the first transfer, the declaration, articles, bylaws, management contracts, and other plan documents last submitted bind later purchasers. The plan has reasonable arrangements for delivering control to the purchasers and for management, maintenance, preservation, operation, use, resale, and control.

Section 11010.2 defines a substantially complete application as one that contains the documents the commissioner’s regulations require. Title 10, sections 2792, 2792.1, 2792.15, and 2792.23, are that list: what the developer files with the Bureau, what common area is conveyed to the association, and what copies are delivered to the governing body. The inventory, and the duty each document lets the association perform, is in [community-manager.md](../community-manager.md).
