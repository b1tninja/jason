# Reserve studies and reserve funds

Source: the 2025 session publication. SB 900 is Stats. 2024, Ch. 288, effective January 1, 2025. AB 2114 is Stats. 2024, Ch. 100, effective July 15, 2024. SB 410 is Stats. 2025, Ch. 516, effective January 1, 2026. These pages are an obligation index for Jason. They are not legal advice. When a task needs the words of a section, read that section.

The association's recorded CC&Rs and bylaws still control what the association must maintain. Section 4205 says the statute controls when it conflicts with the governing documents. A declaration can still assign a utility line to the owner, and section 4775 says so.

## What changed

### Gas, water, and electrical lines (SB 900)

Section 5550(c) now says that, for the reserve study, “major components” includes gas, water, and electrical service to the extent the association is responsible for repair or replacement of those lines under section 4775.

Section 4775(a)(2) makes the association responsible for the repairs and replacements needed to restore interrupted gas, heat, water, or electrical service that begins in the common area, even when the failure runs into a separate interest or its exclusive use common area. That duty yields when the declaration says otherwise, and when a utility provider is the one required to repair the line. It does not shift a utility company’s own duty onto the association.

Heat is in that repair duty. Heat is not in the section 5550(c) list. Sewer and storm drain are not in that list either. They enter the study only when some other duty makes them a major component the association must repair or replace.

When reserves will not cover a section 4775 restoration, section 4775(b) lets the board start the work and finance it:

| Step | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Start | Commence the repair process within 14 days of the interruption. § 4775(b)(1) |
| If reserves are short | Competitive financing under § 5735, without a member vote, and an emergency assessment to repay the loan. The board first adopts a resolution that states the expense and that reserves do not cover it, and distributes that resolution with the assessment notice. § 4775(b)(2) |
| No quorum in the 14 days | At the next duly noticed meeting, the directors present are a quorum, only for the vote to commence that process. The notice says so. § 4775(b)(3) |
| How the directors vote | Electronic vote, including email, is allowed for this vote. The record is an association record under § 5210. § 4775(b)(4) |
| Declared emergency | Section 4775 does not apply when a declared federal, state, or local emergency materially affects the association’s ability to perform. § 4775(e) |

Section 5610(b) is the companion change. An emergency assessment, outside the section 5605 cap, now includes an extraordinary expense to operate, repair, or maintain property the association is responsible for when a threat to personal health or safety, or another hazardous condition, is discovered. The older text was limited to a threat to personal safety.

Using reserves for a line the association must repair is the ordinary section 5510 rule, once that line is a major component the fund was established for. Section 4775(b)(2) is the path when the reserve balance is not enough. It is not a path for a line the declaration assigns to the owner.

### Exterior elevated elements (AB 2114 and SB 410)

Section 5551 is the balcony inspection. It applies to a condominium project, to buildings with three or more attached multifamily dwelling units, and to exterior elevated elements the association must maintain or repair. Those are load-bearing components, with their waterproofing, that extend beyond the exterior walls, have a walking surface more than six feet above the ground, are designed for people to use, and are supported in whole or in substantial part by wood or wood-based products. Decks, balconies, stairways, walkways, and their railings are the examples in the definition.

AB 2114 added licensed civil engineers to the inspectors. A licensed structural engineer, civil engineer, or architect may do the inspection. The chaptered bill amends section 5551 and stops there. It does not amend section 5510.

SB 410, effective January 1, 2026, changes the report and who must receive it:

| Duty | Section |
| --- | --- |
| First page of the report states the inspection date, unit counts, element counts, how many were inspected, how many pose an immediate threat and how many units that affects, and a certification that the sample was statistically significant | § 5551(e)(5) |
| The report is stamped or signed, given to the board, and incorporated into the § 5550 study | § 5551(f) |
| An immediate threat is reported to the association at once and to local code enforcement within 15 days. The association keeps occupants off the element until repairs are inspected and approved | § 5551(g) |
| First inspection was due January 1, 2025, then every nine years, coordinated with the reserve-study inspection. Reports are kept for two inspection cycles | § 5551(i) |
| The reports are association records | § 5200(a)(15) |
| Members may inspect them for that same two-cycle period | § 5210(a)(3) |
| A seller gives a prospective purchaser the report from the most recent inspection | § 4525(a)(11) |

The statute requires the inspection, requires the report inside the reserve study, and requires the association to keep maintaining those elements. Section 5510 still decides whether a particular invoice is paid from the reserve account. The inspection fee is a reserve expenditure when the board has identified that inspection as a component the reserve fund was established for, which is what a line in the study does. The fee is not a reserve expenditure merely because the report is filed with the study.

## Rules that did not change

| Rule | Section |
| --- | --- |
| Reserve accounts are money the board identified for future repair or replacement of, or additions to, major components the association must maintain. Construction-defect settlement funds are a separate item | § 4177 |
| Two signatures to withdraw: two directors, or one director and one officer who is not a director | § 5510(a) |
| Spend reserve funds only on repair, restoration, replacement, or maintenance of those components, or litigation about that work | § 5510(b) |
| Borrowing reserves for operating cash needs notice under § 4920, a written finding in the minutes, and repayment within one year ([reserve-transfers.md](../reserve-transfers.md)) | § 5515 |
| Visual inspection at least every three years when major-component replacement value is at least half the gross budget, excluding reserves. The board reviews the study every year | § 5550(a) |
| The study lists components with remaining life under 30 years, remaining life, cost, the annual contribution, and a funding plan | § 5550(b) |
| The board adopts the funding plan in an open meeting. An assessment increase that funds the plan is a separate action under § 5605 | § 5560 |
| The annual budget report includes the reserve summary and the Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary | §§ 5300, 5565, 5570 |
| A component with more than 30 years of remaining life may be shown as a capital asset or left out of the calculation, if the study and the disclosure summary say which | § 5570 |

There is no dollar minimum in section 5510. A board's own spending policy may set one, such as reserve spending only on expenditures above a threshold, with competitive bids unless the work is an emergency. That is the board's policy, not the statute, and it binds only once the board adopts it; the minutes are where to look.

Such a policy rarely defines an expenditure as one invoice. Nothing in the Civil Code forbids the board from treating related replacements of one component as one expenditure. The practice the bidding rules are aimed at is the reverse: splitting one job into pieces so it stays under a bid threshold. Grouping is the component, not the calendar. Work on another component is not added in to clear a threshold. A withdrawal still needs the two signatures in section 5510(a). If the board follows a spending policy, the vote and the bid note go in the minutes with the component name and the count of what was replaced.
