# PDF splitter: prior art and what to borrow

Research for the splitter in [pdf-splitter.md](pdf-splitter.md) and [console/screens/split.md](console/screens/split.md). The question: how do other products and projects (a) split one large scanned PDF into documents and (b) let a person cull a long sequence of images, and what is worth borrowing. Researched 2026-10-11 from public pages; each claim carries its source. **Unverified** marks a claim a search result implied but a primary page did not confirm, or a figure taken from a snippet. This is a general document: no association facts.

## 0. What the splitter already has (the baseline)

Built: album, filmstrip, scroll and list views; tap a first page; suggestions with reasons in words (rule pass over `document_segments` cues, optional local-model pass, ghost boundaries, accept, edit, reject, bulk accept above a band); review before apply; a magnifier; drawers and a focus layout; browser-side rendering (pdf-splitter.md sections 4, 5.3 to 5.12). Open in the design but not yet built: session-profile rescoring from a person's marks (4.6), the gold set and `scripts/split_fuzz.py` (4.7). Existing cues include `after-blank` (with the `DUPLEX` guard), `page-one`, `numbering-reset`, `size-change`, `title-block` ([document-segmentation.md](document-segmentation.md)).

What is missing, in one line each: no deterministic mark a person can put in the stack; no stacking of runs the person has judged ("these 40 pages are one thing"); no metric that scores a whole segmentation (only per-boundary precision and recall); no calibration on the person's own confirmed splits; no compare view for a doubtful boundary.

## 3. Separator sheets: a printed "start of document" sheet

### 3.1 The technique is old and free

Patch codes and barcode separator sheets are long-standing scanning-industry practice (the Kofax profiles above; Paperless-ngx PATCHT). A separator sheet is plain printing; a QR or Code 128 symbol is an open standard. The decoding libraries differ in license: zxing-cpp is Apache-2.0 with prebuilt Python wheels, ZBar is LGPL-2.1 ([zxing-cpp packaging](https://openapps.pro/packages/zxing-cpp), [Debian copyright](https://metadata.ftp-master.debian.org/changelogs/main/z/zxing-cpp/stable_copyright), [ZBar](https://zbar.sourceforge.net/)); pyzbar's own license was **not confirmed**. No patent claim is known to the author for printing or reading a QR code (the QR code's inventor has stated the patent is not enforced; **unverified** here, confirm before relying). Recommendation: read with zxing-cpp (permissive, no system library), as an optional extra.

### 3.2 What jason has

`jason.community.qr` makes QR codes with the optional `segno` extra (`pip install -e ".[qr]"`), defaults to error correction M and a four-module quiet zone, and **encodes links only** (`is_link`); it also prints the link as text beside the code ([qr.py](../src/jason/community/qr.py), [packets.md](packets.md)). A separator code is not a link, so it needs a small second entry point (`segno` itself accepts any text); `is_link` stays as is for letters.

### 3.3 Design sketch

**The sheet** (US Letter or A4, one side, no color, no photograph; photocopy-safe):
- A large QR code, centered, error correction **H** (a fold or a smudge is likely on a sheet that sits between papers), about 2 inches square, with the quiet zone.
- Above it, in large type: **START OF A NEW DOCUMENT**, and below it, small: "Place this sheet on top of the first page of each document. Scan it with the document. The splitter removes it."
- A hand-write line "Kind (optional): ______" and a row of check boxes for the common kinds drawn from the profile's kind list, so the person can mark a kind in ink for the reader to see, not for the machine.
- Below the code, the payload printed as text (a reader without the splitter can still see what it is).
- A second, **continuation** variant ("this page belongs to the document above": use for a back side or an oversized insert) and an **end of batch** variant. Three sheets, not a menu of twenty.
- Print a sheet of numbered sheets ("1 of 50") so a stack can be checked for a missing or doubled sheet: the sequence number is the batch audit.

**QR payload** (short, no private facts, no URL needed):
`jason-split:v1:S:<batch>:<n>` where `S` is the sheet type (`D` document start, `C` continue, `E` end of batch), `<batch>` is a random 6-character id printed when the sheets are generated, and `<n>` is the sheet's serial in that batch. It names no person, address, unit, account or association; it is meaningless without the splitter. Do not encode a title or kind in the code: a kind is a fact a person can read from the page, and the code stays valid when the answer changes. A checksum character is appended so a damaged read is rejected, not guessed.

**Detection in the splitter.** The light pass already renders a thumbnail per page. Decode barcodes from a **mid-resolution render of each page** (about 150 dpi; the Paperless discussion about inconsistent recognition at low resolution is the reason not to decode the 72 dpi thumbnail), only on pages whose thumbnail is mostly white with a dark square block, to stay fast; or on all pages in a worker, cancellable. A valid `D` sheet becomes a suggestion with confidence above every other signal and the reason in words:

> Page 12 is a "start of a new document" sheet (sheet 7 of batch K3F9Q2). The splitter will not include the sheet in the new file.

Behavior rules:
- The sheet page itself is **set aside** (excluded from outputs, kept in the source and listed in the review), not deleted. A "keep the sheet pages" option mirrors the Paperless choice.
- The next page is the boundary. A `D` sheet makes a *suggestion* like any other (accept-all-above includes it at High, but it is still a suggestion until the review). A person's own boundary is never moved by a sheet.
- Missing and doubled sheet numbers are reported: "sheets 7 and 9 were found, 8 was not".
- A damaged read (checksum fails) shows "a sheet seems to be here but could not be read" as a Low suggestion, never a silent miss.
- A sheet in a stack from another batch id is reported, not trusted blindly (a stale sheet left in a pile).
- A scanner's own blank removal can delete a mostly white sheet; the QR block makes the page non-blank, but the person is told to turn "skip blank pages" off or leave the code printed dark and large.

**How it fits AGENTS.md.**
- A suggestion only: never applied by jason on its own; the review step stays ("Do not approve, deny, or assign").
- General code with no association fact: the payload carries none; the sheet's title and kind list come from `Community` methods with empty defaults (kinds from the profile's document kinds); the base sheet is a **general base template** rendered by a profile with its own letterhead if wanted ("Templates are written once").
- Optional extras: sheet printing needs `segno` (already `.[qr]`); reading needs one new optional extra (zxing-cpp) and fails with "install the extra" when missing, like `qr.py`.
- Recorded reason: the detection reason is stored with the suggestion; a new decision is a rule row (`sheet-start`) in the cue table, not a function.
- Print goes through the person's printer; nothing is mailed ("Do not mail a letter on jason's own initiative").
- Lesson to add when built: stacks with sheets split exactly; stacks without them still use the engine.

**Effort.** Print side: low (a template, a numbered PDF writer, a non-link `qr` entry). Read side: medium (render at 150 dpi, decode, new cue row, the review wording, the audit of missing numbers). **Highest value of the whole list for future stacks**, because it replaces a guess with a read.

### 3.4 Archival practice

Digitization guidance in the results asked for one file per folder in original page order and for naming from box and folder identifiers ([NSW State Records specification](https://staterecords.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/Recordkeeping/City%20of%20Sydney%20RFT%20931%2009%20-%20Specification.pdf); a repository's own scanning guidelines put a worksheet and container list in the first folder: [HARC](https://harc-archives.libraryhost.com/s/HARCSM/page/scanning)). No source in the search confirmed a standard rule for target or separator sheets between folders; FADGI and NARA target requirements were **not verified**. The practice supports one design choice: sheets carrying the box and folder as handwriting (not in the code) and a sequence number so progress can be checked against a container list. Archivematica arranges transferred material into packages by dragging files into a directory structure and attaching a level of description ([library guide](https://dallibraries.atlassian.net/wiki/x/IIA2fQ)): the analogue here is arranging the output documents into a folder tree in the review step.

## 4. What would make suggestions good

### 4.1 Literature and numbers

- The task is **page stream segmentation**. A CNN combining page image and OCR text reached 91.9 percent on the Tobacco800 subset in the often-cited result ([Wiedemann and Heyer, 2019](https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/en/inst/ab/lt/publications/2019-wiedemann-lre-pss.pdf); the figure's attribution is ambiguous in secondary sources, the subset is small: 1,290 images).
- **TABME** (DocEng 2022) combines LayoutLM text-and-layout features with a ResNet and reports F1 of 0.953 on its synthetic folders of business documents; dataset and code at [github.com/aldolipani/TABME](https://github.com/aldolipani/TABME) ([paper](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10154824/1/Lipani_DocEng22_paper_8148.pdf)). Its folders are assembled from other collections, so a real mixed pile is probably harder (**inference**).
- **OpenPSS** (TPDL 2024, about 141 thousand pages, about 32 thousand documents from Dutch government sources) compares non-learned baselines, KNN and XGBoost on pretrained features, EfficientNet, BERT and a late ensemble ([paper](https://irlab.science.uva.nl/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/heus_open24.pdf)). Findings: ensembles of text and image beat either alone (late ensembling best: page-level F1 0.83 and 0.76 on its LONG and SHORT sets in the standard task); **image models transfer poorly to another corpus** and text models are more robust; LSTM sequence models underperformed on long streams; the best document-level weighted F1 was 0.77 and 0.69. So the headline is that a good boundary is reachable for a known corpus, but a plain document-level exact match is far lower than the page-level number: a person should expect to correct a meaningful share of boundaries.
- **Non-learned baselines matter**: in OpenPSS "assume the mean document length" scored page-level F1 of only 0.42 on LONG, so any learned score must be read against it.
- Segmentation lineage: Pk, then WindowDiff, then edit-distance Boundary Similarity ([Pevzner and Hearst via the Cambridge note](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/1516/R216/Pevzner.pdf), [Fournier and Inkpen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.2847), [Fournier, ACL 2013](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/P13-1167.pdf)).

### 4.2 Features worth having (all local)

Ranked by cost: (1) the signals already in the cue table; (2) **a repeated-header and footer text model learned in the session** (already designed as 4.6); (3) page-number and "x of N" parsing, including an "N of M" count that tells where a document *ends* (a start when the previous page reads "5 of 5"); (4) a visual embedding of the page thumbnail from a small pretrained network, compared with the mean of confirmed first pages (OpenPSS's KNN and XGBoost on pretrained VGG16 or BERT features are the strong simple baselines and train in seconds); (5) a small text classifier on the first lines (OpenPSS: text is the more robust modality); (6) a LayoutLM-style joint model, which TABME shows is best on its data but is heavy and needs training data we do not hold (**do not start here**). The local-model "is this a first page" question is already in 4.4; OpenPSS's finding that text transfers better supports keeping the text cues strong and treating image-only judgment as a second opinion.

### 4.3 Metrics

The design's boundary precision, recall and "taps saved" (4.7) stay the main numbers. Add:
- **Boundary F1 with a tolerance of one page** (reports near misses separately, since a boundary one page off costs one move, not a miss and an addition).
- **WindowDiff adapted to pages**: slide a window of k pages (k about half the mean document length of the reference); count windows where the number of boundaries differs. Reported with the Pk bias caveats (Pevzner and Hearst), as a whole-stack score that does not collapse when a stack is mostly long documents.
- **Document-level exact match** (a document counts only if its first and last page are both right), the strict number OpenPSS reports as a weighted F1.
- **Edits per 100 pages** after accepting everything as shown: the person-facing form of the above, and the one to show on the review screen ("you changed 6 of 31 suggestions").
- **Calibration**: the share of High that were accepted; already in 4.3/4.7.

### 4.4 Calibrating from a person's confirmed splits

Every accept, reject, edit and manual add is a label. Use them in the session only (as 4.6 already decides): after about 8 confirmed starts, fit (a) similarity of the page's feature vector to the mean confirmed first page versus the mean of non-first pages (the KNN-style baseline from OpenPSS), (b) a per-signal reliability: for each cue, the fraction of its suggestions the person accepted, which becomes that signal's weight for the rest of the session (a cue accepted 9 of 10 times rises, one rejected 8 of 10 falls). Show "suggestions updated from your last 8 marks" and a switch. Keep the persisted record of final accepted boundaries only as the file's provenance, not as a training set. A reusable improvement comes from editing the cue table after a measured trial on the made-up gold set, as the design already says.

## 6. Open questions

1. Does the optional model reader's finding "leans yes on uniform packets" hold with the text-only and image-embedding features of section 4.2? A trial on the gold set decides; add a row to the trials table in [document-tools.md](document-tools.md) after.
2. Scanner behaviors differ: does the person's scanner offer a "skip blank pages" option that would delete a separator sheet, and at what resolution does a QR code at 2 inches read reliably? Print and scan a test sheet before printing 50.
3. Should a sheet carry a *kind* in its code, or stay kind-free as proposed? Kind-free is safer; a printed check-box row serves the human reader.
4. Do separator sheets belong in the permanent record of a file? Proposed: set aside, listed in the review, absent from outputs, kept in the source.
5. What is the right tolerance for "near miss" in a stack where documents are 1 to 2 pages long (a one-page error is 50 percent of the document)? Report both strict and tolerant scores.
6. Licenses: confirm pyzbar's license, and the QR code patent position, before shipping any reader; zxing-cpp's Apache-2.0 is the safe default.
7. Which of Premiere's and Resolve's scene-cut review practices (markers, thresholds) are worth copying has only secondary sources here; a hands-on look would settle it.
